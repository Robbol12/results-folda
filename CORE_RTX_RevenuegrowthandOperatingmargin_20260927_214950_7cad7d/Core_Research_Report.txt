[CORE RESEARCH] RTX: Revenue growth

Quarters searched: 3
Filing sections searched: 89

Across the period, management’s revenue-growth narrative is anchored to organic net sales performance and underlying air-traffic recovery, with repeated references to commercial and defense demand drivers. In Q3 2023, transcript commentary cites 12% organic revenue growth and a $190 billion backlog, linking growth to forward-looking commercial indicators. By Q4 2023 and into 2024, the filings provide explicit net sales totals and organic net sales bridge language showing year-over-year increases driven by commercial aerospace aftermarket/OEM volume and defense program volume. For example, full-year 2024 net sales rise to $80,738 million from $68,920 million in 2023, and in the nine months ended September 30, 2024 net sales increase to $20,747 million from $19,133 million in 2023.

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance/actual pairs for revenue growth were identified in the provided evidence, so forecasting credibility cannot be evaluated on a guidance-vs-actual basis. As a result, credibility is set to null rather than inferred from general growth commentary or backlog references.

Trends:
  - Revenue growth sustained from 2023 into 2024
  - Organic growth framed as linked to air-traffic recovery and higher flight hours
  - Defense volume contributes to net sales growth alongside commercial aftermarket/OEM

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Commercial air traffic recovery as a recurring driver of revenue
  - Defense program volume as a recurring contributor
  - Geopolitical/export-control and macro volatility risk affecting operations

Key concerns:
  - Because no revenue-growth guidance/actual pairs were found, the ability to assess forecasting accuracy is not supported by evidence.
  - Some guidance-related excerpts are generic risk language, which does not provide a comparable revenue growth target to validate against actuals.

Conclusion:
Revenue growth was positive and strengthened over the period based on reported net sales and organic-net-sales bridge disclosures in the filings. Total net sales increased from $67,074 million (2022) to $68,920 million (2023), and further to $80,738 million (2024). In-year, nine months ended September 30, 2024 net sales were $20,747 million versus $19,133 million in the prior year (+8%), and Q3 2024 organic net sales increased by $0.4 billion in the quarter and $1.6 billion for nine months, driven primarily by higher defense sales and higher commercial aerospace aftermarket and OEM activity. This pattern supports that management’s growth narrative was reflected in realized revenue growth, though forecasting credibility cannot be quantified without explicit guidance/actual pairs.

[CORE RESEARCH] RTX: Operating margin

Quarters searched: 3
Filing sections searched: 89

Across 1Q-2Q 2023, operating margin/trend commentary focused on mix and year-over-year changes driven largely by discrete items in “other operating profit,” including the absence of Russia/export-control-related charges and other one-time effects; the filing shows operating profit margin at 9.6% in Q1 2023 versus 6.9% in Q1 2022. In mid-2023, reported operating profit comparisons were affected by program/accounting items and EAC adjustments, culminating in a large third-quarter 2023 sales charge in Pratt & Whitney (Powder Metal Matter) that management said matched expectations and had a large pre-tax operating profit impact. For 2024, filings attribute operating profit changes to sizable legal-related charges in the first half of the year, while later periods reflect gains (e.g., gain on sale) and improved segment operating performance. By Q4 2024, management reported segment operating margin expansion and full-year adjusted operating margin expansion for multiple segments (Collins, Pratt & Whitney, Raytheon), indicating operating margin improved materially versus prior year, though the excerpts provided do not directly reconcile to a single consolidated operating margin figure.

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance/actual pairs were provided specifically for the operating margin line item, so forecasting credibility cannot be assessed. The provided guidance in Q3 2023 and Q4 2024 relates to adjusted operating profit (and segment operating profit), not operating margin itself, and the outcomes are not presented as resolved guidance-vs-actual pairs for operating margin in the evidence.

Trends:
  - Operating margin (Q1 2023 vs Q1 2022) improved but was strongly influenced by discrete items in operating profit composition
  - 2023 operating profit/margin volatility tied to mix, EAC adjustments, and large program-related charges (Powder Metal Matter)
  - 1H 2024 operating profit/margin pressured by legal and contract-related charges
  - Late 2024 improved segment operating margin and adjusted operating profit margin expansion across major segments

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Sensitivity of operating margin to one-time charges/credits (legal matters, contract termination, customer insolvency, sanctions/export-control impacts, EAC adjustments)
  - Program execution and estimate updates (EAC/mix) driving operating profit swings
  - Mix and volume (commercial OEM mix, aftermarket, military volume) affecting margin expansion/pressure

Key concerns:
  - Operating margin is not consistently evidenced as a single consolidated metric across periods; provided “operating profit margin” is explicit only for Q1 2023 (9.6% vs 6.9% in Q1 2022), while later excerpts emphasize segment margin expansion and adjusted operating profit.
  - Much of the observed movement in operating profit/margin is attributable to discrete events and estimate changes (e.g., Powder Metal Matter charge, Expected Resolution of Certain Legal Matters, Raytheon contract termination), which can limit the usefulness of trend signals for underlying run-rate profitability.
  - The guidance provided appears to be for adjusted operating profit/segment operating profit (not margin), so any inferred conclusions about margin performance from those figures would not be evidence-backed here.

Conclusion:
Operating margin expanded meaningfully from the 1Q 2022 level to 1Q 2023: the filing reports operating profit margin of 9.6% for the quarter ended March 31, 2023 versus 6.9% for the quarter ended March 31, 2022. However, the broader 2023-to-2024 trajectory shows that operating profit and margin were frequently driven by discrete charges/credits and estimate adjustments, including the third-quarter 2023 Pratt & Whitney Powder Metal Matter charge (with management stating the operating profit impact matched expectations) and major first-half 2024 charges related to legal/contract matters. By Q4 2024, management reported segment operating margin expansion and full-year margin expansion (e.g., Collins, Pratt & Whitney, and Raytheon each showing 100 basis points of year-over-year margin expansion for the full year), indicating a late-period improvement in margin performance relative to prior year, although the consolidated operating margin figure is not explicitly provided in the supplied filing excerpts for the full-year.
