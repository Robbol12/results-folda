[CORE RESEARCH] UPS: Revenue growth

Quarters searched: 3
Filing sections searched: 81

Across 1H 2023, revenue growth was driven largely by mix/rate improvements and, importantly, acquisition-related logistics revenue (Bomi Group). In Q1 and Q2 2023, the filings show increases in healthcare logistics revenue ($98 million in Q1; $106 million in Q2) and mail services revenue ($55 million in Q1; $48 million in Q2), while Supply Chain Solutions (forwarding) revenue declined due to volume and market rate declines, partially offset by logistics growth. In Q3 2023, management expected fuel-related headwinds to be more than offset later by pricing/base-rate actions; actuals continued to show mail services growth ($19 million) and healthcare logistics growth ($138 million), with Bomi Group still contributing a large portion of the increases. By 1H 2024, consolidated revenue declined year over year (Q1 2024 revenue was $21,706 million vs. $22,925 million, down $1,219 million or 5.3%), despite some logistics revenue growth, reflecting pressure elsewhere (e.g., revenue quality/ADV trends). In 2H 2024, management guided to back-half revenue growth in the mid-single digits and reported revenue per piece turning positive in Q4 2024 (up 2.4% YoY), with growth partly supported by USPS/Asia demand and acquisitions, while Supply Chain Solutions revenue declined in the year due to the Coyote divestiture.

Forecasting credibility: Medium
Only two guidance/actual pairs were provided with a verdict, and both were misses (back-half 2024 revenue growth around 5% and second-half revenue growth in the mid-single digits were marked Missed). The later-period narrative still shows revenue strength in specific areas (e.g., logistics and revenue per piece turning positive in Q4 2024), but that does not align with the specific consolidated revenue-growth targets that were missed. With a 0-for-2 outcome, credibility on the guided revenue-growth level is reduced, though segment mix and special items (acquisition/divestiture) appear to complicate translation of guidance into consolidated results.

Trends:
  - Healthcare logistics revenue increased each quarter in 2023, with continued acquisition contribution (Bomi Group).
  - Mail services revenue grew in Q1–Q3 2023 (positive quarter-level dollar increases).
  - Supply Chain Solutions (forwarding) revenue faced volume and market-rate headwinds in 2023, partially offset by logistics.
  - Consolidated revenue declined YoY in Q1 2024 despite logistics growth.
  - By Q4 2024, revenue per piece growth turned positive (+2.4% YoY), though FY dynamics included a Supply Chain Solutions decline tied to the Coyote divestiture.

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Revenue growth sensitivity to mix and pricing vs. fuel surcharge impacts.
  - Forwarding segment headwinds from volume and market rate declines.
  - Dependence on acquisitions (Bomi Group, MNX Global Logistics) for logistics-related revenue growth.
  - Non-recurring portfolio changes/divestitures (e.g., Coyote) affecting Supply Chain Solutions revenue trends.

Key concerns:
  - Guided consolidated/back-half 2024 revenue growth targets were missed in both provided guidance/actual pairs (around 5% and mid-single digits).
  - Consolidated revenue declined in Q1 2024 (down 5.3% YoY to $21,706 million), suggesting that segment outperformance did not translate into overall growth at that time.
  - Supply Chain Solutions revenue declined in 2024 primarily due to the September 2024 divestiture of Coyote (partially offset by other growth), indicating portfolio effects can dominate underlying run-rate trends.

Conclusion:
Revenue growth was supported in 2023 primarily by higher healthcare logistics revenue and mail services increases, while Supply Chain Solutions (especially forwarding) showed declines from volume and market rate pressures that were only partially offset by logistics growth. In early 2024, consolidated revenue fell year over year in Q1 2024 ($21,706 million vs. $22,925 million, -$1,219 million or -5.3%), even as logistics segments increased (e.g., healthcare logistics revenue growth continues in the period narratives). Management’s specific back-half 2024 consolidated revenue-growth guidance (around 5% and mid-single digits) was marked as missed based on the provided verdicts. In late 2024, revenue per piece improved (up 2.4% YoY in Q4 2024), but FY revenue trends for Supply Chain Solutions were pressured by the Coyote divestiture, implying that overall revenue growth was uneven and heavily influenced by mix and portfolio changes.

[CORE RESEARCH] UPS: Operating margin

Quarters searched: 3
Filing sections searched: 81

Across 2023, reported operating margin was highly volatile, falling sharply in filings (e.g., Q1 operating margin to 7.3% and Q3 down to 14.8% and even 4.2% depending on the cited measure) while some transcript segment commentary showed much higher levels (e.g., consolidated operating margin 7.7% and multiple segment margins in Q3 2023). In 2023 Q2, filing excerpts indicated even wider swings in operating margin by the specific presentation (e.g., 20.0% vs 9.1% for the quarter). In 2024, the company again reported margin deterioration in early quarters (e.g., Q1 to 4.1% and 5.8% depending on presentation), then partial recovery in Q3 (operating margin increasing to 8.5% or 6.2% in filing excerpts). By Q4 2024, management described better-than-expected results (consolidated operating margin 12.3% referenced as guidance/expectation), but filings show full-year operating margin of 7.2%, underscoring inconsistency in how operating margin is defined/measured across excerpts.

Forecasting credibility: Low
Using the provided guidance/actual verdicts across the period (with misses included), credibility is low because guidance outcomes were not consistently met. Additionally, the guidance/actual comparisons are complicated by measurement inconsistency in the supplied excerpts (multiple operating margin figures per period, and Q4 2024 shows guidance tied to 12.3% while the filing excerpt shows 7.2% for the year), which reduces confidence in any apparent forecasting precision.

Trends:
  - Operating margin was volatile in 2023 with sharp declines in filing excerpts
  - Early 2024 showed further margin compression (multiple low single-digit/low teens figures depending on measure)
  - Q3 2024 showed sequential improvement in operating margin in filings
  - By Q4 2024, transcript commentary cited improved consolidated margin, while filing excerpt for the year indicates a much lower operating margin

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Revenue declines exceeding operating expense reductions
  - Expense/labor inflation dynamics driving margin pressure
  - Fit to Serve and expense management offsetting some margin deterioration
  - Potential definitional/presentation differences in operating margin across segments/adjusted vs total

Key concerns:
  - Operating margin figures are not consistent across excerpts for the same period (e.g., Q3 2023 transcript vs multiple filing excerpts; Q4 2024 guidance/management statements around 12.3% vs filing excerpt showing full-year operating margin down to 7.2%).
  - Because multiple operating margin metrics appear to be used (total vs adjusted, consolidated vs segment, quarter vs year-to-date), guidance vs actual comparisons may be mismatched if the same definition is not strictly used.

Conclusion:
Operating margin trended down overall through 2023 into early 2024, with sharp quarter-to-quarter swings reflected in filing excerpts (e.g., Q1 2023 operating margin 7.3% and Q3 2023 filing excerpts as low as 4.2% while others cite 14.8%). In 2024, the company reported some sequential improvement in Q3 2024 (filing excerpt operating margin increasing to 8.5% in one presentation), but early 2024 still showed compressed margins (e.g., Q1 2024 to 4.1% and 5.8% depending on measure). By Q4 2024, transcript commentary described a stronger consolidated operating margin (12.3% referenced as guidance/expectation), yet the filing excerpt indicates a full-year operating margin decreasing to 7.2%, implying the overall operating margin outcome remained substantially below management’s highlighted consolidated figure. Given these inconsistencies in reported/measured operating margin, the evidence suggests operating margin performance was not stable and remained pressured despite pockets of improvement.
