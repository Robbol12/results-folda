[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 186

Across 2023, management discussed gross margin largely in terms of auto profitability moving parts and discrete items, while actual results in filings showed a sharp step-down in automotive gross margin versus prior-year periods (e.g., total automotive gross margin fell to 19.9% in Q1’23 from 30.5%). Sequential commentary in late 2023 suggested stabilization/improvement (“auto gross margin improved sequentially”), but year-over-year comparisons in filings remained depressed through 2024, with automotive gross margin staying mostly in the high-teens to low-20s. The energy generation and storage segment showed the opposite pattern, improving meaningfully versus prior-year periods (e.g., -11.7% to 11.0% in Q1’23; 7.4% to 18.9% in full-year 2023; 18.9% to 26.2% in full-year 2024). By Q3 2024 and full-year 2024, filings attribute some automotive margin moves to mix/revenue factors (FSD and regulatory credits) and cost per unit, but full-year automotive gross margin still declined year-over-year in 2024 (19.4% to 18.4%).

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance/actual pairs were identified specifically for gross margin (i.e., there is no forward gross-margin target paired to subsequent reported gross margin where the timing and figure match). Because there are zero guidance-vs-actual pairs, forecasting credibility cannot be assessed under the required met/missed framework.

Trends:
  - Automotive gross margin stepped down sharply in 2023 vs prior-year periods and remained pressured through 2024
  - Sequential improvement was referenced in late 2023, but year-over-year automotive gross margin stayed lower
  - Energy generation & storage gross margin improved materially through 2023 and further in 2024

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Auto gross margin affected by many moving parts including pricing, incentives/tariffs, and one-time items (e.g., warranty adjustments, deferred revenue for Autopilot features)
  - Difficulty predicting auto gross margins due to external factors (tariffs, local incentives) and operational variables
  - Margin sensitivity to vehicle ASP and cost per unit, partially offset by regulatory credits and FSD revenue

Key concerns:
  - Even where management provided qualitative direction (e.g., sequential improvement), the filing data show automotive gross margin remained substantially below prior-year levels across multiple subsequent periods, indicating limited durability of any near-term improvement.
  - Gross margin composition appears increasingly driven by non-auto factors: energy storage margin improvements and revenue credits/FSD effects can offset auto weakness, complicating interpretation of “overall” gross margin trends.

Conclusion:
Gross margin evolution was dominated by a sustained deterioration in automotive gross margin beginning in 2023 and persisting through 2024, with total automotive gross margin dropping from 32.9% to 21.1% in Q1’23 and from 19.4% (full-year 2023) to 18.4% (full-year 2024). Interim results show continued volatility but generally depressed auto levels (e.g., 18.5% in Q1’24 and 18.5% in Q2’24 on total automotive gross margin), though some periods improved sequentially (e.g., total automotive & services and other segment increased from 17.4% to 18.7% in Q3’24). Offsetting this, the energy generation and storage segment’s gross margin improved dramatically, moving from negative in Q1’23 (-11.7% to 11.0%) and reaching 26.2% in full-year 2024 (from 18.9%), which likely helped stabilize consolidated gross margin despite ongoing auto pressure. The evidence provided is more consistent with structural/segment-driven divergence than with a single, forward-predictable gross margin trajectory.
