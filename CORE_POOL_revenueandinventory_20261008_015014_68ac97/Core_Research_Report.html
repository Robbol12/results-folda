Longitudinal Summary

Across 2024, management repeatedly attributed changes in inventory to ongoing inventory management efforts (e.g., swimming pool season execution and first-half measures), and the filing figures show inventory levels trending down from $1.5 billion at March 31, 2024 to about $1.2 billion at September 30, 2024, followed by a modest step up/flat near $1.3 billion by December 31, 2024. Inventory turns improved from 2.7x (March 31, 2024) to 2.8x (September 30 and December 31, 2024), consistent with faster turnover as inventory levels were reduced and normalized. The inventory reserve also generally increased over the year, rising to $28.6 million at September 30, 2024 (from $24.2 million at March 31, 2024), before ending at a level not explicitly tied to December 31 in the excerpts provided. Commentary in the Q3/Q4 filing excerpts highlights that gross margin was impacted by carrying a larger amount of lower-cost inventory early in 2023 and by higher vendor incentives tied to increased purchasing, indicating that inventory strategy interacts with gross margin outcomes.

  • Inventory management efforts reduce inventory levels but can affect gross margin via mix and mix-related factors
  • Higher vendor incentives earned vs prior year linked to increased purchasing during inventory actions
  • Inventory obsolescence reserve methodology tied to lower sales velocity and no sales for past 12 months

Key Concerns

  • Inventory reserve rose notably by September 30, 2024 ($28.6 million vs $25.9 million at 9/30/23), suggesting accumulating obsolescence risk even as inventory levels were reduced.
  • Q4 provided a qualitative statement that sales centers maintain well-stocked inventories, which may indicate less control/greater seasonality near year-end; the excerpt does not quantify how that aligns with the earlier net inventory declines.
  • A 2024 risk to gross margin is explicitly noted: the company carried a large amount of lower-cost strategically purchased inventory at the beginning of 2023 that was only reduced to normalized levels by end of 2023, implying that inventory composition effects may lag.

Conclusion

Inventory in 2024 appears actively managed, with net inventory levels decreasing from $1.5 billion at March 31, 2024 to $1.2 billion at September 30, 2024, and ending at about $1.3 billion at December 31, 2024. Turnover improved over the year (inventory turns rose from 2.7x at March 31, 2024 to 2.8x by September 30 and December 31, 2024), supporting the effectiveness of inventory reduction. However, the inventory reserve increased over the year (to $28.6 million at September 30, 2024), indicating ongoing exposure to inventory obsolescence despite lower balances.
