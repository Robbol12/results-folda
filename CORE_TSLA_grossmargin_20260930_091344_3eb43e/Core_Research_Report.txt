[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 92

Across 2025, the filings show generally declining automotive gross margin year-over-year through Q3 2025, with total automotive & services and other segment gross margin falling from 16.7% (Q1 2025) to 16.2% (Q2 2025 three months) and down further in the nine-month period (17.6% to 15.4%). The Q2 transcript mentions an S-curve period with “not usually always negative gross margin” while debugging, but the provided Q2 “guidance” is not a directional gross-margin forecast. In Q4 2025, management emphasized a step-change, stating the company ended the quarter with “over 20.1%” total gross margin; the filing for the full year 2025 shows total automotive & services and other segment gross margin decreased for the year ended December 31, 2025 versus 2024 (16.9% to 16.2%). Energy generation and storage gross margin improved throughout the year (e.g., Q1 24.6% to 28.8%; full-year 26.2% to 29.8%), partially offsetting automotive declines.

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance/actual numeric pairs for gross margin were provided for comparison, so a Met/Missed credibility assessment cannot be computed. While management made qualitative comments about gross margin (e.g., “over 20.1%” in Q4), there is no corresponding forward gross-margin guidance figure paired to later actual gross margin within the provided evidence set.

Trends:
  - Automotive gross margin generally declined year-over-year through Q3 2025
  - Energy generation and storage gross margin increased year-over-year throughout 2025
  - Management highlighted a Q4 gross margin step-up, but full-year filings still show overall automotive segment decline versus prior year

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Regulatory credits revenue as a driver of automotive gross margin (decrease cited in multiple periods)
  - Pricing (lower average selling price per unit) pressuring automotive gross margin
  - Debugging/transition dynamics during product ramp and S-curve affecting gross margin

Key concerns:
  - Potential mismatch between quarter-level narrative (“over 20.1%” total gross margin in Q4) and the full-year automotive & services/other gross margin trend shown in the filing for the year ended December 31, 2025 (16.9% to 16.2%).
  - Gross margin comparability is influenced by regulatory credits revenue and changes in revenue/cost composition, which may cause volatility versus a purely operational margin trend.

Conclusion:
Gross margin in 2025 appears to have been pressured in the automotive business despite some late improvement signals: automotive gross margin declined in each interim period shown (e.g., Q1 total automotive & services and other segment from 16.7% to 14.3%; Q3 three months from 20.1% to 17.0%; nine months from 17.6% to 15.4%). The dominant explanatory factors in the filings were lower average selling price per unit and a decrease in regulatory credits revenue, partially offset in some periods by lower average cost per unit or improved services margins. In contrast, energy generation and storage gross margin improved meaningfully (Q1 24.6% to 28.8%; full-year 26.2% to 29.8%), indicating a mix shift favorable to overall gross margin. Overall, the provided evidence supports an automotive-driven headwind through most of 2025 with improving energy margins, and management’s Q4 messaging of “over 20.1%” total gross margin does not clearly reverse the full-year automotive segment decline reported in the filings.
