[CORE RESEARCH] SBUX: Revenue growth

Quarters searched: 1
Filing sections searched: 93

Across fiscal 2023, Starbucks reported steady consolidated revenue growth year over year (Q1 +8% to $8.7B; Q2 +14% to $8.7B; and FY2023 +12% to $36.0B). The company’s revenue expansion was repeatedly attributed to mix of new store openings and comparable store sales, with reported headwinds primarily from foreign currency translation and China-related disruption recovery patterns. In early fiscal 2024, revenue growth continued in Q1 (+8% to $9.4B), but by Q2 management guidance pointed to full-year global revenue growth of low single digits (revising from 7%-10%), indicating expectations of a slower growth profile. The provided excerpts for later 2024 (Q3, Q4) describe continued revenue growth in some segments/markets, including North America +2% in fiscal 2024 vs fiscal 2023, but the excerpt set does not include a disclosed full-year fiscal 2024 revenue growth outcome to directly resolve the Q2 guidance. Overall, the evidence shows a shift from strong 2023 growth rates to a more moderate 2024 growth stance driven by store openings and comp dynamics.

Trends:
  - Consolidated revenue growth decelerates from double-digit Q2 2023 (+14%) toward low-single-digit full-year expectations in FY2024 guidance
  - Growth increasingly tied to store expansion and comp store sales mix effects (average ticket vs transaction changes)
  - Foreign currency translation and China disruption/recovery remain recurring headwinds

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Foreign currency translation headwind cited in multiple periods
  - China market disruption/recovery dynamics affecting regional revenue growth
  - Comparable store sales sensitivity to transactions vs average ticket
  - Revenue growth partially offset by mix shifts and by divestiture/annualization effects

Key concerns:
  - The only explicit revenue-growth guidance provided (FY2024 global revenue growth: low single digits) is not resolved by a disclosed full-year actual global revenue growth figure within the excerpts, leaving the guidance outcome unresolved.
  - Accounting/operational impacts can distort comparability (e.g., Seattle’s Best Coffee brand sale cited as impacting operating margin; Q3 2024 notes Global Coffee Alliance revenue decline tied to the same transaction), which can complicate interpreting underlying revenue growth momentum from segment discussions.

Conclusion:
Revenue growth was positive throughout fiscal 2023, with consolidated net revenues rising in Q1 (+8% to $8.7B) and Q2 (+14% to $8.7B), and full-year fiscal 2023 revenues up 12% to $36.0B. In fiscal 2024, growth continued into Q1 (+8% to $9.4B), but by Q2 management revised full-year fiscal 2024 global revenue growth expectations to low single digits from 7%-10%, signaling slower anticipated growth. Later excerpts indicate continued segment-level growth in 2024 (e.g., North America revenue increased 2% in fiscal 2024 vs fiscal 2023), but the provided evidence does not include a full-year fiscal 2024 global revenue growth actual to conclusively confirm the guided low-single-digit outcome. Therefore, the data support a narrative of slowing but still positive revenue growth into 2024, with the key resolution gap being the missing FY2024 global revenue growth actual.

[CORE RESEARCH] SBUX: Operating margin

Quarters searched: 1
Filing sections searched: 93

Across FY2023, operating margin moved sharply quarter to quarter, with declines tied to labor/commodity inflation and other deleverage factors (e.g., Q1 2023 operating margin decreased 40 bps to 18.5%), while large increases were driven by discrete items and mix (e.g., Q2 2023 operating margin increased 1,180 bps to 54.5% from a gain on sale of Seattle’s Best Coffee). FY2024 shifted to a more structurally lower and more pressured operating margin profile, with Q2 2024 global operating margin contracting 140 bps to 12.8% and management attributing it to deleverage, partner wage/benefit investments, and promotional activity, partially offset by reinvention efficiencies. Through the remainder of FY2024, segment operating income (as presented in the excerpts) declined for International and slightly for North America, consistent with continued margin pressure rather than a rebound to the prior FY2023 peak levels. In Q1 2025, the excerpts show continued weakness in operating income for North America and Channel Development, with International slightly down versus the prior-year quarter, indicating margin pressure persisted into FY2025.

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance/actual operating-margin pairs were identified in the provided evidence, so a guidance credibility rating cannot be established based on a met/missed pattern. While FY2024 Q2 includes qualitative/relative guidance references (e.g., full-year operating margin target range and statements of “in line with expectations”), the provided evidence does not include a specific guidance figure paired with a later reported operating-margin actual for a direct comparison.

Trends:
  - FY2023 operating margin highly volatile (discrete gains and deleverage offset)
  - FY2024 global operating margin materially lower (Q2 global margin 12.8%) versus earlier periods
  - International operating income and operating margin pressured through FY2024
  - North America operating income modestly down in FY2024 and down again in Q1 2025

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Labor/partner wage and benefits investments weighing on margins
  - Promotional activity and sales mix shifts reducing margin rate
  - Inflationary pressures on commodities and supply chain costs
  - Deleverage risk when sales do not grow enough to absorb fixed costs
  - Discrete items (gains/impairments) creating one-off margin swings

Key concerns:
  - Operating margin appears extremely sensitive to discrete transactions and mix effects in FY2023 (e.g., Q2 2023 operating margin increased to 54.5% largely due to the Seattle’s Best Coffee brand gain), which can obscure underlying operating performance trends.
  - The FY2024 period shows a sharp step-down in consolidated/global operating margin (Q2 2024: 12.8%), and subsequent excerpts reflect operating income declines for key segments; however, the provided evidence does not include specific guidance-to-actual operating-margin comparisons to validate management’s forecast accuracy.

Conclusion:
Operating margin deteriorated into FY2024 and remained pressured thereafter. In Q2 2024, management reported global operating margin contracting 140 bps to 12.8%, attributing the decline to deleverage, partner wage and benefit investments, and promotional activities.
