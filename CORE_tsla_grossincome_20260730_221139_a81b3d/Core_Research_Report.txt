[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross income

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 174

Over the reported periods from Q1 2021 through Q4 2022, gross income, particularly automotive gross margin, showed a general upward trend with some fluctuations. Management consistently highlighted improvements in automotive gross margin excluding credits, with notable milestones such as the Model Y Shanghai ramp and the return of positive gross margin for Model S. Energy generation and storage margins were more volatile, often negative or declining, but showed improvement by the end of 2022. The automotive segment's gross margin increased from around 25-26% in early 2021 to peaks above 30% in early 2022 before slightly declining towards the end of 2022. Services and other segments generally improved or offset some automotive margin declines. Management's guidance was optimistic and mostly aligned with actual results where specific guidance/actual pairs were available.

Forecasting credibility: High
There is one explicit guidance/actual pair with a verdict in Q4 2021 where management's forecast of automotive gross margin excluding credits increasing to a record high was met, indicating credible forecasting. Throughout the periods, management's qualitative guidance about gross margin strength and improvement was consistent with actual reported figures from filings and transcripts. No evidence of systematic misses was found, supporting a high credibility rating.

Trends:
  - Increasing automotive gross margin through 2021
  - Peak automotive gross margin in early 2022
  - Volatility and slight decline in automotive margin in mid to late 2022
  - Negative but improving energy generation and storage margins
  - Improvement in services and other gross margin

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Impact of regulatory credits on gross margin
  - Product ramp effects (Model Y Shanghai, Model S and X)
  - Energy segment margin volatility
  - Cost reduction and factory utilization as margin drivers

Key concerns:
  - Energy generation and storage segment showed significant margin volatility and negative margins for extended periods, which could impact overall gross income stability.
  - Automotive gross margin excluding credits peaked and then declined slightly in 2022, indicating potential margin pressure despite overall growth.
  - No accounting or reporting inconsistencies were noted across filings; figures from filings and transcripts were consistent.

Conclusion:
Gross income, as reflected primarily by automotive gross margin, improved steadily from early 2021 through early 2022, reaching record highs above 30% excluding regulatory credits. This improvement was driven by product ramp-ups, cost reductions, and factory utilization. However, in mid to late 2022, automotive gross margin experienced a modest decline, though it remained strong relative to prior years. Energy generation and storage margins were a drag for much of the period but showed recovery by the end of 2022. Overall, gross income trends indicate strong operational performance in automotive segments with some risks from energy segment volatility.
