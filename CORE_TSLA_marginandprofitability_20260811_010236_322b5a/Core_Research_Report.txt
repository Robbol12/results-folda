[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: margin

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 97

Over the course of 2024, the company's automotive gross margins generally declined compared to the prior year periods, with Q1 and Q2 showing decreases from 21.1% and 19.2% to 18.5% respectively. However, Q3 saw a quarter-over-quarter improvement in automotive margins, rising from 18.7% to 20.1%, attributed to a feature release, though the nine-month cumulative margin remained lower year-over-year. The energy generation and storage segment experienced a notable margin increase in Q1. By year-end, automotive gross margin declined to 18.4% from 19.4% the prior year, driven by lower average selling prices and ramping of new products, partially offset by cost reductions and increased regulatory credits. Management acknowledged challenges sustaining margins in Q4 and indicated automotive margins would not match the rest of the business. The single guidance/actual pair available was met, reflecting some forecasting accuracy.

Forecasting credibility: High
There is one explicit guidance/actual pair related to Q4 2024 automotive margins, where management forecasted challenges sustaining margins and the actual results aligned with this expectation. This met guidance instance supports a high credibility rating. The detailed filing data across quarters provides consistent and transparent margin reporting, reinforcing reliability in management's margin outlook. No missed guidance pairs were identified, further supporting credibility.

Trends:
  - Declining automotive gross margins year-over-year in early 2024
  - Improvement in automotive margins in Q3 2024
  - Energy generation and storage margin increase in Q1 2024
  - Lower average selling prices impacting margins
  - Cost reductions and regulatory credits partially offsetting margin pressure

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Pressure from lower average selling prices
  - Challenges sustaining automotive margins
  - Impact of new product ramp (e.g., Cybertruck)
  - Regulatory credits and FSD revenue as margin offsets

Key concerns:
  - Automotive gross margin consistently declined year-over-year except for a Q3 quarter-over-quarter improvement, indicating ongoing margin pressure.
  - The year-end margin decline was attributed to lower ASPs and new product ramp, which may signal structural margin challenges.
  - No accounting or reporting inconsistencies were noted, but the reliance on regulatory credits and FSD revenue to offset margin declines could pose sustainability risks.

Conclusion:
The company's automotive gross margins faced downward pressure throughout 2024, primarily due to lower average selling prices and the ramp of new products like the Cybertruck. While cost efficiencies and increased regulatory credits helped mitigate some margin erosion, the overall trend was a decline in automotive margins year-over-year. The energy generation and storage segment showed margin improvement, highlighting segment variability. Management's guidance about margin challenges was met, indicating credible forecasting. Going forward, sustaining or improving automotive margins will likely require addressing pricing pressures and product cost structures.
