[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 186

Across 2023, management repeatedly attributed weaker auto gross margins to pricing/actions and one-time items, while energy generation and storage gross margins improved overall. Despite initial commentary in Q1 2023 referencing “mid-20% gross margins” for launched programs, reported automotive gross margin fell sharply from 32.9% to 21.1% in Q1 2023 and continued to weaken through subsequent quarters (e.g., 27.9% to 19.2% in Q2 and 29.6% to 19.7% for nine months). Through 2024, the automotive gross margin remained below 2023 levels overall, but showed pockets of improvement (e.g., Q3 2024 auto gross margin up to 20.1% vs 18.7% in Q3 2023), supported by lower average costs per unit, higher FSD and regulatory credit revenue, partially offset by lower vehicle selling prices. By full-year 2024, automotive & services and other segment gross margin declined to 16.9% (from 18.2% in 2023), while energy generation and storage gross margin rose strongly to 26.2% (from 18.9% in 2023), widening the divergence across segments.

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance/actual pairs were identified for gross margin, so forecasting credibility cannot be quantified. Management did provide qualitative commentary (e.g., Q1 2023 referencing “mid-20% gross margins” for launched programs and Q4 2023 noting prediction is “extremely challenging”), but those statements are not paired with a specific forward-looking gross margin target that can be checked against later reported results.

Trends:
  - Auto gross margin structurally declined throughout much of 2023 (large drops in both automotive-only and combined auto & services metrics).
  - Energy generation and storage gross margin improved materially in 2023 and continued rising through 2024.
  - In 2024, auto gross margin showed intermittent quarter-to-quarter improvement (not sustained), while the full-year 2024 level remained below 2023.

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Vehicle pricing pressure and affordability actions weighing on automotive gross margin
  - One-time items/warranty adjustments and timing-related effects
  - Regulatory credit and FSD revenue acting as partial offsets to margin headwinds
  - External factors (e.g., tariffs/incentives) making auto gross margin difficult to forecast

Key concerns:
  - The only quantitative-style directional benchmark in transcripts (“mid-20% gross margins for any program that we launch” in Q1 2023) is not borne out by the reported automotive gross margin levels for that period (e.g., total automotive gross margin fell from 32.9% to 21.1% in Q1 2023), and there is no explicit forward guidance target/measurement window to evaluate accuracy.
  - Segment divergence increased: automotive & services gross margin generally weakened (full-year 2024: 16.9% vs 18.2% in 2023) while energy storage margin strengthened (full-year 2024: 26.2% vs 18.9% in 2023), complicating consolidated gross margin interpretation.

Conclusion:
Gross margin evolution is dominated by a sustained deterioration in automotive gross margin through 2023 and continued sub-2023 levels in 2024, offset at times by regulatory credit and FSD-related revenue. In 2023, total automotive gross margin dropped from 32.9% to 21.1% in Q1 2023 and remained around the high-teens/low-20s thereafter (e.g., down to 19.4% in Q4 full-year comparison: 28.5% to 19.4%). For the full year 2024, the automotive & services and other segment gross margin decreased to 16.9% from 18.2% in 2023, with drivers including lower average selling price and Cybertruck ramp, partially offset by lower average combined cost per unit and higher regulatory credit and FSD revenue. By contrast, energy generation and storage gross margin improved strongly, reaching 26.2% in 2024 (from 18.9% in 2023), meaning consolidated gross margin outcomes likely depended increasingly on segment mix and the improving energy/storage profitability rather than auto margins alone.
