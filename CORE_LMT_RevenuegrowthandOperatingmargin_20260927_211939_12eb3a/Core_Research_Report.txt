[CORE RESEARCH] LMT: Revenue growth

Quarters searched: 6
Filing sections searched: 128

Across 2023, the filings emphasize ongoing monitoring of program execution and the risk of additional losses due to performance, scope, schedule, or cost growth, rather than committing to specific revenue growth guidance. In Q4 2023, management highlighted multiple programs of record entering growth stages (including F-35 sustainment, PAC-3/HIMARS/GMLRS, radar/CH-53K, and Trident II D5 modernization) and discussed near-to-mid-term growth elements. In 2024, commentary continued to tie revenue growth to product sales volume and classified program growth, with Q1 2024 transcript noting “robust revenue growth,” while Q2 2024 shifted emphasis to profit booking rate adjustments driven by higher material costs and unfavorable classified program cost impacts. By Q3 2024, management referenced “medium-term targets for low single-digit revenue growth,” but the provided evidence does not include a resolved actual-versus-target pair to confirm whether targets were met.

Forecasting credibility: None
No explicit guidance/actual revenue-growth pairs are provided in the evidence set, so forecasting credibility cannot be assessed. The only quasi-target referenced is a “medium-term targets for low single-digit revenue growth” statement in Q3 2024, but no corresponding actual outcome is shown here to determine whether the target was met or missed.

Trends:
  - Shift from execution-risk monitoring (2023) toward more explicit growth-stage program discussion (Q4 2023)
  - 2024 revenue growth framed as driven by higher production volumes and classified program growth
  - Qualitative confidence increases around “unlocking” revenue growth (Q4 2024)

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Program execution and cost/scope/schedule risks could lead to additional losses
  - Revenue growth linked to conversion of backlog and scaling of product/program volumes
  - Classified program cost impacts and material cost inflation affect profitability even when revenue grows

Key concerns:
  - Revenue-growth performance outcomes (actual revenue numbers by period) are not provided in the evidence, preventing verification of stated growth expectations.
  - While management cites revenue growth drivers in multiple programs, the filings simultaneously emphasize potential for future additional losses tied to execution and cost growth.

Conclusion:
Based on the provided evidence, management consistently characterizes revenue growth as occurring or expected, citing growth-stage programs in Q4 2023 and higher product sales / classified program growth in 2024. Q1 2024 transcript explicitly states “robust revenue growth across the company,” but no filing-sourced actual revenue figures are included here to quantify the growth. In Q3 2024, management references a medium-term target of “low single-digit revenue growth,” yet the evidence does not include the corresponding actual results to confirm achievement. Overall, the direction of change is positive per management commentary, but the specific magnitude and confirmation versus targets cannot be determined from the supplied guidance/actual evidence.

[CORE RESEARCH] LMT: Operating margin

Quarters searched: 6
Filing sections searched: 128

Across 2023, operating profit/margin commentary repeatedly emphasized the role of profit adjustments (e.g., contract mix and net favorable/unfavorable profit booking rate adjustments) as the primary driver of margin direction rather than a steady underlying cost structure. Q2 2023 explicitly stated a segment operating margin of 11.1%, while Q4 2023 guided to a down-at-midpoint outcome due to lower expected profit adjustments and classified losses, and actual results later matched the described decline in operating profit driven by lower profit adjustments and a classified program loss. In 2024, management described operational execution producing improving margins (11.3% in Q2 2024 and 10.9% in Q3 2024), though results continued to be influenced by adjustments and classified-program reach-forward loss expectations (e.g., $100 million at MFC). By Q4 2024, management guided to an “11% target” for 2025 operating margins; the disclosed 2024 results indicated margins “returned to 11%,” setting the baseline for the next-year target.

Forecasting credibility: Medium
Only one explicit guidance/actual pair for operating margins was scored with a verdict (Q2 2024: management anticipated consolidated margins to remain at 10.5%, and actual consolidated margins were 11.3%, corresponding to an Exceeded outcome). Because the guidance/actual pair count is small (1), the forecasting credibility is assessed as Medium rather than High. Other guidance statements (e.g., Q4 2023 segment operating profit range and Q4 2024 2025 margin target) were marked Unresolved in the provided evidence, so they do not contribute to the Met/Exceeded/Missed count.

Trends:
  - Margins generally held in the ~10.5%–11.3% range from 2023–2024 with variability tied to profit adjustments
  - 2024 showed sequential/relative improvement in margins in Q2 and continued solid levels in Q3
  - Classified program charges and reach-forward losses were repeatedly cited as margin swing factors

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Profit booking rate adjustments and net favorable/unfavorable adjustments materially affecting reported margins
  - Contract mix driving operating profit and margin direction (both favorable and unfavorable)
  - Classified programs and reach-forward losses creating predictable but volatile impacts on margins
  - Comparability disclosures (profit booking rates and other factors) highlighted as a recurring source of quarter-to-quarter changes

Key concerns:
  - Margin outcomes appear heavily dependent on adjustments (profit booking rate adjustments and classified-program-related items), which can obscure underlying operational margin durability.
  - Guidance for 2025 operating margins and a Q4 2023 segment operating profit range were provided but remained Unresolved in the evidence, limiting assessment of forward-call accuracy beyond the single scored pair.
  - Potential comparability/accounting effects are repeatedly referenced via disclosures (e.g., profit booking rates and other factors in segment reporting), suggesting earnings/margin presentation sensitivity to contract accounting mechanics.

Conclusion:
Operating margin performance over the period was characterized by stability around the low- to mid-10% range, with notable quarter-to-quarter movement driven primarily by profit adjustments and contract/classified-program effects rather than consistent, adjustment-independent expansion. The clearest margin datapoint in the record is Q2 2023 where segment operating margin was reported at 11.1%. In 2024, management reported segment operating margins of 11.3% in Q2 (with consolidated margins of 11.3% in the transcript) and 10.9% in Q3, while Q1 2024 included margins up to 10.1% with a stated $100 million reach-forward loss expectation at MFC. Overall, management’s operating margin narrative culminated in Q4 2024 with operating margins “returned to 11%,” and the forward view emphasized an “11% target” for 2025, though that 2025 outcome is not resolved in the provided evidence.
