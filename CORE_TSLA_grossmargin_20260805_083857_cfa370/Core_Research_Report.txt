[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 97

Over the reported periods in 2022, gross margin for Tesla's automotive segment showed variability but generally remained strong. Q1 2022 saw a notable increase in automotive gross margin to 32.9%, exceeding 30% when excluding regulatory credits. However, in Q2 and Q3, automotive gross margin declined sequentially to around 27.9%, with some offset from services and other segments. By Q4 2022, management guided for automotive gross margin above 20% excluding leases and rent credits, but the actual full-year automotive gross margin decreased slightly to 28.5%. Energy generation and storage margins improved significantly by year-end. The filings consistently reported these figures, with some discrepancies between management commentary and actual segment results, especially in Q2 where guidance was missed.

Forecasting credibility: Low
There is only one explicit guidance/actual pair with a verdict, which was a miss in Q2 2022 where the gross margin for total automotive & services and other segment stayed flat at 25.7%, missing expectations. Management's forward guidance was often qualitative or vague, and the single quantitative guidance in Q4 2022 (above 20%) was not paired with a precise actual figure in the same period for direct comparison. This limited data and the miss in Q2 reduce confidence in management's forecasting credibility on gross margin.

Trends:
  - Automotive gross margin peaked in Q1 2022
  - Sequential decline in automotive gross margin through Q2 and Q3
  - Energy generation and storage gross margin improved over the year
  - Services and other segment gross margin showed modest improvement
  - Full-year automotive gross margin slightly decreased in 2022

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Impact of regulatory credits on gross margin
  - Operational inefficiencies affecting margins
  - Price cuts influencing average selling price and margins
  - Variability in automotive vs. energy segment margins

Key concerns:
  - The Q2 2022 miss on gross margin guidance indicates some forecasting challenges.
  - Management's guidance often lacked precise quantitative targets, limiting assessment of accuracy.
  - There is a slight inconsistency in how gross margin is reported with and without regulatory credits and leases, complicating direct comparisons.

Conclusion:
Tesla's automotive gross margin demonstrated strength early in 2022, reaching a high of 32.9% in Q1, but faced pressure in subsequent quarters, declining to around 27.9% in Q2 and Q3. Despite management's optimistic guidance for Q4 to maintain above 20% automotive gross margin excluding leases and credits, the full-year automotive gross margin slightly decreased to 28.5%. Energy generation and storage margins improved notably, reflecting operational gains in that segment. The variability and a missed guidance in Q2 suggest some challenges in forecasting gross margin accurately, but overall, Tesla maintained relatively healthy automotive gross margins throughout 2022.
