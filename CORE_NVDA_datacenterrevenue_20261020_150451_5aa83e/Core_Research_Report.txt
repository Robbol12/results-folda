[CORE RESEARCH] NVDA: data center revenue

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 87

What the sources show:
Across FY25, NVIDIA’s data center revenue increased rapidly from $22.6B in Q1 FY25 to $26.3B in Q2 and $30.8B in Q3, reaching $35.6B in Q4. The growth narrative consistently emphasizes demand for the NVIDIA Hopper GPU computing platform in Q1 and Q2, while Q4 shifts toward Blackwell as “the Blackwell ramp commenced” and highlights delivered Blackwell revenue alongside continued H200 growth. Management also provided more detail over time on mix and profitability: in Q2, GAAP/non-GAAP gross margins declined sequentially due to higher mix of new products within Data Center and inventory provisions for low-yielding Blackwell material; in Q3 and Q4, gross margins remained under sequential pressure (though specific drivers are described mainly in Q3 for mix) while management guided toward mid-70s gross margins later in the year. Geography and customer mix disclosures evolved as well, moving from China down significantly in Q1 (post-October export controls) to later emphasis on U.S. strength from initial Blackwell ramp (Q4) and broader diversification and sovereign AI investment (Q1). Over the fiscal year, management reported that Data Center revenue more than doubled and cited accelerated computing and networking growth components for the full year 2025.

Watch points:
  - Gross margin commentary suggests profitability sensitivity to product mix and Blackwell-related execution: Q2 cites inventory provisions for low-yielding Blackwell material and expects the trend to continue into Q4 as mix shifts to new products. Q3 attributes sequential gross margin decline to a mix shift of H100 systems to more complex, higher-cost systems within data center.
  - Geographic risk remains visible: Q1 explicitly states Data Center revenue in China is down significantly from pre–October export control restrictions levels, which implies regulatory-driven variability in regional revenue.
  - Product/platform transition risk: Hopper strength alongside Blackwell ramp effects
  - Gross margin volatility driven by mix shifts and Blackwell material yield/provisions
  - Geographic/export-control exposure (China down after October export control restrictions)
  - Data center “inference” contribution persists (estimated ~40% in trailing four quarters in Q1 and “more than 40%” in Q2)

Sources: transcript_nvda-q2-2025.html | filing_10-q-q2-2025.html | Presentation_9e363f58f13c8252.html
Management commentary:
  - Data Center revenue of $26.3 billion was a record, up 16% sequentially and up 154% year on year, driven by strong demand for NVIDIA Hopper, GPU computing, and our networking platforms.
  - Cloud service providers represented roughly 45% of our Data Center revenue, and more than 50% stemmed from the consumer Internet and enterprise companies.
  - Over the trailing four quarters, we estimate that inference drove more than 40% of our Data Center revenue.
  - GAAP gross margins were 75.1% and non-GAAP gross margins were 75.7%, down sequentially due to a higher mix of new products within Data Center and inventory provisions for low-yielding Blackwell material.
  - As our Data Center mix continues to shift to new products, we expect this trend to continue into the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025.
  - And the world builds about $1 trillion worth of data centers.
  - $1 trillion worth of data centers in a few years will be all accelerated computing.
  - We are at the beginning of our journey to modernize $1 trillion worth of data centers from general-purpose computing to accelerated computing.
  - Our Data Center revenue in China grew sequentially in Q2 and a significant contributor to our Data Center revenue.
  - As a percentage of total Data Center revenue, it remains below levels seen prior to the imposition of export controls.
  - We expect to grow our Data Center business quite significantly next year.
  - The data center worldwide are in full steam to modernize the entire computing stack with accelerated computing and generative AI.
  - Starting with Data Center.
  - In the future, every single data center will have GPUs.
  - Key workloads driving our Data Center growth include generative AI model training and inferencing; video, image, and text data pre and post processing with CUDA and AI workloads; synthetic data generation; AI-powered recommender systems; SQL and Vector database processing as well.
  - And the reason for that is because we can, in a liquid-cooled data center, in any data center -- power-limited data center, whatever size data center you choose, you could install and deploy anywhere from three to five times the AI throughput compared to the past.
  - So, what you're seeing there is sometimes just a swift shift in terms of who they are using to complete their full configuration before those things are going into the data center, going into notebooks and those pieces of it.

  - And so, what's going to happen over the course of the next X number of years, and let's assume that over the course of four years, the world's data centers could be modernized as we grow into IT, as you know, IT continues to grow about 20%, 30% a year, let's say.

  - It could be, of course, tens of megawatts in the past, and now it's -- most data centers are now 100 megawatts to several hundred megawatts, and we're planning on gigawatt data centers, it doesn't really matter how large the data centers are.

  - As a percentage of total data center revenue, it remained well below levels prior to the onset of export controls.
  - Cloud service providers were approximately half of our data center sales with revenue increasing more than 2x year on year.
  - And when you're in the power-limited data center, the best -- the highest performance per watt translates directly into the highest revenues for our partners.
  - Our ability to sell our networking with many of our systems that we are doing in data center is continuing to grow and do quite well.
  - We are a data center-scale AI infrastructure company.
  - Blackwell is a full-stack, full-infrastructure, AI data center scale system with customizable configurations needed to address a diverse and growing AI market from x86 to ARM, training to inferencing GPUs, InfiniBand to Ethernet switches, and NVLink.
