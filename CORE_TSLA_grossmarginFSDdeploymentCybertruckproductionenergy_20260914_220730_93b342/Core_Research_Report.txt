[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 97

Over the reported periods in 2022, gross margin for the automotive segment showed notable fluctuations. Q1 2022 saw a significant increase in automotive gross margin to 32.9%, exceeding 30% when excluding regulatory credits, marking a new financial record. However, in Q2 2022, the automotive gross margin declined sequentially to 27.9%, which was in line with management's guidance about inefficiencies impacting margins. Q3 2022 maintained a similar automotive gross margin of 27.9%, with management noting that excluding regulatory credits and new factory costs, margins would have been near 30%. By Q4 2022, despite concerns about price cuts potentially pushing margins below 20%, the actual reported gross margin for automotive decreased to 27.9%, with mixed results in the combined automotive and services segment. Overall, the gross margin trend showed improvement year-over-year but with some sequential softness and variability quarter-to-quarter.

Forecasting credibility: High
There is one explicit guidance/actual pair with a verdict, from Q1 to Q2 2022, where management forecasted inefficiencies would impact gross margins in Q2 and the actual margin of 27.9% met this expectation. This alignment between guidance and actual results supports a high credibility rating for management's forecasting on gross margin. Although only one pair is available, the consistency of management commentary with reported figures across other quarters further supports this assessment.

Trends:
  - Automotive gross margin increased year-over-year
  - Sequential decline in gross margin from Q1 to Q2 2022
  - Margins near 30% excluding regulatory credits and new factory costs
  - Mixed automotive & services gross margin trends
  - Concerns about price cuts impacting margins in late 2022

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Impact of regulatory credits on reported gross margin
  - Factory inefficiencies affecting margins
  - Price cuts and their effect on average selling price and margins
  - Variability in services and other segment margins

Key concerns:
  - No accounting or reporting inconsistencies were noted across filings; however, the impact of regulatory credits and factory startup costs complicates direct margin comparisons.
  - Management's guidance on future gross margin (Q4 2022) remains unresolved, leaving some uncertainty about margin sustainability amid price cuts.

Conclusion:
Gross margin for the automotive segment improved significantly year-over-year in 2022, reaching a peak of 32.9% in Q1 before declining sequentially to 27.9% in Q2 and Q3. Management's guidance accurately anticipated the margin decline due to inefficiencies, demonstrating credible forecasting. Excluding regulatory credits and new factory costs, margins would have been closer to 30%, indicating underlying strength. Despite concerns about price cuts potentially pushing margins below 20%, actual reported margins remained above this level through Q4 2022. Overall, the evidence suggests that while gross margin is subject to quarter-to-quarter variability driven by operational factors and pricing strategies, the company has maintained relatively strong automotive gross margins throughout the year.

[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: FSD deployment

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 97

Throughout 2022, Tesla consistently emphasized the importance of improving and deploying its Full Self-Driving (FSD) capabilities as part of its vehicle performance enhancements. Early in the year, management highlighted ongoing development and deployment of FSD beta with city streets capability to over 100,000 owners by Q2, and later expanded this to roughly 400,000 customers by Q4. Despite ambitious guidance to release FSD beta to all North American customers by the end of 2022, this target was missed. The company continued to invest in hardware and software improvements, including plans for FSD Hardware 4, but the full deployment to all customers did not materialize within the stated timeframe. Overall, Tesla's FSD deployment progressed in scale but fell short of some public timing commitments.

Forecasting credibility: Low
Tesla provided specific guidance that FSD beta would be released to all North American customers by the end of 2022, which was not met, indicating a missed forecast. Another guidance point about full FSD Beta access within 2022 was also missed. There are no instances of exceeded or met guidance for FSD deployment timing, leading to a pattern of missed targets. Therefore, management's forecasting credibility on FSD deployment timing is low based on the available guidance/actual pairs.

Trends:
  - Increasing FSD beta deployment scale
  - Missed timing targets for full deployment
  - Ongoing hardware and software improvements
  - Focus on AI-based vehicle functionality

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Supply chain and factory shutdown challenges
  - Macroeconomic condition monitoring
  - Complexity of AI and neural network development
  - Hardware upgrades linked to new vehicle models

Key concerns:
  - Repeated missed guidance on FSD beta full deployment timing raises concerns about forecasting reliability.
  - No accounting or reporting inconsistencies were noted in the FSD deployment disclosures.
  - Reliance on vague or non-quantitative guidance statements limits clarity on progress.

Conclusion:
Tesla made significant progress in expanding FSD beta deployment during 2022, growing from over 100,000 to roughly 400,000 customers with city streets capability. However, the company missed its public guidance to release FSD beta to all North American customers by the end of the year, indicating challenges in scaling or regulatory approval. While Tesla continues to develop hardware and software to support FSD, the timing of full deployment remains uncertain. The pattern of missed guidance suggests that investors should be cautious in relying on Tesla's forward-looking statements about FSD deployment timelines.
