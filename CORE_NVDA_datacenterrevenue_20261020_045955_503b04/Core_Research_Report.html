THE ANSWER

What the sources show

Over fiscal 2025, data center revenue rose sharply each quarter, starting with $22.6B in Q1 FY25 and reaching $35.6B in Q4 FY25. Management attributed early growth primarily to demand for the NVIDIA Hopper platform, including both compute and related platforms, while inference was estimated to drive about 40% of data center revenue in the trailing four quarters (noted in Q1 and again as “more than 40%” in Q2). During Q2 and Q3, management also emphasized an improving shift to newer products and preparation for the Blackwell ramp, alongside changing gross-margin dynamics. By Q4, growth was framed as driven by the Blackwell ramp and continued H200 growth, with delivered Blackwell revenue noted and regional growth strongest in the U.S. due to Blackwell ramp-up. Across the year, the company highlighted geographic and regulatory impacts (e.g., China down significantly in Q1 after new export control restrictions in October) and varying gross-margin pressures tied to product mix and inventory provisions.

Watch points

  • Gross margin pressures were linked to Data Center product mix and inventory provisions for low-yielding Blackwell material in Q2, and to mix shift toward more complex/higher-cost H100 systems in Q3—suggesting margins may remain sensitive during platform transitions.
  • China demand was described as down significantly in Q1 versus the pre-export-control level, indicating potential continued volatility from regulatory restrictions.
  • There is some granularity inconsistency in how compute revenue is presented versus overall data center revenue across quarters (e.g., Q1: Data Center revenue $22.6B vs. Data Center compute revenue $19.4B; Q2: overall $26.3B vs. compute $22.6B; Q3 compute revenue $27.6B—while overall data center revenue is $30.8B), though each period still presents a compute subset alongside total data center revenue.
  • Product-platform demand focus: Hopper initially, then transition/ramp to Blackwell (and continued H200 growth)
  • Gross margin volatility tied to product mix shift and provisioning (e.g., Blackwell material inventory provisions in Q2; mix shift to more complex higher-cost systems in Q3)
  • Geographic/regulatory exposure: China decline after October export control restrictions (Q1)
  • Customer concentration by type: CSPs consistently described as a large portion of data center revenue (e.g., ~45% in Q2)
