[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 97

Over the course of 2024, gross margin trends showed a general decline in the total automotive segment, with margins decreasing from 21.1% in Q1 2023 to 18.4% by year-end 2024. Conversely, the energy generation and storage segment experienced significant margin improvement, rising from 11.0% in Q1 2023 to 30.5% by Q3 2024. The total automotive & services and other segment margins fluctuated, with decreases in the first half of the year followed by a slight increase in Q3 before declining again by year-end. Management commentary highlighted factors such as lower average selling prices, Cybertruck ramp, and improvements in service utilization impacting margins. Overall, automotive gross margins faced pressure while energy generation margins improved markedly.

Forecasting credibility: None
No forward guidance paired with actual results was provided for gross margin in any period, making it impossible to assess management's forecasting credibility on this topic. Without guidance-actual pairs, no pattern of met, exceeded, or missed forecasts can be established. Therefore, the credibility rating must remain null.

Trends:
  - Declining automotive gross margin
  - Improving energy generation and storage margin
  - Fluctuating automotive & services margin
  - Lower average selling prices impacting margins
  - Cybertruck ramp effects

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Pressure on automotive gross margins
  - Service utilization improvements
  - Regulatory credit and FSD revenue contributions
  - Vehicle cost management challenges

Key concerns:
  - No forward-looking guidance on gross margin provided, limiting forecasting assessment.
  - Automotive gross margin decline driven by lower selling prices and product mix changes.
  - Potential volatility in service and other segment margins noted with sequential declines despite year-over-year improvements.

Conclusion:
Gross margin in the automotive segment showed a consistent downward trend throughout 2024, declining from 21.1% in Q1 2023 to 18.4% by year-end 2024, primarily due to lower average selling prices and the ramp-up of the Cybertruck. In contrast, the energy generation and storage segment experienced substantial margin expansion, reaching 30.5% by Q3 2024. The total automotive & services and other segment margins were more variable, with some sequential improvements but an overall decline year-over-year. Management attributed margin changes to product mix, cost efficiencies, and revenue from regulatory credits and FSD. However, the absence of forward guidance on gross margin prevents evaluation of management's forecasting accuracy on this key profitability metric.
