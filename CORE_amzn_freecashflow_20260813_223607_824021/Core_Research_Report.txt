[CORE RESEARCH] AMZN: free cash flow

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 130

Over the periods from Q1 2021 through Q4 2022, free cash flow has been consistently reported as cash flow from operations minus purchases of property and equipment, net of proceeds and incentives. The reported free cash flow showed a declining trend, starting from positive values such as $26.4 billion in Q1 2021 (trailing twelve months) and dropping to negative values by Q4 2021 and continuing negative into 2022, with -$9.1 billion in 2021 and -$11.6 billion in 2022 (year ended). Management acknowledged the shift to negative free cash flow starting in 2021 and expected it to continue in 2022. The decline is primarily driven by increased capital expenditures, which rose from about $40.8 billion in Q1 2021 to over $58 billion by Q4 2022. Throughout, the company provided detailed reconciliations in filings, but no forward guidance on free cash flow was given.

Forecasting credibility: None
There are zero guidance/actual pairs for free cash flow, as management does not provide forward guidance on this metric. Therefore, it is not possible to assess forecasting credibility for free cash flow. The assessment is based solely on reported actuals from filings and management commentary acknowledging trends, without any forecast comparisons.

Trends:
  - Increasing capital expenditures
  - Declining free cash flow
  - Transition from positive to negative free cash flow
  - Stable or slightly declining net cash from operations

Recurring themes/risks:
  - High capital spending pressure
  - Negative free cash flow persistence
  - Management focus on free cash flow despite negative results

Key concerns:
  - No forward guidance on free cash flow limits forecasting assessment
  - Significant increase in capital expenditures impacting free cash flow
  - Negative free cash flow for two consecutive years may raise liquidity concerns

Conclusion:
Free cash flow has deteriorated significantly from positive levels in early 2021 to negative territory by the end of 2021 and throughout 2022, primarily due to rising capital expenditures that outpaced net cash provided by operating activities. Management has acknowledged this negative free cash flow trend and indicated it is likely to continue. The company consistently reports free cash flow reconciliations in SEC filings, providing transparency on the calculation. However, the absence of forward guidance on free cash flow means forecasting credibility cannot be evaluated. Overall, free cash flow is a key area of concern given its negative trajectory and the company's heavy investment spending.
