[CORE RESEARCH] CSCO: revenue

Quarters searched: 1
Filing sections searched: 38

Over the periods from Q1 2024 through Q1 2025, Cisco's revenue showed a consistent pattern of declining product revenue alongside growing service revenue. Product revenue declines ranged from 9% to 19%, while service revenue increased steadily between 4% and 6%. Management provided full-year 2024 revenue guidance twice, initially expecting $51.5 billion to $52.5 billion and later $55 billion to $56.2 billion, but the actual results missed the initial guidance range. Quarterly guidance for Q4 2024 was given but remains unresolved due to lack of final reported figures. The company consistently allocates revenue based on standalone selling prices and recognizes revenue upon transfer of control, with no noted changes in accounting policies.

Forecasting credibility: Low
There is only one guidance/actual pair with a verdict, which was a miss for the full-year 2024 revenue guidance. Subsequent guidance for Q4 2024 and FY 2024 remains unresolved, providing no additional confirmed pairs to assess forecasting accuracy. The single miss and lack of confirmed met or exceeded guidance instances indicate low forecasting credibility based on the available data.

Trends:
  - Declining product revenue
  - Increasing service revenue
  - Geographic revenue declines
  - Growth in software subscription revenue

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Customer inventory issues impacting product revenue
  - Shift from product to service revenue
  - Revenue recognition based on standalone selling prices
  - Geographic segment revenue variability

Key concerns:
  - The initial FY 2024 revenue guidance was missed, raising concerns about management's forecasting accuracy.
  - No resolved guidance/actual pairs for Q4 2024 or FY 2024 guidance updates limit confidence in forward-looking statements.
  - Consistent declines in product revenue despite growth in services may indicate structural challenges.

Conclusion:
Cisco's revenue trajectory over the reported periods shows a clear decline in product revenue offset partially by growth in service and software subscription revenues. The company missed its initial full-year 2024 revenue guidance, reporting $12.8 billion in Q2 revenue with a 6% overall decline and a 19% drop in product revenue. Service revenue increased modestly, reflecting a strategic shift. Management's revenue recognition policies remained consistent, but the missed guidance and unresolved later forecasts suggest caution in relying on management's revenue projections. Overall, revenue growth is challenged by product sales declines, though service and software segments provide some offset.
