[CORE RESEARCH] MSFT: Azure revenue growth

Quarters searched: 2
Filing sections searched: 216

What the sources show:
Across Q1 to Q3 2025, Azure revenue (reported within “Azure and other cloud services revenue growth”) consistently grew at 31%–33% year over year, while the broader “Server products and cloud services” line grew in the low-to-mid 20% range. In Q1 2025, Azure and other cloud services revenue growth was 33% alongside “Server products and cloud services revenue” growth of 23%. In Q2 2025, Azure and other cloud services increased to 31% (from 33% in Q1), and management highlighted AI services growth within Azure of 157% year over year, also noting demand remained higher than available capacity. In Q3 2025, Azure and other cloud services returned to 33% growth, and for the full fiscal year (Q4 2025 reporting), Azure and other cloud services growth was 34% with “Server products and cloud services” up 23%, indicating continued strength but without evidence of acceleration beyond the mid-30s range.

Watch points:
  - The excerpts report Azure growth as part of “Azure and other cloud services revenue growth,” without isolating pure Azure revenue, limiting precision on Azure-specific performance.
  - There is no explicit reconciliation between quarterly management expectations (e.g., Q3 constant-currency growth guidance) and the subsequently reported growth metrics for Azure; only guidance and separate reported growth figures are provided.
  - Capacity constraint is cited as an issue in Q2 2025, but the evidence provided does not quantify how this constraint changed over time.
  - AI services contributed meaningfully to Azure growth (AI services grew 157% YoY in Q2 2025)
  - Demand for Azure remained higher than available capacity (explicitly noted in Q2 2025)

=== Q2 2025 ===
Sources: transcript_msft-q2-2025.html | filing_10-q-q2-2025.html | Presentation_acbe6a9ed29c0664.html
Management commentary:
  - In Azure, we expect Q3 revenue growth to be between 31% and 32% in constant currency driven by strong demand for our portfolio of services.
  - Azure growth included 13 points from AI services, which grew 157% year over year, and was ahead of expectations even as demand continued to be higher than our available capacity.
  - Execution was strong across our core annuity sales motions with growth in the number of $100 million-plus contracts for both Azure and Microsoft 365.
  - Microsoft 365 commercial cloud revenue growth should be between 14% and 15% in constant currency, relatively stable compared to our better-than-expected Q2 results.
  - And in Dynamics 365, we expect revenue growth to be in the mid-teens driven by continued growth across all workloads.
  - However, the growth rate will be lower than FY '25 and the mix of spend will begin to shift back to short-lived assets, which are more correlated to revenue growth.
  - Azure other cloud services revenue grew 31%.
  - Commercial bookings increased 67% and 75% in constant currency and were significantly ahead of expectations driven by Azure commitments from OpenAI.
  - Dynamics 365 revenue increased 19% and 18% in constant currency, slightly ahead of expectations with growth across all workloads.
  - And in gaming, revenue decreased 7% and 8% in constant currency as content and services growth continued to be offset by hardware declines.
  - LinkedIn revenue increased 9% with continued growth across all lines of business.
  - M365 consumer cloud revenue growth should be in the mid- to high single digits, driven by M365 subscriptions.
  - And in Enterprise and Partner Services, we expect revenue growth to be in the low to mid-single digits.
  - The number of Azure OpenAI apps running on Azure databases and Azure App Services more than doubled year over year, driving significant growth in adoption across SQL, hyperscale, and Cosmos DB.
  - In commercial bookings, with a relatively flat expiry base and a strong prior year comparable in terms of large Azure contracts, we expect growth to be roughly flat year over year.
  - As a reminder, larger long-term Azure contracts, which are more unpredictable in their timing, can drive increased quarterly volatility in our bookings growth rate.
  - Revenue will continue to be driven by Azure, which, as a reminder, can have quarterly variability primarily from in-period revenue recognition depending on the mix of contracts.
