Longitudinal Summary

Over the reported periods from Q1 2021 through Q4 2022, the company consistently recorded impairment losses primarily related to the carrying value of bitcoin holdings. These impairment losses increased from $27 million in Q1 2021 to $204 million for the full year 2022, reflecting volatility in digital asset valuations. The filings also note gains on sales or conversions of bitcoin, which partially offset these impairments. The allowance for credit losses remained relatively stable, with minor fluctuations noted between $41 million and $45 million. Management disclosed that impairment losses are recognized in the period identified and that foreign exchange gains and losses vary with currency movements, but no forward guidance on losses was provided.

What was asked

Q: why does Tesla continue to fight dealership laws on a state-by-state basis versus taking it federal? Separately, why isn’t Tesla using 800-volt architecture in its vehicles? What are the advantages or disadvantages?
A: So, from Tesla’s standpoint, obviously, we’d like to have federal legislation that allows direct sales in all states, but we have not seen willingness on the part of the Congress to enact such law that would override a variety of state laws. So unfortunately, we have to fight it on a state-by-state basis. And Drew, do you want to answer the 800-volt question? Yes, sure. On the 800-volt thing, yes, so it’s really a case-by-case thing. For the smaller platform vehicles like 3 and Y, there’s some wins and losses with 800 volts, not everything is better.
