[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 186

Across 2023, management characterized gross margin for new/automotive programs as targeting “mid-20%,” but reported automotive gross margins fell sharply year over year, with total automotive gross margin declining to 21.1% in Q1 2023 (from 32.9%) and continuing down to the high-teens by late 2023 (e.g., Q3 2023 total automotive gross margin 18.7% vs. 27.9% YoY). Energy generation and storage gross margin moved in the opposite direction, improving from negative levels in Q1 2023 (-11.7% to 11.0%) to strong double-digit margins by Q4 2023 (7.4% to 18.9% for the year). In 2024, automotive gross margin remained depressed overall (e.g., Q1 2024 total automotive 18.5% vs. 21.1%; six months ended June 30, 2024 18.5% vs. 19.2%), though it showed some quarter-to-quarter improvement tied to cost, FSD, and regulatory credit dynamics. By full-year 2024, automotive gross margin was still below 2023 (total automotive 18.4% vs. 19.4%), while energy storage/energy generation strengthened further (26.2% vs. 18.9%).

Trends:
  - Automotive gross margin declined materially through 2023 (early-20s to high-teens YoY).
  - Energy generation & storage gross margin improved strongly through 2023 and remained strong/improving into 2024.
  - 2024 automotive gross margin showed modest quarter-to-quarter recovery but ended below 2023 on a full-year basis.
  - Drivers shifted toward cost per unit reductions, higher FSD/revenue and regulatory credits partially offset by lower vehicle ASPs and ramp effects (Cybertruck).

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Auto gross margin influenced by many moving parts and partially out of management’s control (tariffs, local incentives).
  - Pricing/ASP pressure (vehicle affordability actions, lower average selling price) negatively impacts gross margin.
  - Warranty/deferred revenue and other one-time adjustments can swing gross margin quarter to quarter.
  - Regulatory credits and FSD revenue can provide partial offset to pricing and cost pressures.

Key concerns:
  - The “mid-20%” gross margin expectation for launched programs was not borne out by reported automotive gross margin levels during 2023 (e.g., Q1 2023 total automotive 21.1% vs. prior-year 32.9%, and Q3 2023 total automotive 18.7% vs. 27.9%).
  - Reported gross margin progression appears highly sensitive to items such as warranty adjustments, deferred revenue timing, and technology transitions (explicitly noted in Q1 2023 discussion), indicating potential volatility.
  - Energy segment improves can mask or offset automotive weakness at consolidated/company levels, so mix/segment reporting is important when assessing gross margin quality.

Conclusion:
Gross margin evolution was bifurcated: automotive gross margin compressed significantly from 2023 into 2024, while energy generation & storage improved and strengthened. For automotive, total automotive gross margin was 21.1% in Q1 2023 (down from 32.9% YoY) and 18.7% in Q3 2023 (down from 27.9% YoY); by full-year 2024 it remained below 2023 at 18.4% vs. 19.4%. For energy, gross margin moved from -11.7% to 11.0% in Q1 2023 and reached 18.9% for the year ended December 31, 2023 (from 7.4%), then improved further to 26.2% for the year ended December 31, 2024 (from 18.9%). Overall, the evidence supports that automotive gross margin remained the persistent drag, with partial sequential recoveries in 2024 tied to cost per unit, FSD revenue, and regulatory credits, but not enough to surpass 2023 levels on a full-year basis.
