[CORE RESEARCH] VST: production

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 44

Over the periods from Q1 2019 through Q4 2019, production volumes showed variability across segments, with notable decreases in Q3 2019 in PJM, NY/NE, and MISO segments, reflecting lower power prices and reduced generation output. By Q4 2019, management referenced stable production volumes consistent with prior years, and filings indicated increased production volumes in ERCOT driven partly by acquisitions. The company’s generation facilities produced significant CO2 emissions in 2019, highlighting the environmental impact of production. Despite some fluctuations, the integrated energy model was described as stable and reliable throughout the periods.

Forecasting credibility: None
No explicit forward guidance paired with actual production results was provided in the available evidence, preventing any assessment of management's forecasting credibility on production. Without guidance-actual pairs, it is not possible to judge whether management’s forecasts were met, exceeded, or missed. Therefore, the forecasting credibility rating for production is set to null.

Trends:
  - Decreasing production volumes in Q3 2019
  - Increased production volumes in ERCOT by Q4 2019
  - Stable generation fleet operation
  - Fluctuating power prices impacting production revenue

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Impact of power price volatility on production revenue
  - Environmental regulations potentially increasing production costs
  - Reliability and safety of generation fleet
  - Integration effects from acquisitions on production volumes

Key concerns:
  - No forward guidance on production volumes was provided, limiting transparency on management’s expectations.
  - Environmental risks noted with potential impairments if regulations tighten or prices decline.
  - Variability in production volumes across segments may reflect operational or market challenges.

Conclusion:
Production volumes experienced notable declines in mid-2019 across key segments, driven by lower power prices and market conditions, but showed recovery and growth in ERCOT by year-end, partly due to acquisitions. Management emphasized stable and reliable operation of the generation fleet, though no explicit production guidance was given to benchmark against actual results. Environmental considerations and potential regulatory impacts remain material risks to production costs and asset valuations. Overall, production performance was mixed but aligned with market dynamics, with no direct evidence to assess forecasting accuracy.

[CORE RESEARCH] VST: capacity

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 44

Over the periods from Q1 2019 through Q4 2019, the company consistently discussed capacity primarily in terms of plant retirements and the composition of its generation fleet. Management highlighted significant retirements of coal and lignite plants totaling several thousand megawatts, including a required 2,000 MW retirement in MISO Zone 4 and additional retirements in Illinois. The fleet's capacity mix shifted toward natural gas and renewables, with coal capacity exposure decreasing notably. While management provided qualitative guidance on expected retirements and capacity mix changes, no explicit forward guidance paired with actual capacity figures was given. Actual capacity figures were disclosed in filings, showing a total generation fleet capacity around 38,500 MW by the end of 2019, with detailed retirements accounted for in filings.

Forecasting credibility: None
There are zero guidance/actual pairs specifically quantifying capacity forecasts against realized capacity figures. Management provided qualitative and directional guidance on retirements and capacity mix but did not issue explicit numeric forward guidance on total capacity or capacity changes that can be directly compared to actual results. Therefore, forecasting credibility for capacity cannot be assessed from the available data.

Trends:
  - Significant coal and lignite plant retirements
  - Shift toward natural gas and renewables in capacity mix
  - Stable total generation capacity around 38,500 MW
  - Decreasing coal capacity exposure over time

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Regulatory-driven retirements (e.g., MISO Zone 4)
  - Environmental and economic pressures on coal assets
  - Capacity payments and market stability in PJM
  - Transition to cleaner energy sources

Key concerns:
  - No explicit numeric forward guidance on capacity to validate forecasting accuracy
  - Potential ambiguity in capacity figures due to joint ownership interests and partial retirements
  - Accounting for capacity payments and retirements appears consistent but lacks detailed reconciliation in commentary

Conclusion:
The company's capacity has evolved through planned retirements of coal and lignite plants, driven by regulatory and economic factors, with a strategic shift toward natural gas and renewable assets. Total generation capacity remained roughly stable at about 38,500 MW by the end of 2019, reflecting these changes. However, management did not provide explicit numeric forward guidance on capacity that can be directly compared to actual results, limiting the ability to assess forecasting accuracy. The disclosed retirements and capacity mix changes are well documented in filings, supporting a clear narrative of capacity transition but without a formal forecasting track record.
