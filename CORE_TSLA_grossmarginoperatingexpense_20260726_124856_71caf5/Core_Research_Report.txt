[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 77

Over the course of 2021, gross margin trends showed a clear improvement in the automotive segment, with automotive gross margins rising steadily from around 26% in Q1 to over 29% by Q4. Energy generation and storage margins remained a challenge, with significant negative margins early in the year that slightly improved but stayed negative by year-end. Management consistently expressed optimism about automotive gross margin strength, driven by cost reductions, factory utilization, and product mix improvements. The automotive & services segment also saw margin improvements, reflecting operational efficiencies and product ramp-ups. Despite some headwinds in energy margins, the overall gross margin trajectory was positive, especially in automotive.

Forecasting credibility: High
Management's guidance on gross margin strength, particularly in automotive, was credible as evidenced by the actual results meeting or exceeding expectations. The single guidance/actual pair available for Q4 2021 was met, with automotive gross margin excluding credits reaching 29.2%, the highest yet. The consistency of margin improvements reported in filings aligns with management's optimistic commentary, supporting a high rating for forecasting credibility.

Trends:
  - Automotive gross margin steadily increasing
  - Energy generation and storage gross margin negative but improving
  - Automotive & services segment margin improvement
  - Cost reduction and factory utilization driving margin gains
  - Product ramp impacting margins

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Negative margins in energy generation and storage
  - Temporary manufacturing underutilization during product ramp
  - Impact of product mix on margins
  - Service maintenance costs in energy segment
  - Transition from Powerpack to Megapack affecting energy margins

Key concerns:
  - Energy generation and storage segment consistently reported negative gross margins, indicating ongoing challenges despite some improvements.
  - Temporary manufacturing underutilization and product ramp issues in Solar Roof and energy storage impacted margins negatively.
  - No explicit numeric guidance was given for gross margin in most quarters, limiting the ability to fully assess forecasting accuracy beyond the single Q4 2021 pair.

Conclusion:
Gross margin performance in 2021 was characterized by strong and consistent improvement in the automotive segment, with margins rising from approximately 26% to over 29% by year-end, driven by cost efficiencies, increased production, and favorable product mix. The automotive & services segment also showed margin gains, reflecting operational improvements. Conversely, the energy generation and storage segment struggled with negative margins throughout the year, primarily due to product ramp challenges and higher service costs, though some improvement was noted. Management's optimistic outlook on automotive gross margins was validated by actual results, while energy margins remain a key area of concern. Overall, gross margin trends indicate a robust automotive business with ongoing challenges in the energy segment.

[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: operating expense

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 77

Over the periods from Q1 2021 through Q4 2021, operating expenses showed a general trend of increasing in absolute terms due to investments in technology, growth, and payroll taxes related to CEO stock option exercises. Management consistently emphasized efforts to improve operational efficiency and process automation, expecting operating expenses relative to revenue to decline as sales expand. However, Q4 2021 saw a significant increase in stock-based compensation and payroll taxes, which negatively impacted operating expenses. Despite these increases, SG&A expenses as a percentage of revenue decreased from 10% in 2020 to 8% in 2021, indicating some operational leverage. Management's guidance about operating expenses relative to revenue was generally consistent with the actual results, except for the Q4 2021 period where expenses exceeded expectations due to one-time items.

Forecasting credibility: Low
There is only one explicit guidance/actual pair with a verdict, which was a miss in Q4 2021 due to higher-than-expected stock-based compensation and payroll taxes. Earlier periods did not provide precise guidance/actual pairs to evaluate forecasting accuracy. The single miss undermines the credibility of management's forecasting on operating expenses, especially given the material impact of one-time charges that were not fully anticipated.

Trends:
  - Increasing absolute operating expenses
  - Decreasing operating expenses as a percentage of revenue
  - Rising stock-based compensation expense
  - Focus on operational efficiency and automation
  - Impact of CEO stock option exercises on payroll taxes

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Non-cash stock compensation expense volatility
  - Payroll tax impact from CEO option exercises
  - Operational efficiency gains expected to offset expense growth
  - One-time impairment charges and restructuring costs

Key concerns:
  - Significant payroll tax and stock-based compensation expenses in Q4 2021 caused a notable miss versus guidance, raising concerns about forecasting accuracy for these items.
  - Non-cash stock compensation expense related to CEO awards is a recurring and material component of operating expenses, complicating trend analysis.
  - No clear accounting changes were noted, but the treatment and disclosure of stock-based compensation and payroll taxes require careful attention.

Conclusion:
Operating expenses increased during 2021 driven by investments in growth and technology, as well as significant stock-based compensation and payroll tax expenses related to CEO stock option exercises. While management consistently communicated expectations for operating expenses to decrease as a percentage of revenue through efficiency gains, the actual results showed mixed accuracy, with a notable miss in Q4 2021 due to unanticipated payroll tax and stock compensation costs. Despite this, the overall SG&A expense ratio improved year-over-year, reflecting some operational leverage. The variability and materiality of non-cash stock compensation and one-time payroll tax charges present challenges in forecasting and interpreting operating expense trends.
