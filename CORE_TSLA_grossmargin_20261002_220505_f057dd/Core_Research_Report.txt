[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 186

Across 2023, management framed auto gross margins as something generally targeted around “mid-20%,” but reported results show a sharp decline into the high-teens/low-20s (e.g., total automotive gross margin fell from 32.9% to 21.1% in Q1’23, then to 19.2% by Q2’23 and 18.7% by Q3’23). Management also emphasized that short-term variances were “minor relative to the long-term picture,” while citing mix, pricing actions, warranty adjustments on older vehicles, and other items as drivers of quarter-to-quarter changes. By Q4’23 and into 2024, total automotive gross margin continued to fluctuate mostly in a narrow band around the high-teens/low-20s (e.g., 19.4% in Q4’23 full-year comparison to 18.4% in full-year 2024). Energy generation and storage gross margin moved in the opposite direction, improving materially over the same period (from negative in early 2023 to 26.2% in full-year 2024), indicating segment mix effects on consolidated/segment-reported gross margins even as auto remained pressured.

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance/actual pairs were found specifically for gross margin beyond a general statement about “mid-20%” for launched programs; moreover, there is no clear periodic gross-margin target issuance paired to the subsequent quarter’s filing results as a basis for a met/missed evaluation. Therefore, forecasting credibility cannot be assessed from a guidance-vs-actual count for this topic, and the rating is set to null as required.

Trends:
  - Total automotive gross margin fell sharply from 2022 levels into ~19% in 2023
  - Auto gross margin fluctuated in 2024, generally in the high-teens to low-20s range
  - Energy generation and storage gross margin improved strongly throughout the period
  - Within combined automotive & services/other, gross margin mirrored the automotive pressure with partial offsets from services/other

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Sensitivity to vehicle pricing (average selling price) and affordability/price actions
  - One-time items and warranty adjustments impacting quarter-to-quarter gross margin
  - Regulatory credits and FSD revenue contributing to gross margin variability
  - Cost per unit dynamics (lower average combined cost per unit sometimes offset by lower selling price)
  - Cybertruck ramp and tariff/incentive uncertainty cited as external moving parts

Key concerns:
  - Management’s “mid-20%” gross-margin framing for launched programs did not align with reported total automotive gross margin levels in the near-term (e.g., Q1’23 total automotive 21.1% and Q2’23 19.2%), suggesting that either the company’s conceptual target did not translate into near-term outcomes or assumptions changed.
  - At the segment level, drivers are heavily mix- and item-dependent (warranty adjustments, deferred/revenue recognition impacts, regulatory credits, FSD revenue), making gross margin less directly controllable and harder to forecast consistently.

Conclusion:
Gross margin evolution is dominated by automotive weakness in 2023–2024, with total automotive gross margin declining from 32.9% to 21.1% in Q1’23 and then to 19.2% by Q2’23 and 18.7% by Q3’23. In full-year terms, total automotive gross margin decreased from 28.5% to 19.4% in 2023 and then to 18.4% in 2024. The decline/pressure in automotive was partially buffered within “automotive & services and other” by services/other gross margin improvements, but that combined measure also fell (e.g., 30.5% to 19.9% in Q1’23; 26.5% to 18.2% in full-year 2023; and 18.2% to 16.9% in full-year 2024). Meanwhile, energy generation and storage gross margin improved markedly (from -11.7% in Q1’23 to 11.0% in Q1’23 comparison, and to 26.2% in full-year 2024), indicating a meaningful offset from non-automotive segments even as auto gross margin remained the primary drag.
