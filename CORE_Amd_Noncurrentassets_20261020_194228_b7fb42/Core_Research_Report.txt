[CORE RESEARCH] AMD: Non current assets

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 86

What the sources show:
Across 2023 and into 2024, the disclosed drivers of working-capital movements that relate to non-current asset support (e.g., inventory ramp and technology-license prepayments) appear repeatedly in the cash-flow discussion of operating assets. In early 2023, changes in operating assets referenced increases in accounts receivable and prepaid expenses/other assets driven by prepayments under long-term supply agreements. Through 2022-2023 and into 2024, the company repeatedly attributes inventory increases to ramping Data Center and Client products in advanced process nodes, with additional inventory build described in 2024 as anticipation of those ramps. The inventory and prepaid expenses pattern suggests ongoing investment and longer-cycle supply/technology arrangements that would be consistent with growth in non-current asset categories, though the excerpts provided do not quantify specific non-current asset balances. In the later 2024 disclosures, the company also introduces tax-valuation-allowance language for deferred tax assets (a non-current asset component), emphasizing that valuation allowances depend on whether recovery is more-likely-than-not.

Watch points:
  - The provided excerpts discuss “Long-Lived and Intangible Assets” and include deferred tax asset valuation-allowance accounting guidance, but do not include the actual non-current asset line-item balances or detailed rollforwards, limiting comparability across periods.
  - The inventory-ramp narrative is repeated, yet the excerpt set does not explain potential impairment, useful-life changes, or asset recovery considerations—important for non-current asset risk assessment.
  - Deferred tax assets: the disclosure specifies a more-likely-than-not (probability level of more than 50%) threshold for recovery; lack of period-specific valuation allowance changes in the excerpts makes the evolution of this non-current asset risk unclear.
  - Long-cycle investment/ramp support reflected in recurring inventory and prepaid expense increases
  - Timing risk in working-capital items (accounts receivable, inventory receipts, accounts payable payments)
  - Deferred tax assets subject to valuation-allowance recoverability assessment
