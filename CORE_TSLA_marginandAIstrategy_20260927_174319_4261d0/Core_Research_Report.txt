[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: margin

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 186

Across 2023, management repeatedly characterized auto margin forecasting as difficult due to multiple moving parts, while reporting sequential declines and citing pricing actions, one-time warranty adjustments, and accounting-related changes (e.g., deferred revenue). Through Q2-Q4 2023, energy margins rose (ultimately reaching strongly positive levels in filings) while automotive gross margins remained depressed versus prior-year periods. In Q1 2024, management highlighted energy margins reaching a record (24.6%) and described slight improvement/exclusions affecting auto margin optics, but filings show auto gross margin still declined sequentially (21.1% to 18.5%). In Q3 2024, management guided that sustaining margins in Q4 would be challenging; the subsequent Q4 commentary acknowledged ramp/idle capacity impacts, and filings attribute the full-year auto gross margin decline to lower ASPs and Cybertruck ramp, partially offset by lower unit costs and higher regulatory credit/FSD revenue.

Forecasting credibility: Low
The guidance/actual verdict pattern includes one explicit Missed item: Q3 2024 guidance that sustaining margins in Q4 would be challenging was later accompanied by an automotive margin outcome that the transcript flags as missed (Q4 commentary: quarter-over-quarter decline primarily due to lower ASPs vs expected recognition effects). The Q1 2023 items were largely non-quantitative/unresolved, and Q4 2024 guidance about ramp-related margin impacts was not paired with a specific numeric “target” verdict. Overall, with at least one Missed and no clear Met/Exceeded guidance paired to subsequent precision, forecasting credibility for margin is low based on the provided guidance/actual verdict evidence.

Trends:
  - Automotive gross margins declined through 2023 versus prior-year periods and stayed pressured in 2024
  - Energy gross margins improved materially over 2023 and remained high into 2024 (record levels)
  - Sequential auto margin volatility: slight stabilization in parts of 2024 but Q4 faced further declines
  - Management increasingly framed margin outcomes around ramp costs, ASPs, and product/feature timing

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Difficulty predicting automotive margins due to moving parts (tariffs, incentives, pricing, mix)
  - Rising/ramp-related costs and idle capacity impacting launch-quarter margins
  - ASP/mix sensitivity as a dominant driver of auto gross margin changes
  - Accounting/timing effects tied to FSD-related revenue recognition and feature releases
  - Cost-reduction lags relative to when savings are expected

Key concerns:
  - Guidance was often non-quantitative for auto margin, making “accuracy” hard to evaluate; only limited guidance/actual verdict pairs were provided.
  - Potential over-reliance on timing/accounting effects (e.g., FSD-related revenue recognition) to explain margin movements, which can lead to missed expectations if market forecasts assume steadier cash gross margin dynamics.
  - In Q4 2024, the transcript indicates a Missed versus the prior expectation, while the filing attributes margin changes largely to lower ASPs and Cybertruck ramp (partially offset by lower costs and regulatory credit/FSD revenue), suggesting that key margin drivers were not fully under control.

Conclusion:
Margin trends show a persistent squeeze in automotive gross margin while energy margins expanded strongly. Filing-sourced actuals confirm automotive gross margin contraction: Q1 2024 total automotive gross margin decreased from 21.1% to 18.5% (three months ended March 31, 2024 vs March 31, 2023), and full-year 2024 decreased from 19.4% to 18.4% (year ended December 31, 2024 vs December 31, 2023). Energy margins rose substantially and reached 24.6% (three months ended March 31, 2024) and 18.9% for full-year 2023, supporting a mix-driven margin divergence. Given the single provided Missed verdict in the guidance/actual pair set and otherwise unresolved/non-quantified guidance, management’s margin forecasting credibility is assessed as low based strictly on the evidence provided.
