[CORE RESEARCH] DIS: Revenue growth

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 71

Across 2023, management framed revenue growth as driven by Parks, Experiences, and Products (with both revenue and operating income increasing by more than 10% versus the prior year) while noting mixed trends in certain segments such as Domestic Linear Networks affiliate revenue (down 2% due to subscriber declines, partially offset by rate increases). In Q4 2023 (fiscal 2022 results presented), Disney reported double-digit growth in service revenues and cited specific drivers such as higher theme park and resort revenues and subscription revenue growth. By Q3 2024, management explicitly highlighted that Q3 revenue growth was only 2%. For fiscal 2024 (reported in Q4 2024), the company reported overall revenues up 3% to $91.4 billion, and revenues service revenues up 3% to $81.8 billion, indicating a slower growth profile versus the stronger 2023 narrative and earlier disclosed fiscal growth rates.

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance/actual pairs specific to the topic of revenue growth were provided in the evidence, so forecasting credibility cannot be assessed. While management commentary described revenue growth drivers (e.g., Parks, Experiences, and Products and Q3 2% revenue growth), there is no comparable forward guidance figure for revenue growth to validate against subsequent results.

Trends:
  - 2023 commentary suggests stronger revenue growth at Parks, Experiences, and Products (more than 10% vs. prior year)
  - Mixed segment performance in 2023 (Domestic Linear Networks affiliate revenue down 2% despite contractual rate increases)
  - Revenue growth moderated by Q3 2024 (explicitly stated as 2%)
  - Fiscal 2024 overall revenue growth was modest (up 3% to $91.4 billion; service revenues up 3% to $81.8 billion)

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Segment-level mix effects (affiliate subscriber trends vs. contractual rate increases)
  - Revenue growth tied to subscription and parks/experiences performance
  - Execution and external conditions impacting growth and profitability (general risk language)
  - Florida legislative/regulatory steps flagged as potentially negatively impacting growth/profitability (not quantified here)

Key concerns:
  - Guidance/actual evidence for revenue growth is not available for a credibility assessment (no guidance-vs-actual pair count provided for this topic).
  - Growth appears to decelerate over time in the evidence (explicitly 2% in Q3 2024; 3% for fiscal 2024), which may indicate underlying demand/segment normalization, but the provided excerpts do not show a single consistent bridge from quarter-by-quarter guidance to results.
  - Potential topical risk from Florida legislation/regulatory steps could affect growth, but the provided excerpts do not quantify the impact on revenue.

Conclusion:
Revenue growth evolved from a 2023 narrative emphasizing stronger contribution from Parks, Experiences, and Products (revenue and operating income increasing by more than 10% vs. the prior year) and specific upsides in theme parks (e.g., higher admissions components and merchandise/food/beverage volume and spend) to a more moderate growth profile later. In Q3 2024, management stated the company had 2% revenue growth. For fiscal 2024, the company reported revenues increasing 3% to $91.4 billion, and revenues service revenues increasing 3% to $81.8 billion. Overall, the evidence supports that revenue growth remained positive throughout, but decelerated by the later period.

[CORE RESEARCH] DIS: Operating margin

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 71

Across the period, management discussed operating margin primarily through segment-level or qualitative comparisons, with recurring references to Parks/Experiences recovery and DTC margin expansion. In Q3 2023, the only forward-looking element related to DPEP operating margins indicated expectations for Q4 2023 to exceed the prior year, but no resolved “guidance vs actual” margin figure is provided in the excerpt to close the loop. In Q2 2024, the guidance is framed as an aspiration for DTC “double-digit operating margin,” while the company simultaneously declined to provide competitors with timing details. By Q4 2024, the filings show operating income/loss movement in DTC and other segments, and disclose a reporting-unit aggregation change for entertainment businesses based on projected long-term operating margins, which can affect comparability of reported “operating margin” measures over time.

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance/actual pairs for operating margin were found with enough precision to judge a Met vs Miss pattern. Where management provided margin-related expectations (e.g., DPEP Q4 2023 to exceed prior year; DTC double-digit operating margin aspiration), the provided evidence does not include a corresponding filing-sourced actual margin metric that resolves the guidance in the excerpts, so forecasting credibility cannot be assessed from a guidance-vs-actual comparison count of zero.

Trends:
  - Margin narrative shifted from Parks/Experiences recovery (Q3 2023) toward DTC operating leverage aspirations (Q2 2024)
  - Segment operating income/loss volatility appears across linear networks (Q4 2023) and DTC turning to positive operating income (FY 2024 disclosure)
  - Reporting structure changed in FY 2024 by aggregating entertainment businesses, potentially impacting how operating margin is presented/comparable

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Reliance on excluding/adjusting items (e.g., Starcruiser accelerated depreciation) for margin comparisons
  - DTC margin as a strategic goal stated as an aspiration rather than a time-bound target
  - Segment mix and regional performance (e.g., international parks, ESPN/Star) driving margin outcomes
  - Potential comparability risk due to entertainment reporting-unit aggregation

Key concerns:
  - Guidance resolution risk: Q3 2023 provides an expectation for Q4 2023 DPEP operating margins to exceed the prior year, but the provided excerpts do not include the corresponding filing-sourced DPEP operating margin outcome needed for a guidance-vs-actual assessment.
  - DTC margin goal lacks timing specificity: Q2 2024 states an aspiration for double-digit operating margin and declines to provide competitors the pathway/timing, limiting ability to evaluate forecast accuracy.
  - Accounting/reporting comparability: FY 2024 filing excerpt discloses aggregation of entertainment businesses into a single reporting unit based on projected long-term operating margins, which may change how operating margin is measured/presented versus prior reporting.

Conclusion:
Operating margin trends are discussed in management commentary through segment recovery (domestic Parks/Experiences down vs prior year in Q3 2023, DPEP slightly higher) and through an aspiration for DTC to reach double-digit operating margin (Q2 2024). However, from the evidence provided, there is insufficient guidance-vs-actual resolution for operating margin to quantify whether margins expanded or contracted relative to stated targets in a way that enables forecasting credibility. Filing excerpts indicate meaningful operating income/loss dynamics around the components that feed operating margin (e.g., Direct-to-Consumer moving from an operating loss of $2,496 million to operating income of $143 million in FY 2024), but a precise, consistently reported operating margin metric for the guidance periods is not provided. Additionally, FY 2024 reporting-unit aggregation for entertainment businesses based on operating margin projections introduces potential comparability constraints when assessing operating margin across time.
