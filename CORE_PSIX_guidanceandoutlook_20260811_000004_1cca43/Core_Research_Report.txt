[CORE RESEARCH] PSIX: guidance

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 31

Throughout 2015, management provided some forward-looking statements and ranges related to revenue and sector outlooks, including raising 2015 and 2016 revenue guidance early in the year. However, many of these statements were qualitative or directional rather than precise numeric guidance. There were mentions of revising guidance downward mid-year due to market challenges, but no specific numeric guidance-actual pairs were documented. The company also noted accounting changes related to debt issuance costs and inventory measurement, but these did not directly affect guidance. Overall, the company appeared cautious and adjusted expectations in response to market conditions without providing consistent, quantifiable guidance figures.

Forecasting credibility: None
No explicit guidance versus actual result pairs were provided in the evidence, preventing any assessment of management's forecasting accuracy or credibility. Without numeric guidance-actual comparisons, it is not possible to judge whether management tends to meet, exceed, or miss their forecasts. Therefore, the forecasting credibility rating must remain null.

Trends:
  - Initial raised revenue guidance early 2015
  - Mid-year downward revision of outlook
  - Qualitative rather than quantitative guidance
  - Accounting changes noted but unrelated to guidance

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Market weakness in oil and gas sector
  - Cautious outlook due to capital spending sluggishness
  - Accounting standard updates affecting financial presentation

Key concerns:
  - Lack of numeric guidance-actual pairs limits ability to assess forecasting reliability
  - Frequent references to external market conditions impacting outlook without precise quantification
  - Accounting changes noted but no indication of impact on guidance comparability

Conclusion:
Management provided some directional and range-based guidance during 2015 but did not supply consistent, quantifiable guidance-actual pairs necessary to evaluate forecasting accuracy. The company adjusted its outlook downward mid-year in response to market challenges, particularly in oil and gas, but these adjustments were described qualitatively. Accounting changes related to debt issuance costs and inventory measurement were disclosed but did not affect guidance comparability. Given the absence of concrete guidance-actual comparisons, no definitive conclusion on the reliability of management's guidance can be drawn.

[CORE RESEARCH] PSIX: outlook

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 31

Throughout 2015, management frequently expressed confidence in their outlook, referencing opportunities in on-road sales and strategic deals as drivers for future performance. They provided a specific full-year revenue outlook range of $430 million to $470 million in Q2 2015 but did not provide precise forward guidance figures in other quarters. Commentary often emphasized the need to align cost structures with expected revenues to sustain profitability. Despite qualitative optimism, no concrete guidance-actual pairs were available to validate the accuracy of these outlook statements.

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance-actual pairs were found for the outlook topic, preventing any assessment of forecasting credibility. Management's qualitative statements about confidence and outlook cannot substitute for measurable guidance versus actual comparisons. Therefore, the credibility rating must remain null due to lack of verifiable data.

Trends:
  - consistent qualitative confidence
  - focus on on-road sales growth
  - emphasis on cost structure alignment
  - limited quantitative guidance

Recurring themes/risks:
  - dependence on large customer reengagement
  - need for cost-revenue matching
  - uncertainty in sustained profitability

Key concerns:
  - absence of concrete guidance-actual pairs limits ability to judge forecasting accuracy
  - no accounting or reporting inconsistencies noted in outlook disclosures
  - reliance on qualitative outlook statements without measurable targets

Conclusion:
The outlook commentary over 2015 reflects management's confidence in growth opportunities and strategic positioning, particularly in on-road sales and key customer relationships. However, the lack of specific, measurable guidance paired with actual results prevents an objective evaluation of forecasting reliability. Investors should be cautious in interpreting the outlook as it is primarily qualitative and lacks the transparency of verifiable targets. Future disclosures with explicit guidance and subsequent actuals would be necessary to establish a credible forecasting track record.
