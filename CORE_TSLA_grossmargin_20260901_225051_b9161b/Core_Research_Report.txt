Over the course of 2024, gross margin trends showed mixed performance across segments. The total automotive segment experienced a general decline in gross margin in the first half of the year, with a slight recovery in Q3 before ending the year lower than the prior year. The automotive & services and other segment followed a similar pattern, with decreases in the first half and some improvement in Q3, but ultimately a year-end decline. Conversely, the energy generation and storage segment demonstrated consistent and significant gross margin improvement throughout the year, driven by cost reductions and favorable product mix changes.

Recurring themes/risks:

Key concerns:
  - No forward-looking guidance on gross margin limits ability to evaluate management's forecasting accuracy.
  - Some inconsistency in segment reporting, e.g., automotive & services combined versus automotive alone, requires careful interpretation.
  - Gross margin declines in automotive segments despite revenue growth suggest margin pressure from pricing and product mix changes.

Conclusion:
Gross margin for the total automotive segment showed a downward trend in the first half of 2024, with a modest rebound in Q3, but ultimately ended the year lower at 18.4% compared to 19.4% the prior year. This decline was attributed to lower average selling prices and the ramp of new products like the Cybertruck, partially offset by cost reductions and increased regulatory credit and FSD revenues. The automotive & services and other segment mirrored this pattern, ending the year at 16.9%, down from 18.2%. In contrast, the energy generation and storage segment demonstrated strong margin improvement, rising to 26.2% for the year, driven by cost efficiencies and favorable product mix shifts. Overall, while automotive margins faced headwinds, the energy segment's margin gains provided a positive offset, reflecting differing dynamics across the company's business lines.
