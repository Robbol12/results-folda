[CORE RESEARCH] PEP: Organic revenue growth

Quarters searched: 2
Filing sections searched: 158

Across 2023, the filings consistently define organic revenue growth as a non-GAAP metric that strips out foreign exchange, acquisitions/divestitures, and (when applicable) the 53rd reporting week, and regional commentary ties changes primarily to net pricing and organic volume partially offset by foreign exchange. In Q1–Q3 2023, the narrative shows organic performance contributions (e.g., APAC net revenue increased 4% in the 12 weeks ended Q3 2023, attributed to pricing and organic volume) while also quantifying FX drag in percentage points. In 2024, the filings continue to attribute net revenue/organic growth dynamics to net pricing and organic volume, again quantifying FX impacts over 12- and 24-week windows (e.g., 5.0–5.5 percentage-point FX headwinds in Q1/Q2 2024). By Q3–Q4 2024, management discussion highlights a step-down from about 4% organic sales growth to low-single digits and references longer-term organic sales guidance of 4% to 6%, but the excerpts provided do not include a completed guidance-versus-actual numeric comparison for organic revenue growth in any quarter.

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance/actual pairs for organic revenue growth were found in the provided evidence, so forecasting credibility cannot be assessed. While management commentary in Q3 2024 discusses a step-down and Q4 2024 references longer-term (4% to 6%) and 2025 low-single-digit organic sales guidance, the provided excerpt set does not include resolved outcomes that match those specific guidance targets with corresponding actual reported figures.

Trends:
  - Organic revenue growth defined consistently as non-GAAP (FX, M&A, and 53rd-week adjusted)
  - Net pricing and organic volume cited as primary drivers, with foreign exchange as a recurring headwind
  - FX quantified as material drag in multiple periods (percentage-point impacts repeatedly mentioned)
  - Management narrative in 2024 indicates organic growth moved from ~4% range toward low-single digits

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Foreign exchange volatility as a persistent headwind (quantified in percentage points)
  - Reliance on net pricing and organic volume to offset FX and other operating impacts
  - Potential domestic improvement shortfalls versus expectations (cited in Q3 2024 discussion)
  - Uncertainty around the trajectory of organic sales growth as guidance range narrows

Key concerns:
  - Forecasting credibility cannot be evaluated because no matched guidance-versus-actual pairs for organic revenue growth are present in the provided evidence.
  - There is an apparent mismatch between management’s discussion of a step-down in organic sales growth (Q3 2024) and the provided excerpted regional net revenue/organic discussion, but the excerpts do not include a full-year or quarter-level organic growth actual that resolves the stated guidance.
  - Accounting/reporting consistency appears stable in the provided excerpts (same non-GAAP definition and 53rd-week adjustment language), but the excerpt set does not provide sufficient numeric organic growth actuals to verify the definition’s application period-by-period beyond FX quantification.

Conclusion:
The evidence provided supports that organic revenue growth was underpinned by net pricing and organic volume, while foreign exchange was repeatedly quantified as a significant offsetting factor. In Q3 2023, APAC net revenue increased 4% over the 12 weeks ended, attributed primarily to effective net pricing and net organic volume growth. In 2024, filings attribute net revenue movements to net pricing and organic volume growth, partially offset by unfavorable FX with explicit percentage-point impacts (e.g., 5-percentage-point in Q1 2024 and 5.5-percentage-point in Q2 2024). However, because the excerpts do not include complete guidance-versus-actual outcomes for organic revenue growth, the dataset is insufficient to conclusively judge the magnitude and direction of organic revenue growth versus any specific forward-looking target.

Therefore, the gross margin topic cannot be assessed quantitatively over time using the evidence supplied.

Forecasting credibility: None

Because there is no disclosed gross margin guidance (or any specified management forecast) and no filing-sourced gross margin actuals to compare against, forecasting credibility cannot be evaluated. Any qualitative management commentary is not available in the excerpts, so no Met/Missed pattern can be constructed.

Trends:

  - No observable directional gross margin movement from the provided excerpts (no gross margin % disclosed)

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Reliance on adjusted/non-GAAP gross profit measures excluding 'items affecting comparability'
  - Potential comparability/accounting adjustments affecting gross profit/gross margin presentation (details not provided in excerpts)

Key concerns:
  - The provided filing excerpts mention gross profit/cost of sales but do not provide gross margin percentages, so the actual gross margin trend cannot be determined from this evidence.
  - No gross margin guidance is shown, preventing any validation of management forecasting against actual results.
  - The excerpts repeatedly reference 'items affecting comparability,' but specific adjustment categories/amounts are not included here, limiting insight into what is driving gross profit presentation changes.

Conclusion:
Gross margin cannot be concluded from the provided evidence because the excerpts do not contain gross margin percentages, gross margin reconciliations, or any forward gross margin guidance to compare against later actuals. What can be evidenced is that PepsiCo’s filings consistently discuss cost of sales and gross profit (and related line items) as non-GAAP measures adjusted for comparability items across the periods shown. Without filing-sourced gross margin figures and without guidance/actual pairs, there is insufficient information to assess whether gross margin improved, deteriorated, or was volatile. Accordingly, the evidence supports only that gross profit/gross margin-related metrics are presented with adjustments, not how gross margin actually performed.
