[CORE RESEARCH] VST: capacity

Quarters searched: 12
Filing sections searched: 321

Over the period from 2019 through early 2026, the company has actively managed its generation capacity through retirements, upgrades, and new developments. Early on, the company retired significant coal-fired capacity as required by regulatory amendments, notably retiring approximately 2,000 MW in MISO Zone 4. Subsequently, the company focused on increasing capacity through upgrades at natural gas plants and expanding nuclear capacity, with guidance consistently highlighting opportunities to add over 3 GW of nuclear capacity at Beaver Valley and Comanche Peak. Capacity revenues and liquidity related to capacity have fluctuated, reflecting market conditions and operational changes. By 2025-2026, the company reported a large fleet of combined-cycle gas assets totaling 26 GW and ongoing capacity additions expected to be online by 2028.

Forecasting credibility: High
The company provided five guidance/actual pairs related to capacity, with a consistent pattern of meeting or exceeding its forecasts. For example, the announced retirement of 2,000 MW in MISO Zone 4 was confirmed by actual retirements and capacity payments reported in filings. Later guidance on nuclear capacity additions of approximately 3.2 GW was supported by actual contract signings and capacity under development. The repeated confirmation of capacity-related guidance through filings and transcripts demonstrates strong forecasting credibility.

Trends:
  - Coal capacity retirements early in period
  - Shift toward natural gas and nuclear capacity additions
  - Increasing liquidity and borrowing base related to capacity
  - Focus on long-term multi-year capacity contracts
  - Upgrades adding incremental summer and winter capacity

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Regulatory-driven retirements impacting capacity
  - Capacity market revenue variability
  - Capital expenditures for capacity upgrades and development
  - Reliance on capacity auctions and bilateral contracts
  - Potential market participant defaults on capacity penalties

Key concerns:
  - No significant inconsistencies or accounting changes were noted in capacity reporting; capacity revenues are consistently recognized ratably over time as capacity is made available.
  - The company disclosed estimates of market participant defaults on PJM capacity performance penalties related to Winter Storm Elliott, which could impact capacity revenue recognition.
  - Liquidity and available capacity under credit facilities fluctuate but are transparently reported.

Conclusion:
The company's capacity management has evolved from mandated coal retirements to strategic capacity additions primarily through nuclear and natural gas plant upgrades. The firm has demonstrated strong execution in meeting its capacity guidance, supported by detailed disclosures of capacity retirements, additions, and related revenues. The consistent recognition of capacity revenues and transparent reporting of liquidity and borrowing capacity underpin a reliable capacity profile. Overall, the company appears well-positioned to maintain and grow its generation capacity, with credible forecasts and ongoing investments aligned with market and regulatory demands.
