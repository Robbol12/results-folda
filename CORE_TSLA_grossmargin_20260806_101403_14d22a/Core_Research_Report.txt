[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 4

Over the course of 2024, gross margin trends showed a mixed pattern across segments. The total automotive gross margin generally declined year-over-year in most periods, dropping from 21.1% in Q1 2023 to 18.5% in Q1 2024 and ending the year at 18.4%. The automotive & services segment followed a similar downward trend, with margins decreasing from 19.9% in Q1 2023 to 16.7% in Q1 2024 and finishing the year at 16.9%. Conversely, the energy generation and storage segment experienced a significant improvement, with gross margin rising from 11.0% in Q1 2023 to 26.2% by year-end 2024. Sequential quarterly fluctuations were observed, including a brief increase in automotive gross margin in Q3 2024 before declining again in Q4. Management attributed changes primarily to vehicle pricing, production ramp effects, and regulatory credits.

Trends:
  - Declining automotive gross margin year-over-year
  - Improving energy generation and storage gross margin
  - Quarterly fluctuations in automotive margins
  - Decreasing automotive & services segment margins

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Lower average selling prices impacting automotive margins
  - Production ramp effects (e.g., Cybertruck) influencing costs
  - Regulatory credits and FSD revenue partially offsetting margin declines

Key concerns:
  - No forward-looking guidance on gross margin limits ability to evaluate management's forecasting accuracy.
  - Gross margin declines in automotive segments may reflect pricing pressures or cost challenges.
  - Inconsistencies in sequential quarterly margin movements suggest volatility in cost or pricing dynamics.

Conclusion:
Gross margin for the total automotive segment showed a general decline throughout 2024, falling from 21.1% in Q1 2023 to 18.4% by year-end 2024, driven by lower average selling prices and production ramp costs, partially offset by regulatory credits and FSD revenue. The automotive & services segment mirrored this downward trend, while the energy generation and storage segment experienced a notable margin improvement, more than doubling from 11.0% to 26.2% over the same period. The absence of forward guidance on gross margin prevents assessment of management's forecasting reliability, but the reported figures indicate ongoing margin pressure in automotive operations alongside growth in energy-related margins.
