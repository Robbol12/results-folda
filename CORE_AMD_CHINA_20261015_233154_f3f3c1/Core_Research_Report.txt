[CORE RESEARCH] AMD: CHINA

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 86

What the sources show:
Across 2023, the company’s disclosures emphasized uncertainty in U.S.-China trade relations, export controls targeting advanced computing/semiconductors/AI, and the operational risk from geopolitical changes involving China and Taiwan that could disrupt Taiwan-based wafer foundries and manufacturing subcontractors. The company also described extensive third-party manufacturing and final testing activity located in China (as well as Malaysia and Taiwan), and later expanded detail around regulatory developments such as ENERGY STAR-based procurement/market-entry rules and restrictions on lead and other materials in electronic products in the EU and China. In 2024, the focus sharpened on specific BIS requirements for advanced computing items destined for China, including a requirement to file a Notified Advanced Computing (NAC) notification 25 days before shipping certain Versal FPGAs to China. In the Q3 2024 earnings discussion, management addressed a “China question” noting underrepresentation in China on the service side, while also describing positive enterprise momentum tied to broader adoption and cloud/on-prem migrations that could relate to China demand, though the excerpt does not quantify China-specific results. Overall, the China-related narrative evolves from broad geopolitical/export-control uncertainty in 2023 to more specific compliance timing and continued supply-chain geography, while management simultaneously points to improving enterprise adoption dynamics in 2024.

Watch points:
  - Supply chain and operational exposure to China/Taiwan geopolitics is repeatedly highlighted as a risk to business/financial results (Q1 2023, Q3 2023, Q1 2024).
  - Export compliance burden becomes more concrete in 2024 with a 25-day NAC notification requirement before shipping certain Versal FPGAs to China, increasing timing/compliance complexity (Q1 2024).
  - Management explicitly states it is “underrepresented” in the China market on the service side (Q3 2024), which may indicate a coverage gap versus demand, but the provided excerpt does not reconcile this with any China-specific revenue metrics.
  - U.S.-China trade relations uncertainty and targeted export regulations
  - BIS export-control requirements for advanced computing items destined for China
  - Geopolitical changes involving China/Taiwan disrupting Taiwan-based manufacturing/wafer foundry operations
  - Third-party manufacturing concentration outside the U.S., including China
  - Additional jurisdictional procurement/material restrictions impacting electronics (including China/EU)

Analyst Q&A:
  Q — Harlan Sur — J: Good afternoon. Thanks for taking my question. Lisa, on your core EPYC business, you did start to see recovery in enterprise last quarter. It was broad-based across many verticals.
You've got -- you've also got a growing share dynamic as well, right? And it sounds like that trend continued in the September quarter. Do you anticipate continued follow-through in terms of quarter-on-quarter growth this quarter for enterprise? And then maybe what sort of enterprise and general cloud demand trends are you seeing out of China?
  A: Sure. So Harlan, thanks for the question. We did see positive growth momentum here in the third quarter in enterprise. We are getting broader adoption and we're seeing that growth both in on-prem deployments as well as cloud third-party deployments.
And so we're very happy with sort of the adoption of our cloud instances when people are doing migrations, they're migrating from on-prem to cloud, they're migrating to AMD or they're migrating from older cloud instances to newer cloud instances. We're seeing migrations to AMD. So I think that helps us in both the Enterprise segment as well as the Cloud segment. As we go forward into the fourth quarter, I think we are expecting another quarter of growth on a sequential basis for our Server business and strength in both Enterprise and Cloud.
And I think as we think about our opportunities going forward, the Enterprise business is a place where we have been underrepresented. I think our portfolio has strengthened. So the platforms that are being offered by the OEMs have broadened with not just Zen 4, but also with the Zen 5 portfolio launch. And so I think those are opportunities for us in 2025. Yes. On your China question, we are underrepresented in China market in the service side. So as Lisa said, that's another opportunity for us to continue to gain share.
