Longitudinal Summary

Across 2023 through 2024, AbbVie consistently described revenue growth as being driven by volume/market share uptake and broader market growth across key products, with some periods noting pricing headwinds (e.g., Rinvoq) or timing effects such as retail inventory destocking. In 2024, filings explicitly linked better operating cost dynamics (SG&A leverage) to “leverage from revenue growth,” reinforcing that revenue expansion was translating into operating leverage. By Q4 2024, management shifted from historical performance descriptions to forward-looking growth framing post–U.S. Humira LOE, emphasizing a runway for mid-to-high single-digit growth and expecting specific contributions from ex-Humira assets (e.g., Skyrizi and Rinvoq). However, in the provided evidence set, there is no guidance-versus-actual comparison for the same metric pairs at the same time horizon (except “outcome: Unresolved”), so the trajectory is supported by actual historical product and geographic growth excerpts rather than validated forecasting accuracy.

Trends

  • Consistent positive product-level net revenue growth across 2023-2024 for multiple key assets
  • Growth drivers repeatedly attributed to market share uptake and market growth (with occasional pricing/timing offsets)
  • Revenue growth improving operating leverage (SG&A percentage favorably impacted by revenue leverage in Q2 2024)
  • By Q4 2024, management forecast a multi-year growth runway tied to ex-Humira assets after Humira LOE

Recurring Themes / Risks

  • Reliance on ex-Humira platform execution for sustained growth
  • Pricing and channel/inventory timing as potential offsets to growth
  • Humira erosion magnitude as a continuing headwind to total company revenue

Key Concerns

  • Forecasting credibility cannot be evaluated from the provided material because no guidance/actual pairs are resolved for revenue growth (all are marked unresolved).
  • Some product growth commentary includes explicit headwinds (e.g., Rinvoq partially offset by unfavorable pricing in Q2 2023; pricing timing effects noted in Q1 2023), suggesting revenue growth may be sensitive to pricing and retail/channel dynamics.

Conclusion

Revenue growth appears robust across the covered historical periods, with repeated filing excerpts showing net revenues rising for major products: Ubrelvy (+10% in Q1 2023; +20% in full-year 2023; +34% in Q1 2024; +18% in Q2 2024), Rinvoq (+51% in Q1 2023; +57% in Q2 2023; +47% in Q3 2024; +53% in 2024), and Venclexta/Vraylar/Qulipta also showing positive growth in the excerpts provided. Management attributed growth primarily to market share uptake and market growth, while noting partial offsets from unfavorable pricing and inventory destocking/timing effects in some quarters. In Q4 2024, management stated a return to sales/revenue growth (full-year revenue up 4.6% on an operational basis despite $5 billion of U.S. Humira erosion in 2024) and guided toward mid-single-digit 2025 revenue growth; however, the provided evidence does not include realized 2025 outcomes to validate the guidance.

Sales rebate accruals for Medicaid, Medicare and managed care programs
Description of the Matter
As discussed in Note 2 to the consolidated financial statements under the caption “Revenue Recognition,” the Company established provisions for sales rebates in the same period the related product is sold. At December 31, 2023, the Company had $13,627 million in sales rebate accruals, a large portion of which were for rebates provided to pharmacy benefit managers, state government Medicaid programs, insurance companies that administer Medicare drug plans and private entities for Medicaid, Medicare and managed care programs. In order to establish these sales rebate accruals, the Company estimated its rebates based upon the identification of the products subject to a rebate, the applicable price and rebate terms and the estimated lag time between the sale and payment of the rebate.
Auditing the Medicaid, Medicare and managed care sales rebate accruals was complex and required significant auditor judgment because the accruals consider multiple subjective and complex estimates and assumptions. These estimates and assumptions included the estimated inventory in the distribution channel, which impacts the lag time between the sale to the customer and payment of the rebate and the final payer related to product sales, which impacts the applicable price and rebate terms. In deriving these estimates and assumptions, the Company used both internal and external sources of information to estimate product in the distribution channels, payer mix, prescription volumes and historical experience. Management supplemented its historical data analysis with qualitative adjustments based upon changes in rebate trends, rebate programs and contract terms, legislative changes, or other significant events which indicate a change in the reserve is appropriate.
How We Addressed the Matter in Our Audit
We obtained an understanding, evaluated the design and tested the operating effectiveness of controls over the Company’s sales rebate accruals for Medicaid, Medicare and managed care programs. This included testing controls over management’s review of the significant assumptions and other inputs used in the estimation of Medicaid, Medicare and managed care rebates, among others, including the significant assumptions discussed above. The testing was inclusive of management’s controls to evaluate the accuracy of its reserve judgments to actual rebates paid, rebate validation and processing, and controls to ensure that the data used to evaluate and support the significant assumptions was complete, accurate and, where applicable, verified to external data sources.
To test the sales rebate accruals for Medicaid, Medicare and managed care programs, our audit procedures included, among others, understanding and evaluating the significant assumptions and underlying data used in management’s calculations. Our testing of significant assumptions included corroboration to external data sources. We evaluated the reasonableness of assumptions considering industry and economic trends, product profiles, and other regulatory factors. We assessed the historical accuracy of management’s estimates by comparing actual activity to previous estimates and performed analytical procedures, based on internal and external data sources, to evaluate the completeness of the reserves. For Medicaid, we involved a specialist with an understanding of statutory reimbursement requirements to assess the consistency of the Company’s calculation methodologies with applicable government regulations and policy.

Sales rebate accruals for Medicaid, Medicare and managed care programs
Description of the Matter
As discussed in Note 2 to the consolidated financial statements under the caption “Revenue Recognition,” the Company established provisions for sales rebates in the same period the related product is sold. At December 31, 2024, the Company had $14,304 million in sales rebate accruals, a large portion of which were for rebates accrued for pharmacy benefit managers, state government Medicaid programs, insurance companies that administer Medicare drug plans and private entities for Medicaid, Medicare and managed care programs. In order to establish the rebate accruals, the Company estimated its rebates based on estimates and assumptions, including the determination of the related payer of the rebate based on sales trends, changes in rebate contracts which impacts the applicable price and rebate terms, and the corresponding lag in payment timing.
Auditing the Medicaid, Medicare and managed care sales rebate accruals was complex and required significant auditor judgment because the accruals consider multiple subjective and complex estimates and assumptions. In deriving these estimates and assumptions, the Company used both internal and external sources of information. Management supplemented its historical data analysis with qualitative adjustments based upon changes in rebate trends, rebate programs, contract terms, legislative changes, or other significant events which indicate a change in the reserve is appropriate.
How We Addressed the Matter in Our Audit
We obtained an understanding, evaluated the design and tested the operating effectiveness of controls over the Company’s sales rebate accruals for Medicaid, Medicare and managed care programs. This included testing controls over management’s review of the significant assumptions and other inputs used in the estimation of Medicaid, Medicare and managed care rebates, among others, including the significant assumptions discussed above. Specifically, we tested management’s controls to evaluate the sufficiency of its reserve estimates by comparing to actual rebates paid, controls over rebate validation and processing, and controls to ensure that the data used to evaluate and support the significant assumptions was complete and accurate.
To test the sales rebate accruals and assess the historical accuracy of management's estimate for Medicaid, Medicare and managed care programs, our audit procedures included independently calculating the sales rebate accruals based on historical payments and performing a hindsight analysis on the reserves recorded. Our testing of significant assumptions included corroborating management's estimate of the rebate claims processing lag time for each type of rebate. We evaluated the reasonableness of assumptions considering industry and economic trends, product profiles, and other regulatory factors. For Medicaid, we involved a specialist with an understanding of statutory reimbursement requirements to assess the consistency of the Company’s calculation methodologies with applicable government regulations and policy.
