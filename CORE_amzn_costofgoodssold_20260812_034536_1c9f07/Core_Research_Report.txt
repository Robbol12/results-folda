[CORE RESEARCH] AMZN: cost of goods sold

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 130

Over the periods from Q1 2021 through Q4 2022, cost of goods sold (COGS) consistently increased in absolute terms, primarily driven by higher product and shipping costs associated with increased sales volumes. Management highlighted several contributing factors including expanded fulfillment network investments, rising transportation costs, increased wage rates and incentives, and inefficiencies due to constrained labor markets and global supply chain challenges. Sensitivity disclosures showed a rising impact of inventory valuation allowances on COGS, increasing from approximately $275 million per 1% allowance in early 2021 to around $405 million by Q3 2022. Foreign exchange rate changes notably reduced reported COGS in 2022, with reductions reaching up to $10.8 billion by year-end. There were no indications of accounting changes affecting COGS reporting during this period.

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance versus actual pairs were provided for cost of goods sold, which is typical for this type of mechanical accounting line item. Therefore, it is not possible to assess management's forecasting credibility for COGS based on the available data. Any commentary on cost trends or drivers does not substitute for formal guidance-actual comparisons.

Trends:
  - Increasing absolute cost of goods sold
  - Rising product and shipping costs
  - Growing impact of inventory valuation allowances
  - Increased investments in fulfillment network
  - Higher transportation and labor costs
  - Foreign exchange rate reductions in 2022

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Supply chain constraints
  - Labor market tightness and wage inflation
  - Inventory valuation sensitivity
  - Fulfillment network inefficiencies
  - Foreign exchange volatility

Key concerns:
  - No forward guidance on cost of goods sold limits ability to evaluate forecasting accuracy.
  - Inventory valuation allowance sensitivity increased notably, indicating rising risk of write-downs impacting COGS.
  - Foreign exchange impacts in 2022 significantly reduced reported COGS, which could obscure underlying cost trends.

Conclusion:
Cost of goods sold steadily increased from 2021 through 2022, driven by higher product and shipping costs linked to increased sales, as well as investments in fulfillment infrastructure and rising labor and transportation expenses. The company disclosed growing sensitivity of COGS to inventory valuation allowances, which could materially affect reported costs. Foreign exchange rate fluctuations in 2022 substantially reduced COGS, partially offsetting cost increases. Management did not provide forward guidance on COGS, so forecasting credibility cannot be assessed. Overall, COGS trends reflect operational challenges from supply chain and labor market constraints, with no accounting changes noted that would affect comparability.
