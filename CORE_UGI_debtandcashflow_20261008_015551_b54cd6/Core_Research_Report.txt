Across the periods shown, operating cash flow increased versus the prior-year comparison in each reported window: $641M vs $424M (Q2 2024 six-month), $1,031M vs $857M (Q3 2024 nine-month), $1,182M vs $1,107M (Q4 2024 fiscal year), and $164M vs $119M (Q1 2025 three-month). Investing cash flow remained consistently more cash outflow than inflow, with cash used by investing activities narrowing in Q2/Q3 (e.g., $353M used vs $473M used; $551M used vs $761M used), before reversing direction in Q1 2025 where investing activities used $232M (vs $165M). Financing cash flow was cash outflow in both fiscal years, widening materially to $506M used in Fiscal 2024 from $168M used in Fiscal 2023. The 'operating cash flow before changes in operating working capital' metric also trended higher (e.g., $1,055M vs $1,089M in Q2 six-month; $1,181M vs $1,209M in Q3 nine-month; $1,215M vs $1,258M in Q4; $427M vs $376M in Q1 2025), indicating operating cash generation and/or working capital effects shifted across periods.

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance/actual pairs for cash flow were provided in the evidence, so forecasting credibility cannot be assessed. Any change in operating or investing cash flows therefore reflects actual filing-reported outcomes only, not validated forecasting performance versus stated forward guidance.

Trends:
  - Operating cash flow increased year over year in every period shown
  - Cash used by investing activities narrowed year over year in Q2 and Q3, then increased in Q1 2025 versus the prior-year quarter
  - Financing cash outflows widened in Fiscal 2024 versus Fiscal 2023

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Cash flow improvements may be sensitive to working capital dynamics (given 'before changes in operating working capital' is tracked alongside reported operating cash flow)
  - Financing cash outflows increased materially in Fiscal 2024, which could pressure free cash flow if sustained

Key concerns:
  - No cash-flow guidance was provided, limiting the ability to attribute changes to management actions versus underlying operating/investing/financing drivers.
  - The 'operating cash flow before changes in operating working capital' metric does not move uniformly upward across all periods (it is lower in Q2/Q3/Q4 vs prior-year comparisons but higher in Q1 2025), suggesting shifting working-capital and/or cash generation characteristics over time.
  - Investing cash outflows fluctuate (smaller in Q2/Q3 vs prior year, larger in Q1 2025 vs prior-year quarter), indicating potential variability in investment spending or timing of cash flows.

Conclusion:
Cash flow improved on the operating side across the entire period presented, with operating cash flow rising versus prior-year comparisons in Q2 2024 ($641M vs $424M), Q3 2024 ($1,031M vs $857M), Fiscal 2024 ($1,182M vs $1,107M), and Q1 2025 ($164M vs $119M). Investing activity produced ongoing net cash outflows, though the magnitude of cash used by investing activities decreased in the six- and nine-month periods (Q2: $353M vs $473M; Q3: $551M vs $761M) before increasing in Q1 2025 ($232M vs $165M). Financing cash flow was a consistent cash outflow, and the outflow expanded materially in Fiscal 2024 ($506M used) relative to Fiscal 2023 ($168M used). Overall, the evidence indicates strengthening operating cash generation, partially offset by continued investment outflows and an increased financing cash drain in Fiscal 2024.
