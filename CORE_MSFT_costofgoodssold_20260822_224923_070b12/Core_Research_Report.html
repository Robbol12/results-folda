Longitudinal Summary

Over the reported periods from Q1 2024 through Q4 2025, cost of goods sold (reported as cost of revenue) has been consistently disclosed in filings with detailed breakdowns between product and service components. The company applies a relative revenue methodology to allocate cost of revenue in certain cases and evaluates investments for impairment when cost exceeds fair value. Unearned revenue and remaining performance obligations are regularly disclosed, reflecting ongoing revenue deferral and recognition patterns. However, management does not provide explicit forward guidance on cost of goods sold, and the filings focus more on revenue recognition and related accounting policies than on forecasting cost of revenue.

Conclusion

Cost of goods sold, reported as cost of revenue, has shown a general upward trend consistent with business growth and revenue increases. The company employs a relative revenue methodology to allocate costs and carefully evaluates investments for impairment, reflecting prudent accounting practices. However, management does not provide forward guidance on cost of goods sold, and thus forecasting credibility cannot be assessed. The disclosures emphasize the interplay between revenue recognition and cost allocation, highlighting the importance of contract terms and unearned revenue in understanding cost dynamics. Overall, cost of goods sold appears to be managed with consistent accounting policies but remains a non-forecasted metric in management communications.
