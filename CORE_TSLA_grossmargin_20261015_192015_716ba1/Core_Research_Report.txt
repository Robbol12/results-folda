What the sources show:
Across 2024, gross margin trends diverged by segment: total automotive and related segments generally declined through mid-year, then improved in Q3 before weakening again by full-year results. In Q1 2024, total automotive gross margin fell from 21.1% to 18.5%, and total automotive & services and other fell from 19.9% to 16.7%, while energy generation and storage rose sharply from 11.0% to 24.6%. Through Q2 2024, automotive gross margin remained lower (e.g., 19.2% to 18.5% for three months ended June 30; and 20.1% to 18.5% for six months ended June 30). In Q3 2024, total automotive & services and other and total automotive increased for the quarter (17.4% to 18.7%; and 18.7% to 20.1%), with management attributing the increase to lower average combined cost per unit, higher FSD and regulatory credits revenue, partially offset by lower average selling price. By Q4/full year 2024, automotive gross margins ended lower year-over-year (total automotive & services and other: 18.2% to 16.9%; total automotive: 19.4% to 18.4%), while energy generation and storage increased (18.9% to 26.2%) on cost reductions (including IRA manufacturing credits) and product mix.

Watch points:
  - Automotive gross margin showed volatility: it decreased in Q1 and Q2, increased in Q3 (with specific drivers cited), but then decreased again for the full year, suggesting offsetting factors that may not be sustained.
  - In Q4/full-year commentary, the company cites lower average selling price and Cybertruck ramp as reasons for automotive margin decline, despite prior-quarter improvement attributed partly to regulatory credits and FSD revenue.
  - Automotive margin pressure tied to lower average selling price
  - Partial offsets from lower average combined cost per unit and higher FSD and regulatory credits revenue
  - Energy storage margin expansion driven by cost reductions, including IRA manufacturing credits, and higher-margin mix
