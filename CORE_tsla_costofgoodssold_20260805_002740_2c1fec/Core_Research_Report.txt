[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: cost of goods sold

Quarters searched: 12
Filing sections searched: 263

Over the reported periods from Q1 2021 through Q4 2023, cost of goods sold (COGS) was discussed primarily in the context of automotive leasing revenue components, including depreciation of operating lease vehicles, direct sales-type leases, and warranty expenses. Management occasionally commented on COGS per vehicle trends, noting reductions such as a $5,000 year-over-year decrease in mid-2021 and a 50% reduction when transitioning from earlier vehicle platforms to newer ones. Inflationary pressures, commodity costs, and logistics were cited as factors influencing COGS increases in 2022, while by Q3 2023, sequential decreases in material cost and freight were reported. Throughout, a $5 billion spending obligation related to combined capital, operational expenses, and COGS in New York was consistently disclosed in filings, with amendments extending timelines but no changes in the amount. No forward guidance on COGS was provided by management, and actuals were only discussed qualitatively or in per-vehicle terms rather than precise aggregate figures.

Forecasting credibility: None
There are zero guidance versus actual pairs for cost of goods sold, as management does not provide explicit forward guidance on this line item. All commentary is retrospective or qualitative, focusing on trends or cost structure components rather than specific forecasts. Therefore, it is not possible to assess forecasting credibility for COGS based on the available evidence.

Trends:
  - COGS per vehicle decreased year-over-year in 2021
  - Significant COGS reduction from platform transition (S/X to 3/Y)
  - Inflation and commodity cost pressures increased COGS in 2022
  - Sequential decreases in material and freight costs by 2023
  - Consistent $5 billion spending obligation related to COGS and other costs in New York

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Impact of leasing vehicle depreciation and warranty expenses on COGS
  - Raw material and commodity price exposure (10-15% of cost structure)
  - Inflation and logistics cost pressures
  - Capital and operational expense obligations tied to state agreements

Key concerns:
  - No explicit forward guidance on cost of goods sold limits ability to evaluate management's forecasting accuracy for this metric.
  - COGS figures are often embedded within broader categories (e.g., automotive leasing revenue) making precise trend analysis challenging.
  - The $5 billion combined spending obligation includes COGS but is aggregated with other costs, obscuring the specific COGS portion.

Conclusion:
Cost of goods sold has shown meaningful improvement on a per-vehicle basis, notably with a $5,000 reduction year-over-year in 2021 and a 50% reduction attributed to platform evolution from S/X to 3/Y models. However, inflation, commodity prices, and logistics costs have exerted upward pressure on COGS in 2022, partially offset by pricing benefits. The company’s COGS includes depreciation and warranty expenses related to leasing activities, which are consistently disclosed but not separately forecasted. The $5 billion spending commitment in New York encompasses COGS but is not broken out, limiting granular analysis. Overall, while management discusses COGS trends qualitatively, the absence of forward guidance and precise actuals precludes a formal assessment of forecasting credibility for cost of goods sold.
