[CORE RESEARCH] LMT: revenue

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 65

Over the 2024 reporting periods, management consistently projected modest to low single-digit revenue growth over the medium to long term, with some expectations of flat or slightly fluctuating revenue in the near term. Actual revenue recognized from contract liabilities showed steady increases year-over-year, but the full-year 2024 revenue of $71.0 billion missed the midpoint of the guided range ($70.5 billion to $71.5 billion). Management cited supply chain improvements and operational pace as drivers for future revenue growth, but also noted challenges such as delayed revenue recognition related to F-35 program funding lapses. The company applies percentage-of-completion accounting for revenue recognition, which remained consistent throughout the periods. Despite optimistic commentary, the single guidance/actual pair with a verdict was a miss, indicating some forecasting challenges.

Forecasting credibility: Low
There is only one guidance/actual pair with a verdict, which was a miss for the full-year 2024 revenue guidance. Management's other revenue guidance statements were unresolved or qualitative without specific numeric targets. The miss on the $70.5-$71.5 billion revenue guidance reduces confidence in forecasting accuracy. While management provided consistent narrative on expected growth, the lack of multiple verified guidance/actual pairs limits the ability to judge forecasting reliability positively.

Trends:
  - steady year-over-year revenue growth in contract liabilities recognized
  - flat to low single-digit revenue growth guidance
  - missed full-year revenue guidance midpoint
  - delayed revenue recognition due to program funding issues
  - consistent use of percentage-of-completion revenue recognition

Recurring themes/risks:
  - supply chain and operational pace impacting revenue growth
  - program funding delays affecting revenue timing
  - revenue recognition dependent on contract progress and delivery milestones

Key concerns:
  - The only guidance/actual pair with a verdict was a miss, raising concerns about management's forecasting accuracy.
  - Revenue recognition is heavily dependent on percentage-of-completion accounting, which can introduce timing variability and complexity.
  - Delayed revenue recognition related to F-35 program funding lapses suggests potential risks in revenue realization timing.

Conclusion:
Revenue guidance over 2024 indicated expectations of modest growth or flat performance, but actual results showed a slight shortfall against the guided range for full-year 2024 revenue of $70.5 to $71.5 billion, with reported net sales of $71.0 billion. The company consistently recognized revenue based on the percentage-of-completion method, reflecting progress on long-term contracts. While contract liabilities recognized as revenue increased year-over-year, delays in program funding and one fewer week in Q4 negatively impacted quarterly sales. Overall, revenue performance was solid but fell short of management's stated guidance, indicating some challenges in forecasting and execution.

[CORE RESEARCH] LMT: operating margin

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 65

Over the course of 2024, operating margins showed a generally positive trend with management consistently highlighting improvements driven by higher volume, favorable contract mix, and profit booking rate adjustments. Q1 2024 saw operating profit growth despite a $100 million reach-forward loss on a classified missile program. By Q2 and Q3, margins expanded modestly, with Q2 margins reaching 11.3% and Q3 at 10.9%, both exceeding management's guidance of approximately 10.5%. In Q4, adjusted segment operating margins were reported at 11.1%, excluding classified program charges, with management setting an 11% margin target for 2025. Favorable contract mix and volume gains were partially offset by lower profit booking rate adjustments in the latter half of the year.

Forecasting credibility: High
Management provided two explicit guidance/actual pairs for FY2024 operating margins, both of which were exceeded. The Q2 and Q3 guidance of approximately 10.5% margins was surpassed with actual margins of 11.3% and 10.9%, respectively. This consistent pattern of meeting or exceeding margin guidance supports a high rating for forecasting credibility. The availability of filing-sourced actuals further strengthens the reliability of these assessments.

Trends:
  - Increasing operating margins over 2024
  - Higher volume and favorable contract mix driving margin expansion
  - Profit booking rate adjustments fluctuating
  - Exclusion of classified program charges improves margin comparability
  - Management raising margin guidance mid-year

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Impact of classified missile program losses
  - Profit booking rate adjustments affecting comparability
  - Contract mix and volume variability
  - Government contract accounting standards influencing reported margins

Key concerns:
  - The $100 million reach-forward loss on a classified missile program at MFC introduces a non-recurring charge that complicates margin comparability.
  - Profit booking rate adjustments vary significantly quarter to quarter, impacting operating profit and margin trends and requiring careful interpretation.
  - Management excludes certain pension and corporate cost adjustments from segment operating profit, which may affect comparability across periods.

Conclusion:
Operating margins improved steadily throughout 2024, supported by higher production volumes and favorable contract mix, with management successfully exceeding their margin guidance targets for the year. Adjusted margins excluding classified program charges were solid at 11.1% in Q4, aligning with management's 11% target for 2025. Despite some volatility from profit booking rate adjustments and non-recurring charges, the overall trajectory indicates effective operational execution and margin expansion. The consistent pattern of exceeding margin guidance and the detailed filing disclosures lend confidence to management's margin outlook and reporting integrity.

  - As previously discussed, operating margins returned to 11% and free cash flow grows 9% at the midpoint from 2024 and adjusted results, setting up double-digit growth in free cash flow per share in spite of non-CAS/FAS pension headwind lowering EPS.
  - Bringing it all together, we expect solid sales growth in 2025 off the higher 2024 base, operating margins at the 11% target, and solid cash flow generation that enables consistent shareholder returns.
  - Excluding the classified program charges, MFC's segment operating margin for the full year was a solid 14.4%.
  - On an adjusted basis, segment operating profit would have grown 5% year over year to $2.1 billion, resulting in segment margins of 11.1%.
  - On an adjusted basis, segment operating profit grew 7% year over year, and adjusted segment margins were 11.1%.
  - Operating profit was up 3% for the year due to the higher sales volume and favorable contract mix, partially offset by lower profit booking rate adjustments.
  - On an adjusted basis, aeronautics full-year operating profit grew by 3%, equating to 10.2% margin for the year.
  - Similar to sales, operating profit was down 11% year over year due to lower profit booking rate adjustments and sales volume, partially offset by favorable contract mix.
  - Similar to the fourth quarter, segment operating profit, segment margins, and earnings per share were impacted by the net program charges on Slide 4.
  - Meanwhile, operating profit increased 6% in 2024 due to favorable contract mix and higher ULA equity earnings, partially offset by lower profit booking rate adjustments.
  - Operating profit decreased 8% compared to Q4 2023, driven by lower volume and lower profit booking rate adjustments, partially offset by higher equity earnings from the United Launch Alliance.
  - Full-year segment operating profit decreased 11%, driven by the same items we saw in the fourth quarter.
  - Full-year segment operating profit declined $1.1 billion year over year due to $1.4 billion of classified program charges, which were partially offset by higher volume from the production ramps.
  - Segment operating profit decreased 43% compared to Q4 2023.
Guidance given:
  - Bringing it all together, we expect solid sales growth in 2025 off the higher 2024 base, operating margins at the 11% target, and solid cash flow generation that enables consistent shareholder returns. (target: FY 2025; Unresolved)
  - Chart 12 bridges the 2024 reported segment operating profit of $6.1 billion to the 2025 guidance midpoint of $8.15 billion. (target: FY2025; Unresolved)
