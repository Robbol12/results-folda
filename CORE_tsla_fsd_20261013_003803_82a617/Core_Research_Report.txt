What the sources show:
Across 1Q23 to 4Q24, Tesla’s FSD narrative shifts from emphasizing FSD Beta mileage and iterative improvements to positioning FSD as scaling software (Version 12/12.5/13/“V13” and training clusters) that supports broader customer deployment and future unsupervised/robotaxi ambitions. In 1Q23 and 2Q23, management highlights large cumulative FSD Beta miles (150M+ and 300M+) and “improvements” with a “two steps forward, one step back between releases,” while answering pricing/take-rate questions largely through “option value” framing. By 3Q23 and 4Q23, Tesla discusses Version 12 as a “complete architectural rewrite” and rolling out to all U.S. customers who request FSD in weeks, while also tying operating performance discussions to changes in FSD revenue recognition and warranty/deferral items. In 1Q24 and 2Q24, management emphasizes FSD price cuts and free trials for supervised FSD, expects regulatory pathways for supervised deployments across geographies, and links capacity/training clusters to accelerating scaling toward unsupervised. In 3Q24 and 4Q24, the company highlights increasing take rate and accelerated training infrastructure (“Cortex”) for V13, while management becomes more confident about releasing unsupervised FSD in California “this year,” stressing very high safety thresholds amid continued uncertainty about hardware upgrade needs.

Watch points:
  - Accounting/metric timing: Tesla repeatedly links changes in results to (i) FSD-related deferred revenue and revenue recognition timing and (ii) FSD Beta wide release in prior-year periods, indicating that period-over-period comparability may be affected by recognition mechanics rather than solely by operational progress.
  - Safety/regulatory uncertainty persists: management expresses confidence in releasing unsupervised FSD “this year” (California), but also emphasizes that approval depends on pathways/approvals “not something we totally control” and that the required safety level must be “significantly above the average human driver.”
  - Hardware migration uncertainty: management in 4Q24 states it “hasn’t started working on Hardware 3 yet” but suggests a need to upgrade Hardware 3 computers for later releases, implying future cost/capex risk without a quantified impact in the provided excerpts.
  - Regulatory approval and safety thresholds for moving from supervised to unsupervised remain central and uncertain
  - Release cadence characterized as improving but with stepwise setbacks (“two steps forward, one step back”)
  - Dependence on training compute/infrastructure clusters (Dojo mentioned; later Cortex/training cluster emphasis; GPU/NPU supply referenced)
  - Economic balancing of pricing, demand, and accounting (deferred revenue, revenue recognition timing, and ASP/mix effects)
  - Hardware constraints and potential need to upgrade Hardware 3 vs relying on Hardware 4 remain a recurring risk
