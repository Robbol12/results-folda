In Q3 2025, management emphasized demand and vehicle pull-through being tied to the rollout and scale-up of supervised FSD, while also noting the paid FSD customer base remained small (about 12%). In Q4 2025, the framing shifted toward near-term demand and delivery outcomes by geography, citing a surge in US demand ahead of the consumer credit cliff and record deliveries in several smaller countries, ending 2025 with a bigger backlog. The later quarter presentation places less emphasis on the small paid-FSD base and more on observed delivery/demand momentum and backlog growth, with a clarification that those countries did not yet have the latest FSD version.

Key quotes:
  - “On the autos front, while in Q3, we saw a surge in US demand before the higher consumer credit cliff, pulling in some demand from Q4. In other parts of the world, we saw an increase in demand leading to record deliveries in smaller countries like Malaysia, Norway, Poland, Saudi Arabia, and Taiwan… We therefore ended 2025 with a bigger backlog than in recent years.”
