[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 97

Over the 2024 reporting periods, gross margin trends showed a general decline in the total automotive segment, with margins decreasing from 21.1% in Q1 2023 to 18.4% by year-end 2024. The energy generation and storage segment experienced a notable increase in gross margin, rising from 11.0% in Q1 2023 to 30.5% by Q3 2024. The total automotive & services and other segment margins fluctuated, with decreases in the first half of the year, a slight increase in Q3, and a decline again by year-end. Management attributed changes primarily to vehicle pricing, production ramp factors, and regulatory credits. Sequential declines in service and other business margins were noted despite year-over-year improvements.

Forecasting credibility: None
No forward guidance on gross margin was provided by management in any period, and thus no guidance versus actual pairs exist to assess forecasting credibility. Without explicit forecasts, it is not possible to judge management's accuracy or reliability in predicting gross margin outcomes. The assessment is therefore limited to observed reported results without credibility rating.

Trends:
  - Declining automotive gross margin over 2024
  - Increasing energy generation and storage gross margin
  - Fluctuating automotive & services segment margins
  - Year-over-year improvement but sequential decline in service margins

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Lower average selling prices impacting automotive margins
  - Production ramp effects (e.g., Cybertruck)
  - Regulatory credits and FSD revenue influencing margins
  - Volatility in service and other business margins

Key concerns:
  - No forward guidance on gross margin limits ability to assess management forecasting skill.
  - Gross margin declines in automotive segment despite some cost improvements suggest pricing pressure risks.
  - Sequential margin declines in service and other business may indicate operational or market challenges.

Conclusion:
Gross margin in the automotive segment showed a downward trend throughout 2024, declining from 21.1% in Q1 2023 to 18.4% by year-end 2024, driven by lower average selling prices and production ramp factors such as the Cybertruck. Conversely, the energy generation and storage segment experienced strong margin expansion, reaching 30.5% by Q3 2024. The total automotive & services and other segment margins were more volatile, with some improvement mid-year but ending lower year-over-year. Management highlighted regulatory credits and FSD revenue as partial offsets to margin pressures. Overall, gross margin trends reflect mixed performance across segments with notable challenges in automotive pricing and service margins, but no forward guidance was provided to evaluate forecasting accuracy.
