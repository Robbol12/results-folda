What the sources show:
Across 2023, AMD’s investing cash flows were consistently dominated by transactions in short-term investments (purchases and maturities/sales) plus purchases of property and equipment, with net cash used remaining relatively steady through the year. In 2022 (comparative in the FY 2023 materials), net cash provided by investing activities was $2.0B, largely reflecting a higher contribution from maturities of short-term investments and acquisition-related cash acquired from Xilinx, partially offset by Pensando-related outflows and short-term investment purchases. For FY 2023 specifically, AMD reported net cash used in investing activities of $1.4B, driven by purchases of short-term investments and property and equipment, partially offset by proceeds from maturities and sales, and including acquisitions net of cash acquired ($131M). In 2024 through the interim filings, investing activity showed both net cash used and periods of net cash provided (e.g., YTD six months net cash provided of $251M; nine months net cash provided of $113M), while management also explicitly highlighted ongoing AI-related investment expectations (“we have to invest”) even though the evidence provided here does not quantify the incremental dollar amounts by period. By FY 2024, net cash used in investing activities was $1.1B, with a similar pattern of short-term investment purchases partially offset by maturities/sales and higher acquisition activity net of cash acquired ($548M).

Key finding (Q4 2023): Net cash provided by investing activities was $2 billion in 2022, which primarily consisted of higher cash provided by maturities of short-term investments of $4.3 billion and cash acquired as part of the acquisition of Xilinx of $2.4 billion, partially offset by higher cash used for purchases of short-term investments of $2.7 billion, cash used in the acquisition of Pensando Systems Inc. (“Pensando”) of $1.5 billion and $450 million for purchases of property and equipment.

Watch points:
  - The evidence does not provide a quantified linkage between management’s qualitative statements about “increasing hardware, software, and solutions investments” and specific incremental investing-cash-flow line items by quarter; therefore, any magnitude attribution to AI investments cannot be established from the excerpts.
  - Balance-sheet cash (cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments) fluctuates (e.g., $6.3B in Q2 2023 to $4.5B by Q3 2024, and $5.1B by Q4 2024), but the excerpts do not fully reconcile these changes to investing cash flows versus operating cash flow or other items, limiting inference about causality.
  - Earlier-period comparative magnitudes include major acquisitions (Xilinx, Pensando) in FY 2022, but the excerpted investing discussion for 2023–2024 focuses on net acquisitions without naming additional deals; this limits visibility into deal-specific cash timing and risk.
  - Cash flow sensitivity to timing of short-term investment purchases versus maturities/sales
  - Capital intensity and timing of property and equipment purchases (reported as quarterly/yearly cash outflows)
  - Potential earnings/capital risk from AI infrastructure investment cycle referenced by management (qualitative, not quantified in the excerpts)
  - M&A cash outflow risk shown by acquisitions net of cash acquired (Pensando, Xilinx comparison in 2022; net acquisitions in FY 2023 and FY 2024)
