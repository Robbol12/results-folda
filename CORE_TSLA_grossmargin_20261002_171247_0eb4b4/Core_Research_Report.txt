[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 186

Across 2023, management’s gross-margin narrative shifted from referencing “mid-20%” gross margins for new programs to emphasizing that automotive gross margin was pressured by a mix of pricing actions and one-time items (e.g., warranty adjustments and Autopilot-related deferred revenue). In the filings, reported automotive gross margin for “total automotive” fell materially through 2023 (e.g., 32.9% to 21.1% in Q1 2023; 29.6% to 19.7% by Q3 2023; and 28.5% to 19.4% for full-year 2023), with energy generation/storage moving in the opposite direction at times. In 2024, automotive gross margin largely stayed lower than 2023 levels overall, with continued year-over-year declines in early 2024 (e.g., Q1 2024: 21.1% to 18.5% for total automotive) and a modest improvement in one quarter (Q3 2024: total automotive increased from 17.6% to 18.7% for “total automotive & services and other segment”). By full-year 2024, filings show automotive gross margin remained down (19.4% to 18.4% total automotive) while energy gross margin rose (18.9% to 26.2%).

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance/actual pairs were found specifically for gross margin (i.e., there is no countable set of management forecasts paired to later realized gross-margin outcomes). As a result, forecasting credibility cannot be assessed based on a guidance-vs-actual met/missed pattern. The evidence provided supports describing what management discussed and what filings reported, but not evaluating forecasting accuracy.

Trends:
  - Automotive gross margin declined sharply through 2023 on a year-over-year basis
  - Energy generation and storage gross margin improved over time relative to automotive
  - Automotive gross margin remained pressured in most of 2024, with limited quarter-to-quarter improvement (Q3 2024)
  - By full-year 2024, energy outperformed with higher gross margin while automotive stayed lower than full-year 2023

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Pricing actions/affordability measures impacting automotive gross margin
  - One-time items (e.g., warranty adjustments, deferred revenue for Autopilot features) affecting comparability
  - Costs and lower average selling price weighing on automotive margins
  - External factors (e.g., tariffs/incentives) making automotive gross margin difficult to predict

Key concerns:
  - Gross margin guidance appears qualitative and/or not directly forecastable each period (e.g., “mid-20%” reference for launched programs; warnings that predicting auto gross margins is challenging), limiting the ability to verify precision against actuals.
  - Automotive gross margin includes multiple drivers with potentially non-recurring components (warranty adjustments, deferred revenue transitions), which can cause quarter-to-quarter volatility and complicate trend interpretation.
  - Segment mix and definitions differ by disclosure (e.g., ‘total automotive’ vs ‘total automotive & services and other segment’; energy segment is separate), so aggregated gross margin comparisons can mask offsetting movements.

Conclusion:
Gross margin dynamics were dominated by a sharp reduction in automotive gross margin during 2023 (total automotive: 32.9% to 21.1% in Q1 2023; 29.6% to 19.7% by Q3 2023; 28.5% to 19.4% for full-year 2023), with filings attributing the decline to the automotive gross margin decrease discussed in management commentary and often noting partial offsets from services. In 2024, automotive gross margin generally stayed below 2023 levels (Q1 2024 total automotive: 21.1% to 18.5%; Q2 2024 total automotive decreased to 18.5% from 19.2%); a modest improvement appeared in Q3 2024 for “total automotive & services and other segment” (17.4% to 18.7%) and then mixed results continued (year-end 2024 total automotive: 19.4% to 18.4%). Energy generation and storage gross margin improved substantially by full-year 2024 (18.9% to 26.2%), increasingly offsetting automotive weakness in overall performance. Overall, based on the filing-sourced figures provided, gross margin trends were persistently challenged in automotive while energy strength drove relative improvement.
