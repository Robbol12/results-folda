[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: profit

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 174

Over the periods from Q1 2021 through Q4 2022, profit showed a general upward trajectory, with gross profit in automotive increasing significantly from $1,311 million in Q1 2021 to $5,539 million in Q1 2022, before moderating somewhat to $4,081 million in Q2 2022. Management highlighted record profitability and operating margins in several quarters, emphasizing the impact of higher volumes and product mix shifts, such as the higher profit Model Y vehicle. The company also noted accumulated profitability turning positive by the end of 2021, marking a milestone. Profit-sharing arrangements and their accounting treatment via the HLBV method were consistently disclosed in filings, indicating a stable approach to recognizing noncontrolling interests related to profit. Energy business profitability also improved, contributing to overall profit growth.

Forecasting credibility: None
No forward guidance on profit was provided by management in any period, and thus no guidance versus actual pairs exist to assess forecasting credibility. Without explicit profit forecasts, it is not possible to judge management's ability to predict profit outcomes. The assessment is therefore limited to reported results and commentary without a credibility rating.

Trends:
  - Increasing automotive gross profit through 2021 and early 2022
  - Record profitability and operating margins in multiple quarters
  - Positive accumulated profitability by end of 2021
  - Energy business profit growth alongside automotive
  - Consistent use of HLBV method for profit-sharing accounting

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Impact of product mix on profit (Model Y vs Model 3)
  - Profit-sharing arrangements affecting net income
  - Volume-driven profitability improvements
  - Accounting treatment of noncontrolling interests

Key concerns:
  - No forward profit guidance limits ability to assess forecasting accuracy or management credibility on profit
  - Repeated disclosures on profit-sharing arrangements and HLBV method suggest complexity in profit attribution that may affect comparability
  - No noted accounting changes but consistent methodology applied for profit-sharing

Conclusion:
Profit demonstrated strong growth over the periods reviewed, driven by higher volumes, improved product mix, and operational efficiencies. The company achieved positive accumulated profitability by the end of 2021 and continued to report record margins and gross profits into 2022. However, the absence of explicit profit guidance means management's forecasting credibility on profit cannot be evaluated. The consistent accounting treatment of profit-sharing arrangements provides transparency but also indicates complexity in profit recognition. Overall, the evidence supports a narrative of improving profitability, but without forward guidance, investors must rely on reported results rather than management forecasts.
