What the sources show:
Across 1Q and 2Q 2024, gross margin weakened in the automotive-related results: total automotive gross margin fell from 21.1% to 18.5% in Q1 and remained below prior-year levels in Q2 (e.g., 19.2% to 18.5% for the three months ended June 30, 2024). Despite continued automotive pressure, gross margin improved in the energy generation and storage segment over the same periods, rising from 11.0% to 24.6% in Q1 and reaching 30.5% in Q3 per the presentation. In 3Q, automotive & services and other showed some stabilization/rebound on a quarter-over-quarter basis (e.g., 17.4% to 18.7% for the three months ended September 30, 2024), with the filing attributing the three-month increase to lower average cost per unit, higher FSD and regulatory credit revenue, partially offset by lower average selling price. By full-year 2024, automotive gross margin declined versus 2023 (19.4% to 18.4%), while energy generation and storage rose materially (18.9% to 26.2%), with energy driven by cost reductions, IRA manufacturing credits, and product mix.

Watch points:
  - Automotive gross margin declined consistently versus prior-year periods in both quarterly and year-to-date comparisons (e.g., Q1, Q2, and year-ended amounts), indicating sustained structural pressure rather than a one-quarter issue.
  - Reported gross margin movement depends on mix and offsets: automotive margin increases are described as partially offset by lower average selling price, implying that positive contributors (FSD and regulatory credits) may not fully counteract pricing/ramp headwinds.
  - Lower average selling price and/or vehicle ramp effects cited as drivers of automotive gross margin compression
  - Regulatory credits and FSD (Supervised) revenue repeatedly cited as supporting automotive margin
  - Energy segment margin improvements tied to cost reductions and manufacturing credits (IRA manufacturing credits cited for full year)
