Over the periods from 2013 through 2015, the company consistently reported using cash to fund its operations, with net cash used increasing notably in 2015 compared to prior years. Despite reporting net income in most periods, the company’s cash generated from operations before working capital changes was positive but insufficient to cover operational cash outflows, indicating working capital or other operational factors consumed cash. Management discussed intentions to generate free cash flow to strengthen the balance sheet, particularly in late 2015, but no explicit forward guidance on free cash flow was provided. The filings consistently highlight net income and changes in working capital as primary drivers of cash flows from operations, with no accounting changes or inconsistencies noted in the reporting of cash flow figures.

Conclusion:
The company has historically used cash to fund its operations even while reporting net income, indicating that operational cash demands, likely from working capital changes, have been significant. Cash generated from operations before working capital changes has been positive but insufficient to offset cash used in operations overall. Management has expressed intentions to improve free cash flow to bolster the balance sheet, but no formal guidance or forecasts have been provided. Without forward guidance and actual comparisons, forecasting credibility cannot be assessed. Overall, free cash flow remains a challenge for the company, driven primarily by working capital dynamics rather than profitability alone.

Over the 2015 reporting periods, the company consistently enhanced its liquidity position primarily through amendments to its revolving credit facility and the issuance of $55 million in 5.50% Senior Notes. The credit facility was increased from $100 million to $125 million early in the year, providing additional borrowing capacity. The Senior Notes issuance in April 2015 was explicitly aimed at facilitating acquisition activity and bolstering liquidity. Throughout the year, management emphasized that primary liquidity sources remained cash flows from operations and borrowing capacity under the credit facility. No forward guidance on liquidity was provided in earnings calls or filings, and liquidity disclosures focused on historical and current financing activities rather than forecasts.

Forecasting credibility: None
There are zero guidance versus actual pairs for liquidity, as management did not provide forward-looking liquidity guidance in any period. Therefore, it is not possible to assess forecasting credibility for this topic. The disclosures are limited to factual statements about credit facility amendments, debt issuance, and sources of liquidity without predictive commentary or targets.

Trends:
  - Increasing revolving credit facility
  - Issuance of senior notes
  - Reliance on cash flows from operations
  - Focus on acquisition-related liquidity

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Dependence on credit facility borrowing capacity
  - Use of debt to support acquisitions
  - Liquidity tied to customer receivables collections

Key concerns:
  - No forward-looking liquidity guidance limits ability to assess management's forecasting skill on this topic.
  - No accounting or reporting inconsistencies noted in liquidity disclosures across periods.

Conclusion:
The company's liquidity position in 2015 was strengthened through strategic increases in revolving credit capacity and the issuance of senior notes, which provided additional funds for acquisitions and operational needs. Management consistently identified cash flows from operations and borrowing capacity as primary liquidity sources. However, the absence of forward-looking liquidity guidance means investors must rely on historical financing actions and operational cash flow trends to assess liquidity risk. Overall, the company appears to maintain adequate liquidity through diversified sources, but transparency on future liquidity expectations is limited.
