[CORE RESEARCH] VST: capacity

Quarters searched: 2
Filing sections searched: 44

Over the 2021 reporting periods, the company disclosed several capacity-related impairments, notably on coal generation facilities such as Joppa/EEI and Zimmer, reflecting decreased economic forecasts and useful lives. The company also sold a portion of its PJM capacity clearing for Planning Years 2021-2022, generating significant cash inflows. Available liquidity fluctuated due to capital expenditures, debt issuances, and repayments, with capacity under the revolving credit facility consistently noted as part of liquidity. By year-end, the company highlighted that certain units represented about 6% of total generation capacity and emphasized ongoing efforts to secure longer-term multi-year capacity transactions through 2025-2026. Nuclear asset capacity was also specifically noted with installed nameplate capacities disclosed for Comanche Peak units.

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance versus actual pairs related to capacity were provided in the evidence, preventing any assessment of management's forecasting credibility on this topic. The disclosures focus on actual impairments, liquidity, and capacity sales without forward-looking capacity guidance or forecasts. Therefore, no credibility rating can be assigned based on the available data.

Trends:
  - Impairments on coal generation capacity
  - Sale of PJM capacity rights
  - Stable liquidity including capacity under credit facilities
  - Focus on securing longer-term capacity contracts
  - Disclosure of nuclear generation capacity

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Decreased useful life and economic viability of coal assets
  - Capital expenditures impacting liquidity
  - Reliance on capacity sales for cash inflows
  - Nuclear asset operational capacity

Key concerns:
  - Significant impairments on coal generation facilities indicate risks to capacity value and economic assumptions.
  - No forward guidance on capacity limits ability to assess management's forecasting reliability in this area.
  - Liquidity changes tied to capacity sales and capital expenditures require monitoring for operational flexibility.

Conclusion:
The company's capacity profile in 2021 was marked by impairments to coal generation assets, reflecting diminished economic forecasts and reduced useful lives, which may constrain future capacity contributions from these units. The sale of PJM capacity rights provided substantial liquidity, indicating a strategic monetization of capacity assets. The company maintains a focus on securing longer-term capacity contracts, suggesting an intent to stabilize future capacity revenues. Nuclear generation capacity remains a significant and clearly disclosed component of total capacity. Overall, while capacity impairments pose challenges, ongoing capacity sales and contract pursuits indicate active management of capacity resources.

  - uncertainty from COVID-19 pandemic
  - storm-related customer billing and curtailment impacts

  - capital investment decisions tied to expected returns
