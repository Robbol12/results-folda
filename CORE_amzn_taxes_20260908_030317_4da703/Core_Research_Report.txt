[CORE RESEARCH] AMZN: taxes

Quarters searched: 5
Filing sections searched: 138

Over the periods from Q1 2024 through Q4 2025, the company consistently reported its income tax provisions and cash paid for income taxes in its filings, with detailed disclosures on net discrete tax benefits primarily related to stock-based compensation. Income tax provisions increased notably in 2025 compared to 2024, with a significant jump in the annual provision from $9.3 billion in 2024 to $19.1 billion in 2025. Cash paid for income taxes showed variability, with a general trend of increasing amounts through 2024 but a marked decrease in 2025, attributed to the impact of the 2025 Tax Act. The company disclosed income tax contingencies that slightly increased over time, and repeatedly referenced Note 7 in their filings for additional tax information. Management highlighted the 2025 Tax Act as a key factor expected to reduce U.S. cash taxes significantly in 2025.

Forecasting credibility: None
There are zero guidance versus actual pairs for taxes, as management does not provide forward guidance on this topic. Therefore, it is not possible to assess forecasting credibility based on the available data. The assessment is strictly based on reported actuals and filing disclosures without any forward-looking tax guidance to compare against.

Trends:
  - Increasing income tax provision from 2024 to 2025
  - Rising net discrete tax benefits related to stock-based compensation
  - Fluctuating cash paid for income taxes with a decrease in 2025
  - Slight increase in income tax contingencies
  - Impact of 2025 Tax Act reducing cash taxes

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Variability in effective tax rate due to multiple factors
  - Significant net discrete tax benefits from stock-based compensation
  - Income tax contingencies around $5 billion
  - Changes in tax laws affecting cash tax payments
  - Complexity from intercompany transactions and jurisdictional mix

Key concerns:
  - No forward guidance on taxes limits ability to evaluate management's forecasting accuracy.
  - Large fluctuations in income tax provisions and cash taxes paid may reflect tax law changes and discrete items, complicating trend analysis.
  - The significant increase in the 2025 income tax provision compared to 2024 warrants scrutiny, especially given the simultaneous decrease in cash taxes paid.

Conclusion:
The company's tax expense and cash tax payments have shown notable variability over the periods analyzed, influenced heavily by discrete tax benefits from stock-based compensation and legislative changes such as the 2025 Tax Act. While income tax provisions increased substantially in 2025, cash taxes paid decreased, consistent with management's disclosures about the Tax Act's impact. The company maintains significant income tax contingencies, reflecting ongoing tax uncertainties. Due to the absence of forward guidance on taxes, it is not possible to assess management's forecasting credibility in this area. Overall, the tax line items appear to be materially affected by external tax law changes and discrete accounting items rather than predictable operational factors.
