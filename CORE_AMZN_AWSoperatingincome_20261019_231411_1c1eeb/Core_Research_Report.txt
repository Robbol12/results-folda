[CORE RESEARCH] AMZN: AWS operating income

Quarters searched: 2
Filing sections searched: 69

What the sources show:
Across Q1–Q3 2025, AWS operating income is described as increasing year over year, with the primary drivers shifting from “increased sales and decreased payroll and related expenses” in Q1 and Q2 to “increased sales” with fewer mention of payroll offset by Q3. The company repeatedly attributes the year-over-year increases to higher sales partially offset by technology infrastructure spending required to support AWS business growth. In Q3 2025, the narrative adds a partially offset component from “severance costs,” indicating higher workforce-related charges compared with earlier quarters. For Q4 2025, management quantifies AWS operating margin as 35% through Q4 and characterizes Trainium/Graviton as already contributing to operating margin and P&L, linking earlier capex/investments to operating performance. For the full year 2025, the filing discloses that operating income includes substantial charges, including FTC settlement charges and estimated severance costs tied to planned role eliminations, which frames the reported year-level performance as occurring alongside large one-time or specific-cost items.

Watch points:
  - AWS operating income increases are consistently attributed to operational improvements, but the disclosed 2025 operating income includes large charge components (FTC settlement and estimated severance), which may complicate interpretation of underlying trends versus one-time items.
  - The quarterly explanations change: Q1 and Q2 highlight decreased payroll and related expenses, while Q2’s incremental narrative omits payroll and Q3 adds severance costs, suggesting shifts in cost drivers that could make results more volatile across quarters.
  - Forward operating income expectations are provided for multiple periods, but the excerpts do not reconcile these forecasts to the disclosed one-time charges (FTC and severance), raising a risk of differing assumptions behind guidance versus reported figures.
  - Ongoing capex/technology infrastructure spending to support AWS growth partially offsets operating income gains
  - Workforce-related costs (severance) can partially offset operating income (explicit in Q3 and disclosed for 2025)
  - Operating performance depends on translating capacity investments into revenue and margin (management discussion linking CapEx to AWS operating margin)
  - Large external/legal and restructuring-type charges affect reported operating income (FTC settlement and severance)
