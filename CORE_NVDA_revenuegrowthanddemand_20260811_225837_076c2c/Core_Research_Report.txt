[CORE RESEARCH] NVDA: revenue growth

Quarters searched: 3
Filing sections searched: 84

Over the reported periods, revenue growth showed strong year-on-year increases initially, with Q1 2022 revenue at $5.66 billion growing 84% year-over-year and Q2 2022 at $6.5 billion with 68% growth. However, sequential growth guidance was mixed, with Q2 2022 guidance expecting over $500 million sequential growth excluding CMP, but actual Q3 2022 revenue missed expectations, declining 19% sequentially to $6.7 billion. By Q4 2022, revenue further declined sequentially to $5.93 billion, missing the $8.1 billion guidance, despite some segments like Data Center showing strong year-over-year growth. Management anticipated a return to sequential growth in Q1 2023, but the recent quarters showed a pattern of missing sequential growth targets. Expenses increased consistently due to employee growth and compensation costs, reflecting investment in future growth areas.

Forecasting credibility: Low
The company provided two explicit guidance/actual pairs with verdicts: Q2 2022 guidance was met or exceeded, but Q3 2022 guidance was missed significantly. The pattern of missing sequential revenue growth targets in later quarters undermines management's forecasting credibility. The discrepancy between optimistic guidance and actual results, especially the large miss in Q3 2022, indicates low reliability in revenue growth forecasting.

Trends:
  - Strong year-over-year revenue growth early in period
  - Sequential revenue growth guidance missed in later quarters
  - Declining sequential revenue in Q3 and Q4 2022
  - Consistent increase in operating expenses
  - Data Center segment showing strong year-over-year growth

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Volatility in sequential revenue growth
  - High compensation and employee growth costs
  - Dependence on cloud and AI workloads for growth
  - Inventory purchase obligations impacting revenue recognition

Key concerns:
  - Significant sequential revenue declines in Q3 and Q4 2022 despite optimistic guidance
  - Increased expenses driven by employee growth and compensation may pressure margins
  - Potential overstatement of near-term revenue growth prospects given repeated misses

Conclusion:
Revenue growth showed strong year-over-year increases initially, driven by key segments like Data Center and cloud computing workloads. However, sequential revenue growth guidance was not consistently met, with notable misses in Q3 and Q4 2022, indicating challenges in sustaining momentum. Management's optimistic forward guidance contrasted with actual declines, reducing confidence in near-term revenue growth forecasts. While long-term growth drivers remain, the recent pattern suggests caution in expecting continuous sequential revenue increases in the near term.

[CORE RESEARCH] NVDA: demand

Quarters searched: 3
Filing sections searched: 84

Over the reported periods, management consistently described strong demand initially, particularly around the launch of the Ampere GPU architecture and the 30 series, which set records for desktop and laptop GPU sales. However, subsequent filings revealed significant inventory reserve charges totaling over $1.9 billion across fiscal 2023 quarters, reflecting downward revisions in demand expectations, especially in Data Center and Gaming segments. The company cited macroeconomic challenges, COVID-19 impacts, and supply chain constraints as factors influencing demand volatility. Gaming revenue notably declined by 51% year-over-year in Q4 2022, indicating a material softening in consumer demand. Management acknowledged the risk of demand estimation inaccuracies leading to supply-demand mismatches.

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance versus actual pairs on demand were provided in the evidence, preventing any assessment of forecasting credibility. Without explicit forward guidance figures and corresponding actual results, it is not possible to judge whether management's demand forecasts were met, exceeded, or missed. Therefore, the credibility rating must remain null.

Trends:
  - Strong initial demand with Ampere launch
  - Increasing inventory reserves indicating demand softness
  - Declining gaming revenue reflecting weaker consumer demand
  - Macroeconomic and COVID-19 impacts dampening demand
  - Supply chain constraints affecting fulfillment

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Demand volatility due to macroeconomic conditions
  - COVID-19 related supply and demand disruptions
  - Inventory reserve charges as a response to demand revisions
  - Risk of supply-demand mismatch from inaccurate demand estimates

Key concerns:
  - Large inventory reserve charges totaling nearly $2 billion suggest significant overestimation of demand in prior periods.
  - Gaming revenue decline of 51% year-over-year signals a sharp drop in end-customer demand.
  - Repeated cautionary language about demand uncertainty and supply chain limitations highlights ongoing risk to demand stability.

Conclusion:
Demand for the company's products initially appeared robust, driven by successful product launches and strong OEM engagement. However, over the course of fiscal 2023, demand softened materially, particularly in Gaming and Data Center segments, as evidenced by substantial inventory write-downs and declining revenues. Macroeconomic headwinds and COVID-19 disruptions contributed to this weakening demand environment. While management highlighted strong demand early on, the large inventory provisions and revenue declines indicate that actual demand fell short of prior expectations. Overall, demand remains a key risk area with significant volatility and uncertainty.
