[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 186

Across 2023, management referenced mid-20% automotive gross margin as a target for launched programs, but reported actual automotive gross margin stepped down sharply: total automotive fell from 32.9% (Q1 2023) to 21.1% (Q1 2023) and continued to trend down through 2023 (e.g., 28.5% to 19.4% for full-year 2023). In filings, the decline was repeatedly attributed to automotive gross margin decreases driven by pricing/actions and one-time items (Q1 2023), with services and other often partially offsetting the automotive line item decline. In early 2024, automotive gross margin remained below the earlier “mid-20%” framing and drifted further down in several periods (e.g., 21.1% to 18.5% in Q1 2024). By late 2024, the pattern became more mixed, with short-term sequential improvements (e.g., automotive & services rising to 18.7% in Q3 2024) but full-year 2024 still below 2023 (total automotive 19.4% to 18.4%).

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance/actual gross-margin pairs were provided beyond a qualitative/target statement (“mid-20% gross margins for any program that we launch”) without a corresponding period-specific forward guidance figure paired to a later actual for the same metric and time window. While the target and commentary can be compared directionally, the instruction requires rating credibility only from guidance/actual pairs, and the count is zero here; therefore forecasting credibility cannot be assessed from a Met/Missed framework.

Trends:
  - Automotive gross margin declined materially during 2023 (Q1 peak into 2023 troughs).
  - Energy generation and storage gross margin improved over 2023 and 2024.
  - 2024 automotive gross margin remained depressed vs 2023 and the earlier mid-20% framing.
  - Late-2024 showed mixed sequential movements (improvements in some quarters, declines in others).

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Automotive gross margin volatility from pricing and mix (average selling price and cost per unit).
  - Regulatory credit and FSD/revenue impacts as offsets or contributors to gross margin.
  - Unpredictability from external factors (e.g., tariffs/incentives) and transition-related items (one-time warranty adjustments, deferred revenue).

Key concerns:
  - Management’s “mid-20%” automotive gross margin framing for launched programs did not align with realized automotive gross margin levels in the cited filing periods (e.g., total automotive 21.1% in Q1 2023; 19.4% for full-year 2023; 18.4% for full-year 2024).
  - Gross margin drivers shift across periods (pricing/cost, regulatory credits, FSD revenue, and Cybertruck ramp), making sustained stabilization harder and potentially weakening comparability quarter-to-quarter.

Conclusion:
Gross margin for automotive weakened sharply from 2023 into the lower-teens/high-teens range, despite occasional sequential improvement. In filing-sourced actuals, total automotive fell from 32.9% to 21.1% in the three months ended March 31, 2023, and for the full year it decreased from 28.5% to 19.4%. In 2024, total automotive declined further from 19.4% to 18.4% for the year ended December 31, 2024, while quarterly movement was mixed (e.g., automotive & services increased to 18.7% in Q3 2024, then the full-year level still ended below 2023). Overall, the longitudinal evidence shows a sustained reduction in automotive gross margin versus earlier “mid-20%” commentary, partially mitigated by services/other and by contributions from regulatory credits and FSD revenue in certain quarters.
