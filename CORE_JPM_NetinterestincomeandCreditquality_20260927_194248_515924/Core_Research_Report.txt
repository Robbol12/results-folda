[CORE RESEARCH] JPM: Net interest income

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 77

Across the period, the topic labeled as net interest income (NII) / net interest revenue (NIR) shows volatility largely tied to net investment securities results and deposit margin dynamics rather than a steady directional NII trend. In Q3 2023, management referenced guidance language for net interest income implying a $22.9 billion fourth-quarter exit run rate, but the provided evidence does not include a resolved FY/Vs-exit-run-rate outcome in the transcript or filing excerpts for that specific metric. Q1 2024 commentary indicates deposit margin compression partially offset NII (consistent with pressure on NII), and Q3 2024/ Q4 2024 commentary shows improvement in NIR driven predominantly by lower net investment securities losses. Overall, the observed variation in NIR/NII is repeatedly attributed to investment securities losses/gains and market-related items, with management also acknowledging a shift away from the view of being “materially overearning on net interest income.”

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance/actual pairs for net interest income were found for this topic that can be used to assess a Met/Missed forecasting pattern. While Q3 2023 includes a guidance statement implying a $22.9 billion fourth-quarter exit run rate for net interest income, the provided evidence does not include a later disclosed actual (from transcript or filing excerpt) resolving that specific exit run-rate figure for comparison.

Trends:
  - Net interest income/revenue appears pressured by deposit margin compression (Q1 2024 transcript).
  - Net interest revenue improved as net investment securities losses declined (Q3 2024 and Q4 2024 transcripts).
  - Management’s framing shifted away from being materially overearning on net interest income (Q4 2024 transcript).

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Deposit margin compression reducing NII (explicitly referenced in Q1 2024).
  - Net investment securities losses/gains heavily influence NIR (explicitly stated multiple quarters).
  - Interest rate environment drives NIM risk (compressions in low/negative rates; higher rates generally supportive per filings).

Key concerns:
  - Guidance credibility cannot be assessed because no complete guidance-vs-actual pair for net interest income is provided; the only specific figure given ($22.9 billion implied exit run rate in Q3 2023) has no corresponding resolved actual in the provided excerpts.
  - Quarterly net interest revenue outcomes are described as driven by net investment securities losses, implying that NIR is not purely a “rates/NII” run-rate story and may complicate forecasting.

Conclusion:
The evidence provided indicates net interest income/revenue (NII/NIR) moved materially quarter to quarter, with drivers repeatedly attributed to net investment securities losses and deposit margin compression rather than a stable NII trajectory. In Q1 2024, commentary states revenue was partially offset by lower NII due to deposit margin compression, and NIR was a net loss of $188 million (from transcript). In Q3 2024, NIR was a net gain of $155 million, predominantly driven by lower net investment securities losses, and NIR ex-markets rose sharply (from transcript). In Q4 2024, NIR remained a net loss of $30 million (from transcript), again attributed to lower net investment securities losses versus the prior year; management also noted it had backed off the view that it was materially overearning on net interest income (from transcript).

Across 2023, management framed credit performance as influenced by normalization in the Card portfolio, with credit costs and reserve movements fluctuating quarter to quarter. In Q3 2023, management guided the 2023 card net charge-off rate to approximately 2.5% (denominator effects), but the guidance/actual pair is unresolved in the provided evidence, so it cannot inform forecasting credibility. In Q1 2024, management guided the 2024 card net charge-off rate to below 3.5%, and the provided outcome indicates this guidance was met. In Q3 2024, management guided the 2024 card net charge-off rate to approximately 3.4%, but the outcome is marked missed, indicating weaker-than-expected credit performance. By Q4 2024, management reiterated 2025 card net charge-off guidance (approximately 3.6%) without a resolvable outcome in the provided evidence.

Forecasting credibility: Medium
Only two guidance/actual pairs with verdicts are provided: Q1 2024 (below 3.5% for 2024 card net charge-offs) is Met, while Q3 2024 (approximately 3.4% for 2024 card net charge-offs) is Missed. With a 1/2 Met-to-Missed split, management’s ability to forecast the card net charge-off rate shows mixed credibility. Unresolved guidance in Q3 2023 and Q4 2024 cannot be credited or penalized under the evidence rules given.

Trends:
  - Credit costs driven predominantly by Card net charge-offs and shifting between net reserve release vs build
  - Net charge-off pressure in 2023 and 2024 quarters described as part of normalization, then worse-than-expected relative to tighter guidance by Q3 2024
  - Management progressively narrowed/updated card net charge-off expectations for 2024 (below 3.5% to ~3.4%) before missing

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Card normalization effects and exposure to revolving balance dynamics
  - Loss assumptions and reserve release/build sensitivity
  - Sensitivity to macroeconomic conditions affecting borrower/counterparty credit quality

Key concerns:
  - Forecasting uncertainty: guidance evolved from below 3.5% (Q1 2024, met) to approximately 3.4% (Q3 2024, missed), suggesting changing assumptions or underestimation of credit deterioration timing/magnitude.
  - In Q3 2024, transcript-reported credit cost components indicate substantial net reserve build (e.g., net reserve build of $876 million predominantly from higher revolving balances), which can complicate forecasting of eventual net charge-off rates.
  - Several guidance items are marked unresolved in the provided evidence (Q3 2023 card net charge-off rate ~2.5%; Q4 2024 2025 guidance ~3.6%), limiting assessment of full-period forecasting consistency.

Conclusion:
Based on the only resolvable guidance/actual verdicts provided, credit quality forecasting for the Card net charge-off rate is mixed: the Q1 2024 target (2024 card net charge-off rate below 3.5%) was Met, but the Q3 2024 target (approximately 3.4%) was Missed. Quarter-to-quarter credit costs described in the transcripts appear to be driven by net charge-offs alongside materially offsetting reserve release/build activity (e.g., Q1 2024 included a net reserve release; Q3 2024 included a large net reserve build tied to higher revolving balances). This pattern supports that credit quality risk is concentrated in the Card portfolio and is sensitive to balance composition and loss-assumption updates, which can reduce forecasting precision even when management identifies the primary driver (normalization). Overall, credit quality directionality is not clearly worsening across all quarters, but the forecasting record for the key metric (card net charge-off rate) provides only medium credibility.
