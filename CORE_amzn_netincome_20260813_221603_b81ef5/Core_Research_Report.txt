[CORE RESEARCH] AMZN: net income

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 130

Net income showed significant volatility over the periods from Q1 2021 through Q4 2022. The company reported strong net income in 2021, peaking in Q4 2021 with $14.4 billion, largely driven by a pre-tax valuation gain of $11.8 billion from its Rivian Automotive investment. However, in 2022, net income turned negative in the first two quarters due to substantial valuation losses related to the same investment, with a net loss of $3.8 billion in Q1 and $2 billion in Q2. The company returned to positive net income in Q3 2022 with $2.9 billion and reported a modest $278 million in Q4 2022, despite ongoing valuation losses. Operating income generally remained positive and often exceeded guidance, but net income was heavily influenced by non-operating items tied to equity investments.

Forecasting credibility: High
There is one explicit guidance/actual pair for operating income in Q3 2022, where management guided operating income between zero and $3.5 billion and actual operating income was $3.3 billion, exceeding the midpoint and near the top of the range. This indicates credible forecasting for operating income. Although net income was more volatile due to large non-operating valuation gains and losses, the operating income guidance and results demonstrate management's ability to forecast core earnings reliably.

Trends:
  - Strong net income in 2021
  - Large valuation gains from Rivian investment in late 2021
  - Significant net losses in early 2022 due to Rivian valuation losses
  - Recovery to positive net income in late 2022
  - Operating income generally stable and often exceeding guidance

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Impact of equity investment valuation gains/losses on net income
  - Volatility in other income (expense), net
  - Amortization of intangible assets
  - Lease impairments and asset impairments

Key concerns:
  - Net income is heavily influenced by large, non-cash valuation gains and losses related to the Rivian Automotive investment, which introduces significant volatility and complicates assessment of core profitability.
  - Operating cash flow changes are often attributed to net income excluding non-cash expenses and working capital changes, indicating reliance on adjustments to understand cash generation.
  - No inconsistencies or accounting changes were noted across filings, but the large swings in other income (expense), net highlight the risk of relying on net income as a sole performance metric.

Conclusion:
Net income over the period was highly volatile, driven primarily by large non-operating valuation gains and losses from the company's investment in Rivian Automotive. While 2021 showed strong net income boosted by an $11.8 billion valuation gain, 2022 experienced net losses in the first half due to valuation losses exceeding $7 billion in Q1 and nearly $4 billion in Q2. The company returned to positive net income in Q3 and Q4 2022, though at much lower levels than 2021. Operating income, which excludes these non-operating items, was more stable and often exceeded guidance, supporting a credible view of management's forecasting ability for core earnings. Overall, net income is not a consistent indicator of operational performance due to the significant impact of equity investment valuations.
