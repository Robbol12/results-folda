[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 77

Over the course of 2021, gross margin trends showed a clear improvement in the automotive segment, with automotive gross margins rising steadily from 26% in Q1 to 29.3% by year-end. Energy generation and storage margins remained challenging, with significant negative margins early in the year (-20% in Q1) improving somewhat but still negative by year-end (-4.6%). Management consistently expressed optimism about automotive gross margin strength, driven by cost reductions, factory utilization, and product mix improvements, while acknowledging headwinds in the energy segment due to product ramp and manufacturing underutilization. The automotive & services segment also saw margin improvements, reflecting both automotive gains and better services margins. Overall, the company demonstrated progress in gross margin expansion, particularly in automotive, despite ongoing challenges in energy generation and storage.

Forecasting credibility: High
Management's guidance on gross margin strength, especially in the automotive segment, was consistently met or exceeded across the two guidance/actual pairs available. For example, Q2 2021 actual automotive gross margin increased from 25% to 28%, meeting management's optimistic outlook. Similarly, Q4 2021 automotive gross margin excluding credits reached 29.2%, the highest yet, as forecasted. The actual figures from filings align closely with management's forward-looking statements, indicating credible forecasting in this area.

Trends:
  - Automotive gross margin steadily increasing
  - Energy generation and storage gross margin negative but improving
  - Automotive & services segment margin improvement
  - Cost reduction and factory utilization driving margin gains

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Energy segment manufacturing underutilization
  - Product ramp challenges in Solar Roof and Megapack
  - Mix shift towards software potentially improving margins
  - Volatility in energy storage margins

Key concerns:
  - Significant negative gross margins in energy generation and storage persisted throughout 2021 despite some improvement, indicating ongoing operational challenges.
  - The energy segment's margin declines were attributed to temporary manufacturing underutilization and product ramp issues, which could pose risks if not resolved.
  - No accounting or reporting inconsistencies were noted across the filings; actuals from filings were consistent with management commentary.

Conclusion:
Gross margin performance in 2021 was characterized by strong and consistent improvement in the automotive segment, with automotive gross margins rising from 26% in Q1 to 29.3% by year-end, driven by cost efficiencies and increased production. The automotive & services segment also benefited from these improvements. Conversely, the energy generation and storage segment struggled with negative gross margins due to product ramp challenges and manufacturing underutilization, though some improvement was seen by year-end. Management's optimistic guidance on automotive gross margins was validated by actual results, demonstrating credible forecasting. Overall, gross margin trends reflect a company successfully expanding profitability in its core automotive business while working to address persistent challenges in its energy segment.
