[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 97

Over the course of 2024, the company's gross margin trends showed a mixed pattern across segments. The total automotive segment experienced a general decline in gross margin from 21.1% in Q1 2023 to 18.4% in the full year 2024, with some quarterly fluctuations including a slight improvement in Q3 2024. The automotive & services and other segment also saw a decline in gross margin over the year, while the energy generation and storage segment showed a significant increase in gross margin from 11.0% in Q1 2023 to 26.2% in the full year 2024. Management attributed changes primarily to vehicle pricing, production ramp, and cost efficiencies. The company met its guidance for the full year 2024 automotive gross margin, indicating some forecasting accuracy.

Forecasting credibility: Medium
There is only one explicit guidance/actual pair available, which was met for the full year 2024 automotive gross margin (18.4% actual vs. guidance). This supports a medium level of forecasting credibility. However, the absence of multiple guidance/actual pairs limits a stronger rating. The detailed filing data provides reliable actuals, but forward guidance was generally qualitative rather than quantitative, reducing the ability to fully assess forecasting accuracy.

Trends:
  - Declining automotive gross margin over 2024
  - Improving energy generation and storage gross margin
  - Quarterly fluctuations with slight recovery in Q3 automotive margin
  - Overall automotive & services margin decline

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Lower average selling price impacting automotive margin
  - Cybertruck production ramp effects
  - Cost per unit improvements partially offsetting margin pressure
  - Regulatory credits and FSD revenue influencing margins

Key concerns:
  - No multiple explicit guidance/actual pairs to robustly assess forecasting accuracy
  - Qualitative guidance on margins limits precision in forecasting evaluation
  - Consistent decline in automotive gross margin despite some offsetting factors

Conclusion:
The company's gross margin for the total automotive segment declined from 21.1% in Q1 2023 to 18.4% for the full year 2024, driven by lower average selling prices and the Cybertruck ramp, partially offset by cost improvements and regulatory credits. The energy generation and storage segment showed a strong margin improvement, more than doubling from 11.0% to 26.2% over the same period. The automotive & services and other segment also experienced margin declines. Management's guidance for the full year 2024 automotive gross margin was met, lending some credibility to their forecasting, though the limited number of guidance/actual pairs and qualitative nature of guidance temper confidence. Overall, gross margin trends reflect operational challenges in automotive offset by gains in energy storage and regulatory benefits.
