[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 186

Across 2023, Tesla’s gross margin deteriorated sharply from 2022 levels, with filing disclosures attributing the declines primarily to automotive gross margin decreases. In Q1 2023, management referenced “mid-20% gross margins” for newly launched programs, but sequential and ongoing commentary emphasized that near-term automotive margin was pressured by factors such as pricing actions and one-time items (e.g., warranty adjustments and deferred revenue transitions). Through Q2 and Q3 2023, management characterized gross margin variances as relatively minor versus the long-term picture, while filings showed continued automotive margin compression (e.g., total automotive gross margin down to ~19% area by mid-2023). In 2024, automotive gross margin remained below 2023 levels overall but showed episodic sequential improvement in Q3 2024, with filings explicitly citing mix/cost reductions and higher FSD/regulatory credit revenue partially offset by lower selling prices and Cybertruck ramp effects in Q4 2024. Energy generation and storage gross margin, by contrast, improved materially over time, rising from negative in early 2023 to mid/high-teens and beyond in 2024, partially offsetting the automotive weakness when viewed at the consolidated/segment level.

Forecasting credibility: None
No explicit guidance/actual numerical pairs were provided for gross margin that can be used to evaluate forecasting accuracy across quarters. Management did provide qualitative direction (e.g., “mid-20% gross margins” for launched programs and that sequential auto gross margins could improve) but there is no countable guidance-to-actual comparison pair set here. Therefore, forecasting credibility cannot be rated from this evidence set.

Trends:
  - Automotive gross margin fell materially in 2023 versus 2022 and stayed in the high-teens/low-20s range thereafter
  - Sequential automotive gross margin improvement appeared at points (notably Q3 2024), then softened again by year-end 2024
  - Energy generation and storage gross margin improved substantially from negative in early 2023 to strongly positive in 2024

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Automotive gross margin driven by pricing/selling price and unit costs (cost per unit)
  - Regulatory credits and FSD revenue contributing to gross margin variability
  - External factors (e.g., tariffs/local incentives) affecting automotive margin volatility
  - One-time items (warranty adjustments) and deferred revenue transitions impacting period-to-period results

Key concerns:
  - Management’s explicit “mid-20% gross margins” framing in Q1 2023 was for “any program that we launch,” but the filings show consolidated/segment automotive gross margin remained far below that level during 2023–2024, creating potential mismatch between program-level aspiration and reported automotive segment outcomes.
  - Automotive gross margin outcomes increasingly relied on offsetting revenue components (e.g., regulatory credits, FSD) and mix/cost factors, which may make margin sustainability dependent on these items rather than core vehicle pricing.

Conclusion:
Gross margin performance was dominated by automotive weakness through 2023 and into 2024, as reflected in filings showing total automotive gross margin decreasing from 32.9% to 21.1% in Q1 2023 and continuing to decline through the year (e.g., 28.5% to 19.4% for 2023 full-year). In 2024, automotive gross margin improved at times but generally remained below prior-year levels, with Q3 2024 showing a sequential increase to 18.7% (from 17.4% in Q3 2023) attributed to lower cost per unit and higher FSD and regulatory credits revenue, partially offset by lower average selling price. By Q4 2024, filings again showed deterioration for the year ended December 31, 2024 versus 2023 (total automotive gross margin 19.4% to 18.4%), driven by lower average selling price and Cybertruck ramp effects, partially offset by lower cost per unit and higher regulatory credit and FSD revenue. Meanwhile, energy generation and storage gross margin improved strongly (e.g., -11.7% to 11.0% in Q1 2023 and 18.9% to 26.2% for full-year 2024), but the automotive decline remained the main determinant of Tesla’s overall gross margin level across the period.
