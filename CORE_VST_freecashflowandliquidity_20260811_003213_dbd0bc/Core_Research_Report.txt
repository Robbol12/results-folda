[CORE RESEARCH] VST: free cash flow

Quarters searched: 2
Filing sections searched: 44

Throughout 2021, the company consistently discussed free cash flow conversion as a key metric, targeting a 60% to 70% conversion of adjusted EBITDA to free cash flow before growth. Early in the year, actual operating cash flows were negative, with cash used in operating activities and investing activities, indicating cash outflows. Management reaffirmed guidance mid-year, projecting adjusted EBITDA around $3 billion with strong free cash flow conversion. By Q3, guidance for 2022 free cash flow before growth was set between $2.07 billion and $2.57 billion, including securitization proceeds. However, actual cash flows for the year showed a significant decline, with consolidated cash flows used in operations of $206 million, a sharp contrast to the prior year's positive $3.337 billion, indicating a miss relative to optimistic guidance.

Forecasting credibility: Low
The single guidance/actual pair available shows a significant miss, with management projecting strong free cash flow conversion and positive cash flows, but the actual results revealed negative operating cash flows and overall cash used in operations for the year. This discrepancy undermines the credibility of management's forecasting on free cash flow. The lack of multiple guidance/actual pairs further limits confidence in forecasting reliability.

Trends:
  - Negative operating cash flows early in 2021
  - Consistent guidance on 60-70% free cash flow conversion
  - Reaffirmed and narrowed guidance mid-year
  - Inclusion of securitization proceeds in free cash flow guidance
  - Actual cash flows deteriorated significantly by year-end

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Volatility in operating cash flows
  - Impact of securitization proceeds on free cash flow
  - Capital expenditures remaining substantial
  - Uncertainty from retail bill credits and weather events

Key concerns:
  - Significant discrepancy between optimistic free cash flow guidance and actual negative cash flows raises concerns about forecasting accuracy.
  - Depreciation and amortization reconciling adjustments vary notably quarter to quarter, which may affect cash flow interpretations.
  - The inclusion of securitization proceeds in free cash flow guidance complicates comparability and may mask underlying operational cash flow weakness.

Conclusion:
The company's free cash flow performance in 2021 was weaker than management's guidance suggested, with actual operating cash flows turning negative and consolidated cash flows used in operations by year-end. Despite management's repeated emphasis on a 60% to 70% conversion of adjusted EBITDA to free cash flow before growth, the realized cash flows did not meet these expectations. The reliance on securitization proceeds to bolster free cash flow guidance further complicates the assessment of core operational cash generation. Overall, the evidence indicates that free cash flow generation was challenged in 2021, and management's forecasting on this metric demonstrated low credibility based on the available guidance and actual results.

Over the reported periods in 2021, the company's available liquidity fluctuated but remained in the range of approximately $2.0 billion to $2.8 billion, composed of cash on hand and available capacity under revolving credit facilities. The company consistently stated confidence in having sufficient liquidity to fund anticipated cash requirements for at least the next 12 months, supported by capital raises such as senior unsecured notes and preferred stock issuances. Liquidity changes were driven by operational cash flows, capital expenditures, share repurchases, dividends, and letters of credit. The company also noted the ongoing uncertainty related to COVID-19 as a potential risk to liquidity. No forward guidance on liquidity figures was provided, only retrospective reporting and qualitative statements about sufficiency.

Forecasting credibility: None
There are zero guidance versus actual pairs for liquidity metrics, as management does not provide explicit forward guidance on liquidity figures. All commentary is retrospective or qualitative regarding sufficiency of liquidity. Therefore, no credibility rating can be assigned based on guidance accuracy.

Trends:
  - available liquidity fluctuating around $2 billion to $2.8 billion
  - consistent internal forecasts of sufficient liquidity for 12 months
  - capital raises supporting liquidity
  - cash used in operations and capital expenditures reducing liquidity
  - COVID-19 as a liquidity risk factor

Recurring themes/risks:
  - capital expenditures impacting liquidity
  - share repurchases and dividends reducing cash
  - letters of credit increasing liquidity usage
  - COVID-19 economic uncertainty

Key concerns:
  - No forward-looking quantitative guidance on liquidity is provided, limiting ability to assess forecasting accuracy.
  - Liquidity figures are subject to fluctuations from operational and financing activities, requiring close monitoring.
  - COVID-19 remains a noted risk that could materially impact liquidity beyond current forecasts.

Conclusion:
The company maintains liquidity in the low billions, supported by cash, revolving credit facilities, and capital market issuances. Management consistently expresses confidence in having sufficient liquidity to meet cash needs for at least the next 12 months, though this is based on internal forecasts rather than explicit forward guidance. Liquidity is impacted by significant capital expenditures, dividends, and share repurchases, as well as letters of credit. The ongoing COVID-19 pandemic is acknowledged as a risk that could affect liquidity. Overall, the company appears to have adequate liquidity currently, but the absence of forward guidance and the noted risks warrant continued monitoring.
