[CORE RESEARCH] NVDA: Data Center revenue

Quarters searched: 7
Filing sections searched: 173

Across fiscal 2023, management framed Data Center results around hyperscale demand strength while highlighting geopolitical and regional headwinds (notably Russia absence affecting guidance for a subsequent quarter, and softness in China). By Q4 2023, the SEC-reported outcome showed Data Center revenue ended fiscal 2023 at $15.01 billion (+41% y/y), supported by hyperscale and CSP-related multi-year cloud agreements. In fiscal 2024, reported Data Center revenue accelerated sharply—$4.28 billion (+14% y/y) in Q1, $10.32 billion (+171% y/y) in Q2, and $14.51 billion (+279% y/y) in Q3—while management emphasized generative AI/HGX ramp and continued manageability of China/license-constrained volumes. In fiscal 2024 full-year and into fiscal 2025, the filings show further expansion to $47.5 billion (+217% y/y) in FY2024 and continued strong growth in Q1–Q3 2025, indicating that the earlier headwind framing did not derail the long-duration upswing. Over the period, the most concrete filing-sourced “actuals” consistently corroborate a sustained uptrend in Data Center revenue, with qualitative guidance largely focused on mix and timing rather than precise numerical quarter targets.

Forecasting credibility: None
Only one guidance/actual pair with a clear Met/Exceeded/Missed verdict was identified across the full period: the claim about launching the first Data Center CPU (Grace) in the first half of 2023 was met. Other guidance items for Data Center (e.g., growth rates or sequential dynamics) were marked unresolved, and the Russia impact guidance lacked a resolved “actual” in the provided evidence. Because the required guidance/actual pair count with a verdict is effectively 1, but the dataset labels most as unresolved, a reliable forecasting-credibility rating cannot be established from a pattern (and the provided instruction requires a null rating when the pattern count is not sufficient/does not meet the stated condition).

Trends:
  - Shift from headwind-focused narrative (Russia/China) toward sustained AI-driven growth
  - Large step-up in quarterly Data Center revenue from FY2024 Q2 onward
  - CSP/hyperscaler strength increasingly highlighted as a core driver
  - Continued full-year surge into FY2025

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Geopolitical/regulatory exposure (Russia absence; China destination licensing requirements)
  - Supply/demand balance and inventory effects (inventory charges/reserves referenced in FY2023 filings and later inventory release benefits)
  - Product/platform ramp dependency (HGX/Hopper networking and GPU platform transitions)
  - Concentration and channel/distributor dynamics (noted distributor customer revenue contribution in Q2 FY2024)

Key concerns:
  - Several Data Center growth guidance statements (e.g., low- to mid-single-digit sequential growth or ~70% y/y growth) were left unresolved in the provided evidence, limiting the ability to judge timing/forecast accuracy versus actuals.
  - FY2023 filings highlight substantial inventory charges ($2.17B) tied to excess Ampere architecture supply versus demand expectations for China, implying forecast/model risk around regional demand assumptions.
  - Early-cycle guidance included estimates of impacts (e.g., $100M Russia absence impact on a subsequent quarter), but the provided excerpt does not include the corresponding filed actual for that specific datapoint.

Conclusion:
Data Center revenue evolved from a strong-but-headwind-aware setup in fiscal 2023 to a sustained, AI-led acceleration across fiscal 2024 and into fiscal 2025. Filing-sourced results show Data Center revenue of $15.01 billion (+41% y/y) for FY2023, rising to $47.5 billion (+217% y/y) for FY2024. Quarterly filings further confirm the step-up: $4.28B (+14% y/y) in Q1 FY2024, $10.32B (+171% y/y) in Q2 FY2024, and $14.51B (+279% y/y) in Q3 FY2024. In fiscal 2025, the filings report continued momentum with Data Center revenue of $22.6B in Q1 (+427% y/y), $26.3B in Q2 (+154% y/y), and rising further into Q3 FY2025 (+112% y/y).

Across FY2023, management’s gross margin trajectory was heavily influenced by large inventory and warranty reserve movements: GAAP gross margin fell to 43.5% in Q2 2023 versus a guided 62.4% (±50 bps) and then improved materially in later quarters (e.g., GAAP 53.6% in Q3 2023 and 63.3% in Q4 2023). By FY2024, gross margin rebounded and stabilized at high levels, with Q3 2024 GAAP gross margin reported at 74% (versus guided 74.5%) and FY2024 ending at 72.7% GAAP after Data Center growth and lower inventory provisions. In FY2025, gross margin remained strong in the mid-to-high 70% range overall (e.g., 78.4% GAAP in Q1 2025), though management disclosed continuing risk from inventory provisions tied to Blackwell low-yielding materials. Overall, the period shows gross margin recovering as inventory reserve overhangs eased and mix shifted toward higher-margin Data Center, while ongoing inventory provisioning risk remained a key swing factor.

Conclusion:
Gross margin performance evolved from a reserve-driven trough in FY2023 to a recovered, more stable level in FY2024 and elevated levels in FY2025. The filing excerpts attribute FY2023 weakness to substantial inventory charges ($2.17B) related to excess supply versus demand expectations (including China), and FY2024 improvement to Data Center revenue growth plus lower net inventory provisions as a percentage of revenue, resulting in FY2024 GAAP gross margin of 72.7% versus 56.9% in FY2023. In FY2025, gross margin remained high (e.g., GAAP 78.4% in Q1 2025 and 75.1% in Q2 2025), but management disclosed continued downside risk from inventory provisions for low-yielding Blackwell material.
