[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 186

Across 2023, management framed gross margin as variable and tied to moving parts (pricing actions, incentives/tariffs, and one-time items), and the filing results showed a pronounced deterioration in automotive gross margin versus the prior-year periods (e.g., Q1 2023: 32.9% to 21.1%; Q2 2023: 27.9% to 19.2%). Through 2023, gross margin remained depressed overall, though management suggested sequential improvement in Q4 2023 while also emphasizing the difficulty of forecasting auto gross margins. In 2024, the year began with further weakness versus 2023 (Q1 2024: 21.1% to 18.5% for total automotive), but results later showed pockets of recovery in 3Q 2024 and then renewed softness in 4Q/year-end automotive gross margin. By full-year 2024, automotive gross margin was lower than 2023 (total automotive 19.4% to 18.4%), while energy generation and storage margins improved materially.

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance/actual pairs for gross margin can be counted as a forecast-versus-outcome comparison because the evidence provided contains no explicit forward guidance for a subsequent period that can be matched to a later reported figure. While management did provide directional qualitative commentary (e.g., “mid-20% gross margins” for launched programs and “extremely challenging” predictability), the instructions require rating credibility only from guidance/actual pair counts, which are zero here. Therefore, forecasting credibility is not assessed.

Trends:
  - Automotive gross margin sharply down in 2023 versus prior-year comparisons
  - Automotive gross margin largely remains in a depressed range through 1H 2024
  - 3Q 2024 shows partial improvement with cited drivers (cost per unit, FSD and regulatory credits)
  - Full-year 2024 automotive gross margin declines modestly versus 2023
  - Energy generation and storage gross margin improves strongly across 2023-2024

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Pricing/incentive actions affecting vehicle gross margin
  - One-time items (e.g., warranty adjustments, deferred revenue impacts tied to Autopilot transition)
  - Externally driven cost and revenue variability (e.g., tariffs and local incentives)
  - Regulatory credits and FSD revenue contributing to gross margin changes
  - Vehicle mix and ramp effects (e.g., Cybertruck ramp) influencing results

Key concerns:
  - Qualitative guidance did not translate into stable predictability: the automotive gross margin target/reference (“mid-20%” for launched programs) was not reflected in reported automotive gross margin through multiple 2023 quarters and full-year 2023.
  - Gross margin is heavily impacted by items beyond controllable operational levers (tariffs/incentives and accounting treatment of transition-related items), which can weaken straightforward forecasting.

Conclusion:
Gross margin evolution is dominated by a major automotive drawdown in 2023 followed by partial, non-linear recovery in 2024. Automotive gross margin fell from 32.9% to 21.1% in Q1 2023 (and similarly from 30.5% to 19.9% for automotive & services/other), with continued weakness in Q2 and Q3 2023 (e.g., total automotive 27.9% to 19.2% in Q2 2023). In 2024, total automotive decreased again in Q1 (21.1% to 18.5%) but improved in 3Q 2024 (total automotive & services/other up to 18.7% from 17.4%, with management citing lower cost per unit and higher FSD/regulatory credits, partially offset by ASP). By full-year 2024, automotive gross margin declined modestly versus 2023 (total automotive 19.4% to 18.4%), indicating that any improvements were not sustained at the year level, even as energy generation and storage margins rose sharply (18.9% to 26.2% from 2023 to 2024).
