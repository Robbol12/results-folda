[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: cost of goods sold

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 174

Over the periods from Q1 2021 through Q4 2022, cost of goods sold (COGS) was discussed primarily in the context of automotive leasing revenue components, product launches, and cost reductions per vehicle. Management highlighted a reduction in COGS per vehicle by approximately $5,000 year over year by Q2 2021 and noted a 50% reduction in COGS transitioning from earlier vehicle platforms to newer ones by Q3 2022. The filings consistently referenced a $5.0 billion obligation related to combined capital, operational expenses, COGS, and other costs tied to Gigafactory New York through 2029. Inflationary pressures, commodity costs, and logistics were noted as ongoing cost challenges, but no direct forward guidance on COGS was provided. The disclosures also clarified that COGS includes depreciation and warranty expenses related to leased vehicles.

Forecasting credibility: None
There were zero guidance versus actual pairs found for cost of goods sold, indicating management does not provide explicit forward guidance on this line item. Therefore, it is not possible to assess forecasting credibility for COGS based on the available data. Any commentary on cost trends or reductions is retrospective or qualitative rather than forecasted with subsequent verification.

Trends:
  - Declining cost of goods sold per vehicle
  - Significant cost reductions from platform transitions
  - Inflation and commodity cost pressures increasing
  - Consistent $5 billion spending obligation including COGS
  - COGS components include depreciation and warranty expenses

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Raw material and commodity price exposure
  - Impact of product launches on cost recognition
  - Obligations tied to Gigafactory New York spending
  - Inflation and logistics cost pressures

Key concerns:
  - No explicit forward guidance on cost of goods sold limits ability to evaluate management's forecasting accuracy for this metric.
  - The $5 billion combined spending obligation includes COGS but is aggregated with other costs, making it difficult to isolate COGS trends from filings alone.
  - Consistent references to depreciation and warranty expenses within COGS suggest some variability in cost recognition timing depending on product launch phases.

Conclusion:
Cost of goods sold has shown a downward trend per vehicle over the periods reviewed, with management citing a $5,000 year-over-year reduction by mid-2021 and a 50% reduction when moving from earlier to newer vehicle platforms by late 2022. Despite inflationary and commodity cost pressures, the company has managed to contain cost increases modestly. The filings reveal that COGS includes depreciation and warranty expenses related to leased vehicles, and that COGS is part of a broader $5 billion spending commitment in New York through 2029. However, the absence of explicit forward guidance and paired actuals for COGS means forecasting credibility cannot be assessed. Overall, COGS appears to be managed carefully with ongoing cost reduction efforts, but transparency on future expectations is limited.
