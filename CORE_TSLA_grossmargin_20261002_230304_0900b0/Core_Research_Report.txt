[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 186

Across 2023, management highlighted mid-20% gross margins as the target for new automotive programs, but reported automotive gross margin deteriorated substantially during 2023. Q1–Q3 2023 showed sharp declines in automotive gross margin, with total automotive gross margin falling from 32.9% (Q1 2022) to 21.1% (Q1 2023), and further weakness through the year (e.g., 19.4% in Q4 2023 vs. 28.5% in Q4 2022 on a year basis). The pattern was accompanied by offsetting improvement in energy generation and storage gross margin (e.g., from -11.7% to 11.0% in Q1 2023; to 18.9% for full-year 2023). In 2024, automotive gross margin remained volatile but showed partial stabilization/improvement in parts of the year (e.g., total automotive & services gross margin increased to 18.7% in Q3 2024), before ending 2024 lower overall on a full-year basis (18.2% in 2023 to 16.9% in 2024).

Forecasting credibility: None
No explicit gross-margin forward guidance/target for subsequent periods was provided in a way that can be paired to later actual outcomes (i.e., there are zero guidance/actual pairs for gross margin). As a result, forecasting credibility cannot be assessed from a guidance-vs-actual hit-rate pattern. The only related forward-looking statement is a general “mid-20% gross margins” comment for launching programs, which does not constitute a period-specific forecast that can be validated against later reported quarters/years.

Trends:
  - Automotive gross margin declined sharply in 2023 vs. 2022 comparatives (e.g., 32.9% to 21.1% in Q1; 28.5% to 19.4% on a full-year basis).
  - Energy generation and storage gross margin improved materially and persistently across 2023 into 2024.
  - Automotive gross margin showed intermittent sequential improvement in 2024 (notably Q3), but ended 2024 lower on a full-year basis for automotive & services.
  - Services/other gross margin sometimes partially offset weaker automotive gross margin within combined reporting.

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Difficulty predicting auto gross margins due to external variables (e.g., tariffs, local incentives).
  - Automotive gross margin pressure tied to vehicle pricing/actions to improve affordability and one-time items (e.g., warranty adjustments, deferred revenue transition).
  - Offsets from regulatory credit and FSD-related revenue (influencing automotive & services gross margin components).

Key concerns:
  - The “mid-20% gross margins” reference in Q1 2023 for launched programs was not realized in the reported automotive gross margin figures during 2023 (e.g., total automotive gross margin of 21.1% in Q1 2023 and 19.4% for the full year 2023).
  - Volatility in automotive gross margin through 2024 (improving in Q3 2024 to 18.7% for automotive & services, then declining again by year-end) suggests that realized margins are highly sensitive to mix/pricing and revenue drivers.
  - Across periods, “total automotive” and “total automotive & services and other segment” are both tracked; conclusions about gross margin can differ depending on which line is used (e.g., combined vs. automotive-only).

Conclusion:
Gross margin for automotive deteriorated materially during 2023 and remained below the earlier “mid-20%” framework for launched programs. Reported total automotive gross margin fell from 32.9% (Q1 2022) to 21.1% (Q1 2023), and on a full-year basis declined from 28.5% (2022) to 19.4% (2023). Energy generation and storage gross margin, by contrast, improved from negative levels to positive and rose to 18.9% for full-year 2023, indicating broad segment divergence. In 2024, automotive & services gross margin improved at points (up to 18.7% in Q3 2024) but ended the year lower on a full-year basis (from 18.2% in 2023 to 16.9% in 2024), reflecting continued pressure from pricing and ramp effects even as credits/FSD and cost reductions provided partial offsets.
