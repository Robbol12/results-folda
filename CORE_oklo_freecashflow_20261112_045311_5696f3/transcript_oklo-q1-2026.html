Jed Dorsheimer

Jake, can you just talk about some of the challenges and maybe the timing of going from uranium to plutonium in your Pluto reactor? And then also, the advantages that that may provide.

Jacob Dewitte

Sure. With a fast reactor system like ours, you can use plutonium as a fuel source. The way that works is you take the plutonium that exists — this is surplus plutonium that the government produced largely as part of weapons programs in the past. It's been deemed surplus and was slated for disposition; the best way to dispose of it is to put it in reactors and fission it, which is what we're intending to do. Plutonium is a concentrated fissile material and can be made available through government programs. That material can be used as a fuel form for reactors like ours. How that works is you mix the plutonium with uranium and zirconium to cast a metallic fuel form — a ternary alloy fuel. There is a long history of research and development in fast reactor programs in the United States and abroad supporting that approach. Long story short, we can use that plutonium as a fuel source instead of HALEU, which is particularly useful because that material exists and is more readily fabricatable than standing up and spinning up larger-scale HALEU supply chains in the near term. Over time, HALEU production and recycling will play larger roles, but plutonium is a powerful bridge. It's a finite resource, so we'll build reactors that start on it and gradually transition to refueling with produced HALEU or fuel from recycling. The government's first tranche of about 20 tons of plutonium is equivalent to roughly 160 to 200 tons of HALEU, which is a substantial amount of fuel to get reactors started and accelerate initial deployment. There are some challenges, but they're manageable. Fast reactor experience with this fuel form exists and we know how to manage its usage. Handling and processing are different in some respects, but there is a deep supporting qualification base and expertise in the U.S. and internationally.
