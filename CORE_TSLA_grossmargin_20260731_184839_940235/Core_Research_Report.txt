[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 186

Over the observed periods from Q1 2023 through Q4 2024, the company's automotive gross margin experienced a general decline from the low 30% range in early 2022 to the high teens by 2024. Management consistently highlighted challenges such as price cuts to improve vehicle affordability, warranty adjustments, deferred revenue changes, and external factors like tariffs and incentives impacting margins. Despite these headwinds, there were occasional sequential improvements, notably in Q4 2023 and Q3 2024, attributed to operational efficiencies and feature releases. Energy generation and storage gross margins showed a contrasting positive trend, improving significantly over the same timeframe. The company’s guidance suggested a mid-20% gross margin target for new programs, but actual results frequently fell below this level.

Forecasting credibility: Low
The two guidance/actual pairs available indicate that management's forecasts for automotive gross margin were not met, with actual margins consistently below the mid-20% guidance or the 20% floor mentioned. The actual reported gross margins from filings show a clear pattern of misses relative to the guidance, undermining the credibility of management's forecasting on this metric. This pattern suggests that management's projections were overly optimistic or failed to fully anticipate the impact of pricing actions and other cost factors.

Trends:
  - Declining automotive gross margin over time
  - Sequential margin improvements in select quarters
  - Energy generation and storage margin improvement
  - Impact of price cuts and warranty adjustments
  - Increasing deferred revenue effects

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Price cuts to drive volume growth
  - Warranty adjustments on older vehicles
  - Deferred revenue changes for Autopilot features
  - External factors like tariffs and local incentives
  - Challenges in predicting automotive gross margin

Key concerns:
  - Consistent decline in automotive gross margin despite guidance for mid-20% margins raises concerns about the accuracy of management's forecasting assumptions.
  - One-time items such as warranty adjustments and deferred revenue recognition changes complicate margin comparability across periods.
  - The company notes the difficulty in predicting automotive gross margins due to many moving parts, some outside management's control, which may contribute to forecasting misses.

Conclusion:
The gross margin for the automotive segment has trended downward from above 30% in early 2022 to the high teens by 2024, reflecting significant pressure from pricing strategies and cost factors. Management's guidance aiming for mid-20% margins was not met in the two documented guidance/actual pairs, indicating a pattern of overestimation. While there were some sequential improvements in certain quarters, these were insufficient to reverse the overall declining trend. The energy generation and storage segment showed margin improvement, but this did not offset the automotive margin pressures. Overall, the evidence supports a conclusion that automotive gross margins have been challenged and management's forecasting on this metric has lacked reliability.
