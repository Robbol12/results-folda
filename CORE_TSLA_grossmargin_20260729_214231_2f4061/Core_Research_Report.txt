[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 77

Over the course of 2021, gross margin trends showed a clear improvement in the automotive segment, with automotive gross margins rising steadily from around 26% in Q1 to over 29% by Q4. Management consistently expressed optimism about gross margin strength, particularly in automotive, driven by cost reductions, factory utilization, and product mix improvements. However, the energy generation and storage segment experienced significant margin pressure, with gross margins declining sharply into negative territory due to product ramp challenges and manufacturing underutilization. The company highlighted the impact of Solar Roof and Megapack transitions on energy margins. Overall, automotive gross margins improved substantially year-over-year, while energy margins remained a drag.

Forecasting credibility: High
There is one explicit guidance/actual pair with a verdict in Q4 2021 where management's forecast of automotive gross margin reaching a high of 29.2% excluding credits was met exactly. This precise match between guidance and actual performance supports a high rating for forecasting credibility. Throughout the year, management's optimistic commentary on automotive gross margin strength was borne out by consistent improvements in reported figures, reinforcing their reliability in forecasting this metric.

Trends:
  - Automotive gross margin steadily increasing
  - Energy generation and storage gross margin declining
  - Improvement driven by cost reduction and factory utilization
  - Negative impact from Solar Roof and Megapack ramp

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Manufacturing underutilization in energy segment
  - Product ramp challenges affecting energy margins
  - Shift in product mix impacting overall gross margin
  - Dependence on Shanghai factory exports for automotive margin gains

Key concerns:
  - Significant negative gross margins in energy generation and storage segment due to product ramp and manufacturing inefficiencies
  - Potential volatility in gross margin from mix of hardware and software business components
  - No direct guidance/actual pairs for energy segment margins, limiting assessment of forecasting credibility there

Conclusion:
Gross margin performance in 2021 was characterized by strong and consistent improvement in the automotive segment, with reported automotive gross margins rising from approximately 26% in Q1 to 29.3% for the full year, driven by cost efficiencies and increased production capacity. Conversely, the energy generation and storage segment faced substantial margin headwinds, with gross margins falling from positive single digits to negative percentages, primarily due to Solar Roof manufacturing underutilization and the transition from Powerpack to Megapack products. Management's guidance on automotive gross margin was credible and met in Q4, indicating reliable forecasting in this area. However, the energy segment's margin challenges and lack of explicit guidance/actual pairs suggest less visibility and forecasting reliability there. Overall, automotive gross margin strength was a key positive driver of the company's profitability in 2021.
