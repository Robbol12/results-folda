[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: margin

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 189

Over the observed periods from Q1 2024 through Q4 2025, automotive gross margins generally trended downward, declining from around 21.1% in early 2024 to approximately 17.8% by the end of 2025. There were some quarter-over-quarter improvements, such as in Q3 2024 and Q4 2025, but these were often offset by declines in subsequent periods. Energy generation and storage margins showed a strong upward trend, increasing significantly from 11.0% in Q1 2024 to nearly 30% by the end of 2025. Management frequently cited factors such as lower average selling prices, regulatory credit fluctuations, and cost pressures as drivers of margin changes. Guidance often acknowledged margin challenges ahead, particularly due to economic conditions, competition, and tariff costs.

Forecasting credibility: Medium
There is only one explicit guidance/actual pair with a verdict, which was met, indicating some degree of forecasting accuracy. However, the guidance was generally qualitative and cautious, often highlighting margin pressures without precise numeric targets. The actual results consistently showed margin declines or modest improvements as anticipated, supporting a moderate level of credibility. The lack of multiple explicit guidance/actual pairs limits a higher confidence rating.

Trends:
  - Declining automotive gross margins over time
  - Improving energy generation and storage margins
  - Quarterly margin volatility with occasional sequential improvements
  - Increasing cost pressures and regulatory credit variability

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Lower average selling prices impacting automotive margins
  - Regulatory credit revenue fluctuations
  - Cost absorption challenges due to factory changes and delivery volumes
  - Competitive and tariff-related margin compression risks

Key concerns:
  - Automotive gross margin consistently declined year-over-year despite some quarter-over-quarter improvements, raising concerns about sustainable profitability in this segment.
  - Regulatory credit revenue changes significantly impact reported margins, complicating the assessment of underlying operational performance.
  - Management's guidance is often qualitative and lacks precise numeric targets, limiting the ability to rigorously assess forecasting accuracy.

Conclusion:
Automotive margins have shown a clear downward trajectory from early 2024 through 2025, primarily driven by lower average selling prices, reduced regulatory credits, and cost pressures related to production and delivery. While management has acknowledged these challenges and provided cautious guidance, the actual results have generally aligned with expectations, albeit with some volatility. Energy generation and storage margins have improved markedly, partially offsetting automotive margin declines at the consolidated level. Overall, the margin outlook remains pressured in automotive, with some stabilization signs late in 2025, but ongoing risks from competition and economic factors persist.

    - In addition, certain government and economic incentives which provide benefits to manufacturers who assemble domestically or have local suppliers, may provide a greater benefit to our competitors, which could negatively impact our profitability.
    - Any reduced availability of these materials may impact our access to cells and our growth, and any increases in their prices may reduce our profitability if we cannot recoup such costs through increased prices.
    - If we are not successful in achieving these goals, we could face delays in establishing and/or sustaining our product ramps or be unable to meet our related cost and profitability targets.
    - Our plan to grow the volume and profitability of our vehicles and energy storage products depends on significant lithium-ion battery cell production.
    - We have made in the past and may make in the future certain adjustments to our prices from time to time in the ordinary course of business, which may impact the residual values of our vehicles and reduce the profitability of our vehicle leasing program.
