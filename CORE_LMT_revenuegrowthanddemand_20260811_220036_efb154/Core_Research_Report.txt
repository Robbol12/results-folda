The filings focused more on program risks and cost management rather than projecting revenue figures or growth rates.

Conclusion:
The company did not provide explicit forward guidance on revenue growth during 2023, focusing instead on program performance risks and cost management. While Q4 commentary highlighted programs entering growth stages and potential growth drivers, these were not quantified or linked to revenue forecasts. Consequently, there is insufficient evidence to evaluate revenue growth trends or management's forecasting accuracy. The emphasis on monitoring and risk suggests cautious management of growth expectations rather than clear revenue acceleration guidance.

[CORE RESEARCH] LMT: demand

Quarters searched: 2
Filing sections searched: 63

Throughout 2023, management consistently reported strong and strengthening demand for key products, particularly the F-35 aircraft and missile defense systems, with notable international customer commitments such as Canada and Singapore. Demand was also influenced by geopolitical factors, including U.S. and allied support for Ukraine, which created additional government demand to replenish stockpiles. Despite these positive demand signals, management did not provide explicit forward guidance with quantifiable targets for demand, instead offering qualitative commentary and expectations of continued strength. Backlog levels increased to record highs by Q4 2023, reflecting robust order intake across multiple business segments.

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance-actual pairs were provided for demand, preventing any assessment of forecasting accuracy or credibility. Management's commentary remains qualitative and forward-looking without specific numeric guidance or subsequent verification. Therefore, forecasting credibility for demand cannot be rated based on the available evidence.

Trends:
  - Strong international demand for F-35
  - Increasing government demand due to geopolitical events
  - Rising backlog levels
  - Capacity evaluations to meet anticipated demand
  - Broad portfolio demand growth

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Uncertainty from COVID-19 impacts on government budgets
  - Supply chain and capacity constraints
  - Long-cycle nature of defense contracts
  - Geopolitical influences on demand

Key concerns:
  - Absence of quantitative forward guidance on demand limits ability to assess forecasting reliability
  - Potential risks from unpredictable government budget priorities noted but not quantified
  - No accounting or reporting inconsistencies detected related to demand disclosures

Conclusion:
Demand for the company's products, especially the F-35 and missile defense systems, remained strong and even strengthened over 2023, supported by international customer commitments and geopolitical factors such as support for Ukraine. The record backlog of $161 billion by year-end 2023 underscores robust order intake and a positive demand outlook. However, the lack of explicit numeric guidance on demand and absence of guidance-actual comparisons mean that while demand appears strong, management's ability to forecast demand precisely cannot be evaluated. Overall, evidence supports a conclusion of sustained strong demand but with inherent uncertainties typical of defense contracting.
