[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 186

Across 2023, management emphasized that it typically targets mid-20% gross margins for newly launched programs, but reported automotive gross margins deteriorated sharply in the wake of pricing, one-time warranty adjustments, and Autopilot-related deferred revenue dynamics. The filings show automotive gross margin falling from 30.5% (Q1 2022) to 19.9% (Q1 2023) on an automotive & services basis, with similar declines in subsequent quarters (e.g., 19.0% in Q2 2023; 18.5% in Q3 2023). Through 2024, automotive gross margin remained under the 2023 peaks and then showed some quarter-to-quarter stabilization/improvement before easing again by year-end (e.g., automotive & services and other segment up to 18.7% in Q3 2024, then down to 16.9% in full-year 2024). Energy generation and storage gross margin, by contrast, improved materially over 2023–2024, which partially offsets the weakness in automotive on an aggregate basis.

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance/actual pairs for gross margin were provided in the evidence as “paired” items that can be directly evaluated for forecast accuracy. As a result, credibility cannot be assessed on a Met/Exceeded vs. Missed pattern. Management did provide directional commentary about levels (e.g., mid-20% for launched programs and the difficulty of forecasting auto gross margins), but without an explicit guidance figure paired to a later reported gross margin, a forecasting credibility rating is not supportable.

Trends:
  - Automotive gross margin dropped substantially in 2023 and stayed below prior-year levels
  - Sequential improvement was cited at least once in Q4 2023, but full-year automotive gross margin still declined
  - 2024 showed intermittent automotive gross margin improvements (notably in Q3) followed by softness into year-end
  - Energy generation and storage gross margin improved strongly across 2023–2024

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Vehicle pricing actions and affordability measures affecting gross margin
  - One-time items (e.g., warranty adjustments) and revenue timing effects (Autopilot deferred revenue) impacting reported results
  - Regulatory credits, FSD revenue, tariffs, and incentives driving variability
  - Difficulty forecasting auto gross margins due to multiple controllable and uncontrollable inputs

Key concerns:
  - Potential reliance on one-time items and timing (warranty adjustments; increased deferred revenue for certain Autopilot features) suggests reported gross margin may be less representative of underlying run-rate.
  - Management’s stated framework (mid-20% gross margins for launched programs) does not appear to map to a sustained realized automotive margin level in the filings, with automotive gross margin materially lower through 2023–2024.

Conclusion:
Gross margin performance was dominated by a pronounced automotive decline in 2023, with automotive gross margin reported at 21.1% (Q1 2023) vs. 32.9% (Q1 2022) and 19.9% to 30.5% on an automotive & services and other segment basis for the same quarter. Through the year, automotive gross margin remained depressed, falling from 19.4% (Q4 2022) baseline to 19.4% for 2023 on the “total automotive” metric in the Q4 2023 filing excerpt, and 18.2% on the automotive & services and other segment metric for the year ended Dec. 31, 2023. In 2024, automotive gross margin exhibited limited recovery and volatility—rising to 18.7% for the automotive & services and other segment in Q3 2024 but declining to 16.9% for full-year 2024—while energy generation and storage gross margin increased to 26.2% for full-year 2024, providing the primary offset to automotive weakness. Overall, the evidence supports that gross margin was structurally pressured in automotive throughout the period, with meaningful benefits from the energy segment improving the consolidated picture.
