Over the course of 2024, gross margin trends showed mixed performance across segments. The total automotive gross margin generally declined year-over-year in most quarters, with Q1 and Q2 showing decreases compared to the prior year, a slight improvement in Q3, and a decline again in the full year. The automotive & services segment followed a similar pattern with mostly decreases except for a Q3 increase. Conversely, the energy generation and storage segment demonstrated significant margin improvement throughout the year, with gross margins rising substantially in both quarterly and annual comparisons. These changes were attributed to factors such as cost reductions, regulatory credits, and shifts in product mix.

Forecasting credibility: None
No forward guidance paired with actual results was provided for gross margin in any period, making it impossible to assess management's forecasting credibility on this topic. Without guidance-actual pairs, no pattern of met, exceeded, or missed forecasts can be established. Therefore, the credibility rating must remain null.

Trends:
  - Declining automotive gross margin year-over-year
  - Energy generation and storage gross margin improving significantly
  - Automotive & services segment margins mostly decreasing with a Q3 uptick
  - Cost reductions and regulatory credits impacting margins
  - Lower average selling prices pressuring automotive margins

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Pressure from lower vehicle selling prices
  - Impact of Cybertruck ramp on automotive margins
  - Benefits from regulatory credits and FSD revenue
  - Cost reduction efforts in energy storage products
  - Product mix shifts within segments

Key concerns:
  - No forward-looking guidance on gross margin limits ability to evaluate management's forecasting accuracy.
  - Some inconsistency in margin trends within the automotive & services segment, with quarterly improvements not sustained in year-to-date figures.
  - Potential risk from reliance on regulatory credits and FSD revenue to offset margin pressures.

Conclusion:
Gross margin for the total automotive segment generally declined in 2024 compared to 2023, driven by lower average selling prices and the Cybertruck ramp, partially offset by cost reductions and increased regulatory credit and FSD revenue. The automotive & services segment mirrored this trend with mostly declining margins except for a Q3 increase. In contrast, the energy generation and storage segment showed strong margin improvement due to cost reductions, IRA manufacturing credits, and a higher proportion of higher-margin storage business. Overall, while automotive margins faced headwinds, energy storage margins improved significantly, reflecting effective cost management and favorable product mix changes. However, the absence of forward guidance on gross margin prevents assessment of management's forecasting reliability on this key metric.
