[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: profit

Quarters searched: 8

Across 2022–2023, management consistently emphasized that Tesla’s profit was supported by mix (e.g., shift toward more profitable vehicles such as Model Y), strong energy gross profits (Megapack/Powerwall), and automotive margin strength, with additional contributions from services, used vehicles, and transactions (e.g., parts, paid supercharging). Reported results in the period included explicit profitability metrics such as Q1 2022 record profit and an operating margin of 19%, and Q3 2022 GAAP automotive operating margin of 17.2% with automotive gross margin of 27.9%. In 2022, management also highlighted that profit performance occurred alongside stock-based compensation costs (e.g., SBC expense attributable to a specific CEO award of $48M in Q1). In 2023, profitability commentary continued to focus on energy margin improvements and other business profit drivers (part sales, used vehicle sales, merchandise, and pay-per-use supercharging), while filings included disclosures related to profit-sharing arrangements and adoption of a method (HLBV) for noncontrolling interest measurement that may affect presentation of “profit” for certain structures.

Forecasting credibility: None
No guidance/actual pairs for profit were provided in the evidence set, so forecasting credibility cannot be assessed on a Met/Exceeded vs Missed pattern. The only “outlook” shown for Q1 2022 is a general capacity growth statement and does not constitute profit-specific quantitative guidance that can be matched to subsequent reported profit figures.

Trends:
  - Profit remained a recurring theme: record profitability in Q1 2022 and continued strength noted throughout 2022
  - Energy business became an increasingly prominent driver of gross profit (strongest gross profit yet in Q3 2022; margin improvements in 2023)
  - Other/services profit sources emphasized over time (used vehicles, part sales, supercharging, merchandise)
  - Automotive margins stayed relatively high with explicit operating/gross margin metrics reported in late 2022

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Margin drivers tied to mix shifts and pricing/ASP
  - Energy volume and deal economics as key profit levers (Megapack/Powerwall; cost reductions)
  - Profit influenced by items outside core product margins (e.g., FX, commodities/logistics, fixed costs, SBC volatility, impairment charges)
  - SBC recognized with specific awards can affect comparability of profit across periods

Key concerns:
  - The evidence set lacks profit-specific forward guidance, preventing any guidance-vs-actual credibility assessment for profit.
  - SEC filing excerpts indicate determinations about contractual profit-sharing arrangements and use of the HLBV method for noncontrolling interests; this suggests potential changes in how certain profit-related measures are presented/allocated versus prior assumptions.
  - Some profit discussion references non-core drivers (used cars/transactions, parts/service activities), which may make “profit” less directly tied to core automotive/energy unit economics in any single period.

Conclusion:
Profit performance in 2022–2023 appears supported by multiple recurring engines: automotive mix/pricing and high margins, energy gross profit strength (explicitly described as record/strongest gross profit yet and later margin improvements), and other business contributions (used vehicles, part sales, merchandise, and pay-per-use supercharging). The provided reported evidence includes specific profitability metrics, including Q1 2022 record quarter profit with an operating margin of 19%, and Q3 2022 GAAP automotive operating margin of 17.2% with automotive gross margin of 27.9%. The evidence also shows that profit levels were achieved alongside notable SBC expense in Q1 2022 (SBC attributable to the 2018 CEO award of $48M). Because no profit-specific guidance/actual pairs were provided, the dataset supports describing realized profit drivers, but it does not allow a quantified judgment of management’s forecasting accuracy for profit.
