[CORE RESEARCH] TSLA: gross margin

Quarters searched: 8
Filing sections searched: 186

Across 2023, the company’s gross margin deteriorated materially versus the prior year: total automotive gross margin fell from 32.9% (Q1 2022) to 21.1% (Q1 2023), and this contraction persisted through each 2023 quarter and the full year (e.g., total automotive to 19.4% for 2023). Energy generation and storage gross margin moved in the opposite direction, improving from negative/low levels (e.g., -11.7% in Q1 2023 vs Q1 2022) to strongly positive margins (e.g., 18.9% for full-year 2023). In early 2024, automotive gross margin continued to run below 2023 levels (e.g., total automotive 18.5% in Q1 2024), while energy margins continued to improve (to 24.6% in Q1 2024). By late 2024, automotive gross margin showed some quarter-to-quarter stabilization (e.g., total automotive & services and other increased to 18.7% in Q3 2024), but finished the year lower overall (total automotive 18.4% for full-year 2024 vs 19.4% in 2023), largely influenced by pricing and Cybertruck ramp dynamics. Overall, the period reflects an automotive margin pressure backdrop with partial offsets from mix (services, regulatory credits, FSD-related revenue) and a clear energy margin recovery.

Trends:
  - Automotive gross margin declined sharply in 2023 and remained subdued through 2024 (with some quarter-to-quarter rebounds).
  - Total automotive gross margin decreased from 32.9% (Q1 2022 baseline) to 21.1% (Q1 2023) and ended 2024 at 18.4%.
  - Energy generation and storage gross margin improved consistently from 2023 into 2024, reaching 26.2% for full-year 2024.

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Automotive gross margin affected by vehicle pricing/affordability actions and one-time items (warranty adjustments; deferred revenue for Autopilot features).
  - Difficulty predicting auto gross margins due to external factors (e.g., tariffs/incentives).
  - Mix and non-vehicle revenue offsets (services margin improvement, regulatory credit revenue, FSD/Supervised revenue) partially offset pricing/cost pressures.
  - Product ramp impacts (Cybertruck ramp) contributing to automotive margin volatility.

Key concerns:
  - Automotive gross margin fell substantially versus prior-year baselines throughout 2023 (e.g., 32.9% to 21.1% in Q1; 28.5% to 19.4% full-year), indicating that any prior expectations (e.g., mid-20% framing for launched programs) were not realized in the reported outcomes.
  - Management emphasized that predicting auto gross margins is extremely challenging due to multiple moving parts out of its control, increasing the risk of forecast dispersion.
  - Margin drivers rely on items that can move non-linearly (regulatory credits, FSD revenue, and product ramp effects), making year-over-year comparisons potentially sensitive.

Conclusion:
Gross margin was dominated by sharp automotive weakness during 2023 and sustained softness into 2024, with offsetting improvements in the energy segment. For automotive, total automotive gross margin declined to 21.1% in Q1 2023 from 32.9% a year earlier, and ended 2023 at 19.4% versus 28.5% in 2022. In 2024, automotive remained below prior-year levels overall, with total automotive at 18.4% for full-year 2024 versus 19.4% for 2023, despite some intra-year improvement (e.g., increases in Q3 2024 total automotive & services and other to 18.7%). Meanwhile, energy generation and storage gross margin improved materially, rising to 18.9% in 2023 (from 7.4% in 2022) and further to 26.2% in 2024 (from 18.9% in 2023), providing the primary counterweight to automotive margin pressure.
