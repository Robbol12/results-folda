[CORE RESEARCH] LMT: production

Quarters searched: 4
Filing sections searched: 65

Throughout 2024, production guidance for the F-35 program consistently targeted a production rate of approximately 156 aircraft per year, with delivery expectations ranging between 75 and 110 aircraft for 2024 and an increase to 170 to 190 aircraft deliveries projected for 2025. Despite no customer deliveries in the first half of 2024, management maintained confidence in meeting delivery targets in the second half, supported by backlog stability and ongoing contract negotiations. Production ramp-ups were also noted in related programs such as the F-16 and classified programs, contributing to overall net sales growth. The company emphasized the complexity of integrating new technologies like TR-3 and the importance of government contracts in sustaining production momentum. Negotiations on Lots 18-19 production contracts and the timing of invoicing and funding were recurring themes impacting production visibility.

Forecasting credibility: Medium
There are two guidance/actual pairs with verdicts: the Q1 2024 guidance of 75 to 110 aircraft deliveries was not met in the first half of the year as no deliveries occurred, but deliveries began in July 2024, aligning with the second half guidance. The Q4 2024 guidance of 170 to 190 deliveries for 2025 remains unresolved but is consistent with the production rate and backlog. The pattern shows some initial misses but eventual alignment with guidance, indicating moderate forecasting credibility. The reliance on filing-sourced actuals and ongoing contract negotiations introduces some uncertainty but management's adjustments and clarifications support a reasonable level of trust in their forecasts.

Trends:
  - Stable production rate around 156 aircraft per year
  - No deliveries in first half 2024, deliveries starting in second half
  - Increasing delivery expectations for 2025
  - Production ramp-up in F-16 and classified programs
  - Ongoing contract negotiations impacting production visibility

Recurring themes/risks:
  - Dependence on timely hardware receipt from suppliers
  - Complexity of integrating new TR-3 technology
  - Contract negotiations with U.S. Government (Lots 18-19)
  - Backlog stability and its impact on production planning
  - Sales impact from classified program charges

Key concerns:
  - No customer deliveries in the first half of 2024 despite guidance for 75 to 110 deliveries for the full year raises concerns about delivery timing and supply chain constraints.
  - Ongoing negotiations on Lots 18-19 production contracts create uncertainty around invoicing and revenue recognition.
  - The impact of classified program charges and contract mix adjustments on operating profit and sales introduces variability in financial results.

Conclusion:
Production for the F-35 program in 2024 was maintained at a steady rate of approximately 156 aircraft per year, with no deliveries in the first half but deliveries commencing in the second half, aligning with management's guidance. The backlog of 373 aircraft as of mid-2024 supports continued production momentum. While initial delivery targets were missed in early 2024, management's updated guidance and actual deliveries in the latter half demonstrate a recovery in production execution. The ramp-up in related programs like the F-16 and classified programs further supports overall production growth. However, ongoing contract negotiations and supply chain complexities remain risks to production stability. Overall, production is on track to meet or exceed guidance in the near term, reflecting moderate forecasting reliability and operational resilience.
